## 3. Lead SE reading of the inputs

The owner is asked to correct any line below that misreads the inputs.

| # | Topic | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Surface temperature | Concur. Every external surface a hand can reach stays at or below 48 C (REQ-SYS-113 scope), with no 60 C class. This constrains option C: any heatsink must be guarded or placed so its reachable surface stays at or below 48 C. |
| 2 | RF-exposure legend | Concur. The legend is part of the surface, not a label or film. Engraving is preferred: on a metal part, PCBWay engraves it; on a printed case, it is printed into the surface (embossed or debossed). It passes the strengthened durability test. |
| 3 | Jack markings | Concur (the owner's opening "I agree"). Same rule as the legend. |
| 4 | Flame rating | Declined. No flame-retardant filament is required, and option C prints in PETG. The CR drops the proposed UL 94 V-1 requirement. The loss of a fire-containing case for HZ-002 and HZ-007 (cell venting) is re-assessed in the PDR hazard analysis, and any rise in residual risk goes to the owner as SMA TA for acceptance. The PDR thermal analysis also checks PETG softening near the PA and heatsink (CR-003 review F9). |
| 5 | Build sequence | One circuit board is built first, and one enclosure option at a time. The cheapest, option C (printed PETG with a conductive coating and a heatsink), goes first. If it meets its acceptance criteria (thermal, shielding, hand safety, legend), it is the delivered enclosure. If it does not, the CNC metal enclosure is ordered from PCBWay and evaluated. Option B is no longer prototyped in parallel; it stays in the PDR trade only as an alternative metal fallback route. The number of radios built changes from the three of ADR-025 and SI-035 to one board first; that is a change to baselined items and is raised as its own CR (CR-006) for disposition. |
| 6 | PCB plate edges | Moot while option B is not prototyped. The rule (edges covered or broken, else CNC plates) stays a trade criterion. |
| 7 | Coating | No product named. The PDR enclosure trade researches spray-on conductive coatings for printed parts that have a published surface resistivity, and recommends one. |
| 8 | EMI measurement | The owner verified shielding before with a spectrum analyzer and a near-field probe (professional equipment).
