## 1.0 Purpose and Scope

**Purpose.** This SEMP is the agreement between the owner and the executing technical team (Claude and its independent agents) on how the technical work of cwht is planned, executed, controlled and assessed (NPR 7123.1D §6.1.1.5). It defines how the 17 common technical processes of NPR 7123.1D Chapter 3 are applied, tailored and customized on cwht (SE-58), names the artifact each process produces, and establishes the Measures of Performance and Technical Performance Measures (SE-60) and their reporting (SE-61).

**Scope.** The plan covers the full life cycle of cwht revision A: from stakeholder expectations through design, procurement of the fabricated and surface-mount-assembled hardware, the owner's hand assembly of through-hole and exposed-pad parts (SI-031), firmware implementation, verification, validation, acceptance and hand-over to the owner and friends, plus the on-air operations phase and the formulation of revision B. It covers hardware (RF, power, control, mechanical), firmware (Rust on the owner's `rustos`), the human elements (operator, maintainer; the vendor assembles surface-mount parts and the owner assembles the rest) and the enabling products (simulation decks, host test harness, optional emulator, test fixtures and procedures, tooling).

**Content.** Sections follow SE HB App. J: technical summary (3), technical effort integration (4), the 17 processes (5), technology insertion (6), additional SE functions including system safety, methods and tools, HSI, TPMs and heritage (7), integration with the project plan (8), compliance matrices (9), appendices.

## 3.0 Technical Summary

**Problem.** Build a pocket-sized handheld true-CW (A1A) transceiver for the full US 2 m band, 144.000 to 148.000 MHz (SI-024), 5 W output (SI-003), with a built-in electronic keyer that accepts both a straight key and iambic paddles (SI-018, core requirement), operating semi break-in with an adjustable hang time and no full QSK (SI-036), that works at first power-on because every risk has been closed by analysis, host tests and pre-build inspection before the vendor builds it (SI-010 as refined by SI-026). Stakeholder inputs SI-001 to SI-036 are logged in `docs/requirements/l0-stakeholder/stakeholder-inputs.md`; the log is append-only and later entries are cited by id.

**Influencing factors.** Kit assembly model (SI-031, superseding the no-hand-soldering intent of SI-009): PCBWay fabricates the boards, places every surface-mount part from parts stocked at DigiKey, Mouser or its own distributors (SI-028) and machines the aluminum enclosure (SI-008); the owner hand-solders through-hole parts and modules with exposed pads (Pico 2 castellations, 18650 holders, jacks, encoder, an RF module if one is chosen); no hidden-pad package is left to the owner; the product baseline states which parts are in each category. Owner does no coding or layout (SI-011). Bench gear (SI-013, SI-034, charter §9): NanoVNA, tinySA Ultra spectrum analyzer (purchase committed by the owner, SI-034 and ADR-021; its receipt is recorded by a receipt-inspection report `docs/vv/reports/receipt-inspection-<n>.md` and a tool validation record `docs/cm/tool-validation/TV-NNN-<tool>.md` before TRR, closing OQ-VV-001; charter §9) with a calibrated 30 to 40 dB 10 W attenuator, 50 Ω BNC dummy load, adjustable bench supply, multimeter, Pico-based logic capture; no oscilloscope. Controller is a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 with firmware on `rustos` (SI-007); new peripheral drivers are developed upstream in `rustos`, not in a cwht-local crate (SI-033). Battery is two user-replaceable 18650 cells in series (SI-023); the module's micro-USB is the single connector for firmware load and charging (SI-022). Regulation is 47 CFR Part 97 under the owner's General-class license (SI-014); every operator holds at least a Technician license, so operators are evaluated at the occupational/controlled RF exposure limits and bystanders at the general population limits (SI-030). The project is open source under the MIT license (SI-025). Process rigor is NASA Class A / Criticality 1 by owner election (SI-015, charter §1).

**Critical questions.** (1) Can a 5 W, 146 MHz PA and its harmonic filter meet 47 CFR 97.307(e) with margin, proven by simulation, by NanoVNA filter measurement and by tinySA Ultra spectrum measurement on every unit (TPM-007, MOE-006)? (2) Can the receiver reach -140 dBm MDS in a 500 Hz passband in a pocket form factor with the chosen architecture (TPM-005, MOE-010)? (3) Can `rustos` be extended upstream with the peripheral drivers the radio needs (timer and alarms, GPIO edge interrupts, PWM, ADC, I2C or SPI, watchdog, flash store; `07-software-engineering-plan.md` §19) at Class A rigor within the project? (4) Can the keyer meet the element-timing and latency targets of NGO-013 for straight key and paddles in every mode, with semi break-in feel the owner accepts (TPM-013, MOE-005)? (5) Can battery life (8 h at 1:9, SI-034), mass, envelope and thermal behavior all fit the handheld envelope (TPM-001, TPM-002, TPM-008, TPM-016)?

**Constraints.** Cost: labor-free cost model `docs/plan/cost-estimate.md` (BOM, fabrication, assembly, enclosure, shipping; SRR envelope USD 972 to 1836 for three radios including the USD 120 to 160 tinySA Ultra line and its 20 % contingency) tracked as TPM-014 on a unit basis that excludes the instrument (§8.0); build quantity five bare boards, three assembled by default, at most five complete units (SI-035, ADR-025). Schedule: gates are event-based (charter §3, §12); the owner has approved an expedited target schedule (SI-020: SRR Saturday morning, PDR Saturday evening, CDR Sunday evening with procurement release Sunday night, three owner review windows) that sets when each gate is attempted, not when it passes; a gate whose entrance criteria are not met at its target time is held until they are. Technical: MOPs and TPMs in §7.4.

**Adequate definition of the problem.** The problem is adequately defined when the owner rules each of the twelve SRR success criteria of `01-lifecycle-and-reviews.md` §4.4 Met or Met with lien in the SRR decision memo, and every L1 requirement in `docs/requirements/sys/requirements.json` has passed validation steps V1 to V6 (the six steps of SE HB §4.2.1.2.4, `02-requirements-and-traceability.md` §5) in the independent record `docs/reviews/SRR/checklists/requirements-sys.md` with zero open Major RIDs (02 §12 SRR row).

**Customers and users.** The owner is customer, end user, licensee and Decision Authority; friends holding at least a Technician license are secondary users who receive finished radios (SI-019, SI-030). Priorities, in order: regulatory compliance and safety; works at first power-on; dual-key CW operation; battery life and size; cost.

**Relationship to other projects.** `rustos` (owner's Rust bare-metal OS for RP2350) is the firmware foundation; cwht consumes it as a path dependency and delivers its driver work packages upstream (SI-033; `07-software-engineering-plan.md` §19). cwht revision B (70 cm, SI-002) is a follow-on formulation; revision A keeps the band-dependent functions separable (NGO-030).

### 3.1 System Description

The end product is one handheld radio unit consisting of: a 4-layer PCB (receiver, transmitter and PA, power and charging, controller, audio and key interface) with surface-mount parts placed by PCBWay and through-hole and exposed-pad parts soldered by the owner; a CNC-machined aluminum enclosure with 3D-printed facade and knobs (SI-012); and firmware. Operator interfaces: volume knob, tuning knob (rotary encoder), small LCD, headphone jack, key/paddle jack (3.5 mm TRS: tip = dit or straight key, ring = dah, sleeve = common; SI-034), antenna connector on one end, the Pico 2 module's micro-USB connector for both charging and firmware load (SI-022). Band control is reserved for revision B without occupying a control position. Enabling products: LTspice simulation decks and checkers, the host test harness (`cwht-hal-mock`), the optional emulation scenarios under `firmware/emu/` (harness, vendoring form and scenario format fixed by the PDR emulator ADR; `07-software-engineering-plan.md` §1.2, §9.4), Pico-based test fixtures, test procedures, tooling scripts, review packages.

Product layers and the subsystem modules of the L2 specifications (charter §5, §6; `02-requirements-and-traceability.md` §2.2):

| Layer | Elements | Specification |
|---|---|---|
| L1 system | The radio unit (hardware + firmware + operator interfaces) | `docs/requirements/sys/requirements.json` (REQ-SYS-NNN) |
| L2 subsystems | RX receiver; TX transmitter and PA; PWR battery, charging, regulation; CTL controller, display, encoders, key interface, USB; ME enclosure, thermal, connectors; SW firmware common | `docs/requirements/{rx,tx,pwr,ctl,me,sw}/requirements.json` |
| L2 software module files and design elements | Rust modules (`SW-KEYER` from SRR; further `<SUB>` names created by the firmware architecture ADR at PDR), laid out as `docs/requirements/sw/sw-<sub>/requirements.json` (REQ-SW-<SUB>-NNN; charter §5 example `sw-keyer`); schematic sheets, PCB regions and CAD parts as design elements | `docs/requirements/sw/sw-<sub>/requirements.json`, `docs/design/architecture.md`, `docs/design/software-design.md`, `docs/design/allocation.json` |

Interfacing systems: headphones or earbuds, straight key or iambic paddles, antenna and the RF environment, USB host for charging and firmware load, other amateur stations on air, the human operator.

### 3.2 System Structure

Specification tree: L0 `expectations.json` (NGO-NNN, MOE-NNN, CON-NNN) → L1 SYS → L2 RX/TX/PWR/CTL/ME/SW → L2 software module files `sw/sw-<sub>/`. Measures: `docs/plan/tpm.json` (`mops[]` MOP-NNN selected from the MOEs, `tpms[]` TPM-NNN). Drawing tree: `hardware/kicad/` (schematic sheets named per L2 module, one PCB), `hardware/enclosure/` (OpenSCAD sources, STEP export through headless FreeCAD, SI-032), `hardware/bom/` (design BOM with the surface-mount versus hand-solder category of every line), `hardware/sim/` (LTspice decks and checkers), `hardware/releases/` (fabrication, assembly and CNC packages with order confirmations; the product-baseline split of surface-mount versus hand-solder parts is `hardware/releases/HW-MB-rev<X>-<n>/cwht-MB-rev<X>-hand-assembly.csv`, charter §5, SI-031, 05 §8.2). Interface tree: `docs/icd/ICD-<A>-<B>.md` for every L2 pair that exchanges signal, power, data or mechanical fit, plus `ICD-CTL-SW.md` and `ICD-SW-HOST.md` (charter §5). Firmware tree: `firmware/` workspace (crates `cwht-app`, `cwht-core`, `cwht-hal-mock`, plus the `firmware/emu/` placeholder for emulation scenarios; `07-software-engineering-plan.md` §1.2) depending on `../rustos`. Concept: `docs/design/concept.md` (technical view, SE-36) with `docs/conops/conops.md` (operational view). Technical cost: `docs/plan/cost-estimate.md`; schedule and milestones: `docs/plan/schedule.md`. `docs/design/allocation.json` records which L2 elements satisfy each L1 requirement and which design elements satisfy each L2 requirement; `tools/traceability.py` rule T-18 (`SYS_UNALLOCATED`, implemented: Warning at SRR, Error from PDR under `--gate`; known-answer test `tools/tests/test_traceability_srr_rules.py`) reports every Draft or Active SYS requirement that names no receiving L2 module through `child_ids`, a child's `parent_id` or `allocation.json`.

**Product breakdown and work breakdown (SE HB App. J §3.2).** The product breakdown structure (PBS) is the layer table of §3.1: the radio unit (SYS) decomposes into the six L2 elements RX, TX, PWR, CTL, ME and SW, and SW into its software module files `SW-<SUB>`; the enabling products of §3.1 form a second PBS branch. PBS elements are named by the requirement module tokens of charter §6, so no separate PBS numbering exists. There is no separate WBS document and no project WBS to integrate into (no NPR 7120.5 project plan): a work package is one PBS element times one gate product of §3.4 (for example "TX L2 specification for PDR", "CTL schematic sheet for CDR"), performed through the §5 processes, scheduled against the gates and vendor lead times of `docs/plan/schedule.md`, and costed only where it buys material (`docs/plan/cost-estimate.md`). `docs/plan/technology-assessment.md` assesses maturity against the same PBS elements (SE HB App. G), and `docs/design/allocation.json` maps requirements to them.

### 3.5 Boundary of Technical Effort

Inside the boundary (controlled by the technical team): all hardware and firmware design, the enclosure, the design BOM and the hand-assembly list, simulation and host-test evidence, test fixtures and procedures, the fabrication, assembly and CNC data sent to vendors, documentation, and the `rustos` driver work packages cwht needs (developed upstream, SI-033). Outside the boundary (influences the effort, not controlled): vendor process capability and lead times, part availability, `rustos` core architecture decisions (coordinated with the owner as `rustos` maintainer), FCC rules, the RF environment, the owner's bench inventory and soldering, headphone and key hardware supplied by users. Interfaces across the boundary are physical (antenna connector, key jack, headphone jack, the Pico 2 micro-USB connector per SI-022, 18650 cells per SI-023, enclosure envelope), electronic (USB firmware load protocol, RP2350 bootrom), cognitive (LCD content, knob behavior, sidetone), and functional (on-air CW protocol with other stations).

### 5.1 Stakeholder Expectations Definition (SE-07; SE HB §4.1)

The ConOps `docs/conops/conops.md` (SE HB App. S) defines nominal and off-nominal scenarios `OPS-NNN`, including straight-key operation, paddle operation with the internal keyer, semi break-in behavior, charging, firmware update, low-battery and over-temperature behavior, fault scenarios and hand-over to a friend; `docs/design/concept.md` is the technical view of the concept (SE-36). ### 5.2 Technical Requirements Definition (SE-08; SE HB §4.2, App. C)

L1 requirements are written into `docs/requirements/sys/requirements.json` under `docs/requirements/schema.json`: one shall per statement, quantified with units and tolerances, rationale mandatory, verification method chosen at definition time, `source_ids` back to SI/NGO/CON/MOE/OPS or a regulation clause, `mop_ids` to `mops[]` of `docs/plan/tpm.json` (charter §7). Requirements that control a hazard carry `hazard_ids`, the hazard lists them in each control's `control_req_ids`, and the hazard's `requirement_ids` equals the union of its controls' `control_req_ids` (charter §7; 02 rule T-08). Regulatory requirements (47 CFR 97.305, 97.307, 97.313, RF exposure) carry the `regulatory` tag, cite the corpus file, and are never Goal priority. The dual-key input SI-018 (straight key and iambic paddles) is a core input: it is covered by one L1 requirement per key type, each with children in CTL and SW-KEYER; `priority: KDR` is reserved for cost or schedule drivers with a `KDR:` rationale item (02 §6.1, §6.4) and is not implied by core-input status. The single-valued `priority` field (`KDR`, `Baseline`, `Goal`) is a customization of SE HB §4.2.1.2.3, listed in §9.0 row 10: a KDR cannot also be a `Goal`, and criticality stays independent of the field (tag `safety` and `hazard_ids`). Validation before SRR follows the six steps of SE HB §4.2.1.2.4, each L1 requirement checked by an independent reviewer agent. L2 requirements follow at PDR by the same rules. Measures: TBR count (all L1 TBRs closed by PDR, L2 by CDR), volatility (TPM-012). Detailed in `02-requirements-and-traceability.md`.

## 6.0 Technology Insertion

Technology maturity is assessed with the TRL scale of NPR 7123.1D App. E and the Technology Maturity Assessment and AD2 method of SE HB App. G (NPR 7123.1D §5.1.6). The assessment is `docs/plan/technology-assessment.md` (charter §5), initial at SRR and updated at every review; research findings are cited from `docs/research/` with their confidence tags.

**Two scales.** The App. E TRL measures the maturity of a technology or catalog part in the world at large. cwht's own integration of a mature part starts at the concept level, so a second, project-defined scale, the design maturity level (DML), measures the cwht design of each PBS element (§3.2). NPR 7123.1D §5.1.6 allows other maturity measures when they are mapped back to TRLs. The mapping below does that without inflation: App. E TRL 4 requires a breadboard built and operated in a laboratory and TRL 5 a brassboard validated in a relevant environment, so a hardware design that exists only in simulation is at most TRL 3 (analytical proof of concept), whatever the simulation's fidelity.

| DML | Hardware definition | Firmware definition | App. E TRL of the cwht design |
|---|---|---|---|
| DML-2 | Concept formulated: block named, part family and interfaces identified (`docs/design/concept.md`) | Function and semantics defined, reference behavior chosen | TRL 2 |
| DML-3 | Analytical proof of the critical function: budget or hand analysis against the requirement | Reference model or golden vectors exist; datasheet ICD extracted for the driver | TRL 3 |
| DML-4 | Simulation-verified: LTspice or S-parameter simulation of the block with pass/fail checkers against the budget, catalog parts and vendor models | Logic in `cwht-core` passing HostUnit tests against `cwht-hal-mock` | Hardware TRL 3 (no hardware built); firmware TRL 4 (functionality-critical components integrated and validated in a laboratory environment) |
| DML-5 | Simulated in context: the full block with layout parasitics and component tolerances estimated; or the function measured on a vendor evaluation board on the owner's bench | Firmware image running on a bare Pico 2 development board (Bench, `credit: false`) | Hardware TRL 3 when simulated only, TRL 4 when measured on an evaluation board; firmware TRL 5 when the end-to-end image runs on the dev board, otherwise TRL 4 |
| DML-6 | Assembled cwht board meets its requirements on the bench after TRR | Firmware on the assembled board through the first-power-on procedure | TRL 6 |
| DML-7 | Unit in its enclosure operated on air after the on-air delta TRR | Same | TRL 7 |
| DML-8 | SAR complete: acceptance recorded, V&V report closed | Same | TRL 8 |

**Gate rule for RF hardware.** Every RF block reaches DML-4 by PDR and DML-5 by CDR; an RF block below its gate DML opens a risk and a fallback alternative from its trade study. Both levels are hardware TRL 3: no RF breadboard is built before the CDR procurement release, because the first hardware build follows CDR (charter §3; SI-010 as refined by SI-026). That absence is carried by RSK-008, whose condition states that the hardware is verified before build only by analysis, host tests and optional emulation with no prototype stage. The owner decides at SRR whether to accept hardware TRL 3 at procurement release as a recorded residual of that condition (OQ-SE-006; acceptance recorded per 06 §8).

### 7.1 System Safety

Hazard analysis `docs/safety/hazard-analysis.md` with hazards `HZ-NNN` in `docs/safety/hazards.json` (schema `docs/safety/schema.json`) starts before SRR (preliminary) and is updated at every review. Method, severity and likelihood scales, risk matrix and required response per level, initial versus residual risk and the handling of owner decisions and TBRs are those of `docs/safety/hazard-analysis.md` §3 (§3.1 identification, §3.2 severity, §3.3 likelihood, §3.4 matrix and required response, §3.5 initial versus residual, §3.6 owner decisions), which this section adopts. Hazard closure (every control verified and the residual level accepted) is approved by the owner as SMA TA in the decision memo of the review at which the closure is presented, and at the latest in the SAR decision memo. Hazard scope: RF exposure to the operator and bystanders at up to 5 W with the antenna near the head; RF burn from the antenna connector; PA and enclosure surface temperature; lithium battery charging, over-discharge, short circuit and thermal runaway; hearing damage from headphone level and sidetone; unintended transmission (keyer, sequencer or PA-enable fault, stuck key or wrong plug) including out-of-band or over-power emission; sharp enclosure edges; USB firmware load or reset that leaves the unit in an unsafe state; the owner's hand soldering (burns, fumes, damage to assembled parts). Each hazard lists its controlling requirements in each control's `control_req_ids`, the hazard's `requirement_ids` equals the union of its controls' `control_req_ids` (the field `tools/traceability.py` reads), and each control requirement carries `hazard_ids` (charter §7; 02 rule T-08); controls trace to verification (NPR 7150.2D §3.12.1 Table 1; SWE-192 for software controls tested). Safety-critical software determination (SWE-205; `03-software-classification-and-rmm.md` §4) covers keying, PA enable, charging supervision, thermal protection, audio output limiting and the shared safe-state manager, and the mission-critical frequency control and configuration load (charter §10; components named in 07 §14.1). Those components implement SWE-134 a to l (known safe state at start and restart, safe transitions and termination, two independent actions for operator overrides, out-of-sequence command rejection, memory-corruption detection and recovery, input and output integrity checks, prerequisite checks, no single event initiating a hazard, timely off-nominal response, error handling, ability to reach safe state), with the MC/DC target of 100 % (SWE-219) shown by independently reviewed decision tables and independence-pair tests plus tool-measured region coverage (MSR-13, credit-bearing), plus MSR-14 nightly branch and condition coverage as a required non-credit measure (charter §10; CS-03 date pin), because rustc has no MC/DC instrumentation (tailoring recorded in the RMM; TPM-017), and cyclomatic complexity ≤ 15 (SWE-220). Hardware interlocks are preferred over software for PA enable, transmit time-out and charge cut-off so that no single software event can initiate a hazard. Risk to the public: emission compliance with 47 CFR 97.307(e) (corpus: `47cfr-97.307.md`) is measured on every unit and recorded in `docs/vv/reports/part97-compliance.md` before the on-air delta TRR authorizes the first on-air series. This section governs the hazard analysis; every hazard whose controls are not yet verified also appears in the risk register under the hazard link rule of `06-risk-and-decision-analysis.md` §1; software safety provisions are detailed in `03-software-classification-and-rmm.md` §5 and `07-software-engineering-plan.md` §14.

#### 7.3.1 Human Systems Integration approach (SE-65; baselined at SRR per SE-66)

NPR 7123.1D §5.2.1.3 strongly recommends that Category 1 and Class A programs and projects and piloted aeronautics projects develop a stand-alone HSI Plan, names a section in the SEMP as an option for other projects such as Class C and D science, research and technology development projects, and makes the location of HSI documentation a decision of the program or project manager after negotiation with the technical team. cwht's Class A is the NPR 7150.2D software class elected by the owner, not an NPR 7120.5 category or payload class (01 §4.3 row 9), but because the owner also elected Criticality-1 rigor (charter §1) this SEMP treats the recommendation as applicable. The owner, as project manager, decides against a stand-alone HSI Plan and keeps HSI as this section (charter §12, customization). Rationale: one operator population (licensed amateurs, owner and friends; SI-030) with one set of tasks; a small hand-held product with no crew, habitat, manpower or personnel-selection domains; and HSI content that fits one SEMP section organized on the SE HB App. R outline (relevance, domains and roles, requirements, methods and products by phase, entry and exit criteria). The decision is logged in the SRR decision memo (OQ-SE-001), and Appendix F item F-08 asks for the same rationale in the charter §12 HSI row.

**Relevance.** The radio is operated by hand, by ear and by eye in the field. Human performance drives mission success directly: the operator sends CW with a key or paddles, reads a small display, tunes by knob and listens on headphones. Poor keyer feel, an illegible display or a painful sidetone defeats the purpose regardless of RF performance.

**Domains and roles.** Applicable HSI domains: human factors engineering (controls, display, audio, key and paddle interface), safety and occupational health (RF exposure, hearing, thermal, battery, hand soldering), training (the operations handbook is the only training), maintainability (cells, firmware). Not applicable: manpower, personnel selection, habitability. HSI co-owners (NPR 7123.1D §5.2.1.3): the ETA, the SMA TA and the Health and Medical TA, all held by the owner (§4.1); SE HB App. R §5.2.1 relationship to the Technical Authorities: the HSI integrator reports every HSI finding, requirement and risk to the owner in those three roles through the review package. HSI integrator: Claude. HSI decision maker and test subject: the owner; Technician-class friends are secondary evaluators (MOE-008). HSI risks are entered in `docs/risk/register.json` like any other risk.

**HSI requirements.** Written as L1 and L2 requirements with the `hsi` tag and verified like all others. Project terms: gate (life-cycle review at which a baseline is set), delta TRR (`TRR-Dn`, review before a new run-for-record series, no baseline), review deck (the controlled slide deck of charter §4 item 2 presented slide by slide at every gate and delta TRR), procurement release (CDR approval authorizing vendor orders), kit assembly (the owner's hand soldering of through-hole and exposed-pad parts, SI-031), first power-on (the first powered bench test on the delivered unit after TRR), dev-board run (Bench evidence on a bare Pico 2 with `credit: false`), OnAir (demonstration or validation during actual amateur operation, after the on-air delta TRR), design maturity level (DML-2 to DML-8, the project scale of §6.0 mapped to App. E TRLs), PBS element (a requirement module of charter §6 used as a product breakdown element, §3.2), status note (`docs/plan/status/status-YYYY-MM-DD.md`, the between-review TPM report of §7.4), hand-assembly list (`cwht-MB-rev<X>-hand-assembly.csv` in the hardware release package, the product-baseline list of parts the owner solders).
