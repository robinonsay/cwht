Word count 24 (the group A word "can" of WR-07 is avoided, so the owner's "a hand can reach" is carried by "accessible", defined in the rationale). How the scope is made verifiable (F1; revision 3, R2-F1): (1) the accessible surface set is generated, not chosen: a scripted check on the assembly model of each released enclosure option (the same model tooling as TC-SYS-076) moves a test finger from outside in every direction and lists every face it touches; the finger proposed is 12 mm in diameter with a hemispherical tip and an 80 mm reach, as this author recalls the IEC 61032 test probe B that IEC 62368-1 uses for accessibility (not in the corpus; TBR, confirmed at PDR, where the pocket-carry case of HZ-003 C1, fabric pressed on a guard, is checked against it); (2) the set has no declared exemptions: a face of the case, heatsink, guard, plates, knobs, facade, fasteners, connector shells, the antenna radiator or the SMA body beyond the nut is in it if the finger touches it, and a guard is itself in it; (3) TC-SYS-077 computes the temperature of every face of the set and passes only if the hottest is at most 48 C; (4) a lumped chain bounds each face as follows: every face is assigned to the thermal-chain node of the part it belongs to and takes that node's temperature, computed at the part's heat-entry end (the hottest point of a lumped resistance); a part spanning several nodes takes the hottest of them; a guard not joined to the heatsink is its own node, coupled to the heatsink and to ambient by convection and radiation; the face-to-node table is an artifact of TC-SYS-077. Consequence for option C (status note reading 1, now carried by the statement): a heatsink may be internal, or exposed with every accessible face at or below 48 C, or behind a guard whose openings the finger does not pass, with the guard itself at or below 48 C; fins behind such a guard are not in the set. This is a tightening only: no surface bound before at 48 C is relaxed, and surfaces that were not hand-hold areas (an exposed heatsink, a guard, a plate, the pocket side of the case, the antenna) are now bound at 48 C, below the 55 C burn figure of HZ-003. Revision 2's wording, "every external surface" read as every exterior face of the part models, is replaced because it either binds guarded fins that no hand reaches, stricter than owner input 1 and reading 1, or leaves "external" undefined for a recessed or guarded face (R2-F1; section 3).

## 2. Reason

Origin: owner input SI-037 (2026-09-26, `docs/requirements/l0-stakeholder/stakeholder-inputs.md`; `docs/reviews/SRR/minutes.md` section "Schedule and enclosure inputs"), which opened the enclosure to a catalog metal box (option B) and a printed, metal-coated case (option C), with PCBWay CNC (option A) as the fallback. The lead SE disposition in the minutes named this CR and held every requirement change until after the `baseline/srr` tag (779f93f, 2026-09-27). Revision 1 carried SI-037's plan of two parallel prototypes. The owner inputs of 2026-09-27 (SI-039; status note `docs/plan/status/status-2026-09-27.md`) replace that plan by a sequence, "we're going to do basically one item at a time. I think the cheapest option is the 3D printed option", with the CNC order if "that doesn't work", and settle the questions of revision 1 and of the section 6 review (table in the header).

The baseline cannot carry either plan: REQ-SYS-109 names a solution (CNC-machined anodized aluminum), which a printed case cannot meet, and WR-05 (02 §4.2) admits a solution in a statement only as a constraint; CON-015, NGO-006 and NGO-007 name the CNC aluminum enclosure (INSP-001 finding-16, lien due before PDR); REQ-SYS-124 requires the legend "marked into its enclosure metal", which a printed case cannot meet; REQ-SYS-113 bounds only "hand-hold surfaces", which the owner now wants widened to every surface a hand can reach. ADR-008 section 7 names as a revisit condition "the owner asks for a printed enclosure for a prototype unit", which SI-037 and SI-039 meet, and 06 §14.1 item (a) (enclosure concept) makes the choice a formal trade study at PDR; SEMP customization 11 (i) no longer covers it because more than one viable alternative exists.

Not making the change (SE HB §6.2.1.2.5): the PDR products would design an option C enclosure that fails a baselined requirement by construction, TC-SYS-075 and TC-SYS-086 would fail on it, and the delivered unit would need a product waiver. Workaround while the CR is open: none needed; no enclosure is ordered or printed to a release before CDR, and the PDR trade study proceeds under SI-037 and SI-039 while this CR is reviewed and dispositioned. PDR products that depend on this CR are marked "at risk" until the disposition (status note reading 9).

| Q2 | Is the option C acceptance set of section 5 Effectivity item 3 the right one, and when does the CNC order go? | Yes to the set: accept option C when it meets items (a) to (f) of item 3 on the one assembled unit (thermal and hand safety, shielding, bond, legend and jack markings with the rub on a coupon, antenna-port proof load, edges, plus the PETG margin), all non-destructive. Your four criteria (thermal, shielding, hand safety, legend) are in it; bond, antenna-port load and edges are added because each is a baselined requirement the delivered enclosure must meet. Drop, drip and mass are not tested on the only unit before the choice. The PDR memo fixes the values, not the set. The CNC order goes when a failed item cannot be corrected by one reprint or one coating change, judged at the option C evaluation; the held CNC package is a pre-approved alternate, so the order can go the same day without a CR. A fallback moves TRR later by about three weeks (Schedule row). |
