## 1. Verification versus validation on cwht

SE HB §2.4 and Appendix B: **verification** is proof of compliance with specifications ("Did I build the product right?"); **validation** is proof that the product accomplishes its intended purpose based on stakeholder expectations and the ConOps ("Am I building the right product?"). Both may use test, analysis, inspection or demonstration. Verification relates back to the approved requirements set; validation relates back to the ConOps (SE HB §2.4).

| | Verification | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Does the realized product meet every "shall"? | Does the radio let the owner and friends do what the ConOps says, to the MOE values? |
| Judged against | `docs/requirements/**/requirements.json` (L1 SYS, L2 RX/TX/PWR/CTL/ME/SW), `docs/icd/ICD-*.md` shall statements, hazard controls in `docs/safety/hazards.json` | `docs/conops/conops.md` scenarios `OPS-NNN` (nominal and off-nominal) and `docs/requirements/l0-stakeholder/expectations.json` (NGO-NNN, MOE-NNN, success criteria) |
| Who judges | Claude conducts and records; owner witnesses Bench/OnAir and authorizes acceptability | Owner as the user (SE HB §5.4.1.2: validation is performed by anticipated operators and users whenever possible); friends receiving units (SI-019) are additional users |
| Cases | `TC-<MOD>-NNN` in `docs/test_cases/<module>/test_cases.json`, any module except `VAL` | `TC-VAL-NNN` in `docs/test_cases/val/test_cases.json`, with the two mandatory setup lines below |
| Matrix | Requirements verification matrix (SE HB App. D), section 7.1 | Validation matrix (SE HB App. E), section 7.2 |
| Report | `docs/vv/reports/<TC-ID>-r<N>.md` from `docs/templates/verification-report.md` | Same template; `requirements_baseline` also identifies the expectations and ConOps version used (SE HB §5.4.1.2.4); result feeds the validation matrix |
| Discrepancy | `NCR-NNN` (section 10) | `NCR-NNN`; if the product meets its requirements but not the expectation, the disposition is a `CR-NNN` against the requirement or ConOps, not a rework |
| Completion | Requirement status `Verified`, then `Closed` at SAR (section 5.3) | MOE and OPS rows `Validated` in the validation matrix; owner acceptance at SAR |

**Case modules.** Charter section 6 lists the requirement modules SYS, RX, TX, PWR, CTL, ME and SW-<SUB> and, since charter commit b8214ca, the test-only case modules this process defines: `VAL` (validation cases, `docs/test_cases/val/`), `ATP` (acceptance cases, `docs/test_cases/atp/`), `SW-COV` (coverage, section 12), `SW-REG` (regression, section 10.5) and `SW-TOOL` (toolchain proof and tool accreditation runs, `docs/test_cases/sw-tool/test_cases.json`, case `TC-SW-TOOL-001` of `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 3.1). Each test-only module has a `test_cases.json` and no requirements file, and its cases cite requirements of any module (02 section 2.2). `SW-BOOT` and `SW-CFG` (loaded code and data, section 11.1) are firmware modules of 07 section 4 with their own requirement files and cases, not test-only. After SRR any further module token is a `CR-NNN`.

**Validation cases.** Validation is not deferred to SAR. Per SE HB §2.4 it is repeated in each phase with the phase products: ConOps walk-through against the L1 requirements at SRR, architecture and control-layout review against `OPS-NNN` at PDR (3D-printed facade and knob fit-check parts, SI-012, are the mock-ups), emulator scenarios (or, where the emulator is not accredited, dev-board runs, section 4) that replay `OPS-NNN` at CDR, and the delivered unit on the air in Phase D. Each of these is a `TC-VAL-NNN` case whose `setup` carries two mandatory lines:

- `Validates: <ids>.` naming the `OPS-NNN` and `MOE-NNN` the case validates, for example `Validates: OPS-004, OPS-005, MOE-005.`
- `Phase: <token>.` with one token of `SRR`, `PDR`, `CDR`, `TRR`, `TRR-D<n>` (the delta review number, for example `TRR-D1`), `SAR` or `Ops` (Phase E use). The token names the gate whose package presents the result.

`tools/traceability.py` reads both lines (rule 7.3.5); a `TC-VAL` case never closes a requirement (row key `VAL` of section 5.2), and its `requirement_ids` list the requirements it exercises.

**Keying is a core expectation.** SI-018 requires operation with a straight key and with iambic paddles (built-in electronic keyer). Every validation scenario that involves transmitting is executed twice, once with each key type, and the acceptance test set (section 11) contains both. A unit that works with only one key type has not been validated.

**MOPs.** NPR 7123.1D §3.2.8.2 directs special attention to demonstrating the MOPs defined for each MOE. Every verification report whose requirement carries `mop_ids` records the measured value; Claude copies it into `docs/plan/tpm.json` the same day.

No git commit trailer is used for this purpose; commit trailers follow the CM plan (`docs/process/05-configuration-and-data-management.md` section 4.5).

## 3. Methods

The four methods are those of SE HB §5.3.1.2.1 (sidebar "Methods of Verification") and Appendix B. Each requirement carries exactly one `verification_method` (schema `docs/requirements/schema.json`), assigned when it is written (charter section 7). A test case's `verification_method` is the method it implements. SE HB §6.1 notes that complete verification of a requirement may need more than one method; on cwht the requirement then either closes by the one method section 6.2 names for its kind, with the other methods recorded as supporting cases or as data inside the closing report, or it is split into companion requirements, one per method, each with its own closing case (the method is assigned at definition, 02 section 4.4). A requirement never has two closing methods.

| Method | SE HB meaning | Use on cwht |
|---|---|---|
| **Analysis** | Mathematical modeling and analytical techniques to predict compliance from calculated data or from lower-level verifications; includes modeling and simulation and verification by similarity | LTspice `.ac`/`.tran` decks with automated pass/fail checkers, Python link, power, thermal and noise budgets, S-parameter models of the filter chain, timing analyses of firmware paths, worst-case tolerance analyses. Chosen when the quantity cannot be measured with instruments the project will have (section 6), or when the requirement is a design margin (for example, component derating) rather than a behavior |
| **Inspection** | Visual examination of the realized product, including drawings, documents and records | KiCad ERC and DRC runs, BOM against approved parts list, rendered schematic and layout images reviewed against checklists, `jsonschema` validation of every JSON artifact, `tools/traceability.py` checks, driver-to-datasheet traceability (every register access in a rustos `pico2` driver cites the RP2350 datasheet or Cortex-M33 documentation section it implements; charter section 9; coding-standard rule CS-25 of 07 section 7.5, checked by item CK-CODE-E4 of `docs/templates/peer-review-checklist-code.md` and recorded in the code INSP record), receipt inspection of the delivered assembly (part orientation, solder, markings, enclosure dimensions with calipers) |
| **Demonstration** | Showing that use of the product achieves the requirement; confirmation of capability without detailed data gathering | Owner operates the radio: tunes, keys with a straight key and with paddles, hears sidetone, completes a QSO. Used for functional and human-interface requirements where pass/fail is observable without measurement |
| **Test** | Use of the realized product to obtain detailed data under controlled conditions | Bench measurements with the owner's instruments (section 6), automated firmware tests with recorded numbers (element timing, debounce, state transitions), Pico-based logic capture of key, sidetone, T/R and PA-enable edges, tinySA Ultra spectrum measurements; emulator scenarios contribute event order only (section 5.2) |

Rule: if a requirement states a number with a unit and the number can be measured with an instrument the project will have at TRR, its method is Test. If it states a number that cannot be measured with those instruments, the method is Analysis and the rationale field says why. If it states a capability, the method is Demonstration. If it states a physical or documentary property, the method is Inspection.

**Hazard-tracing requirements.** A requirement traces to a hazard when its own `hazard_ids` is non-empty or when `docs/safety/hazards.json` names it in a hazard's `requirement_ids` or in a control's `control_req_ids` (charter section 7). Every hazard-tracing `REQ-SW-*` is verified by Test (HostUnit, Emulation or Bench per section 5.2); there is no exception (SWE-192; `docs/process/rmm.json` row SWE-192, disposition FC). For firmware a test is always possible: platform-independent logic on the host with the simulated clock, and target timing, peripherals and everything else on the bench with the instruments of section 6.1 (Pico-based logic capture, tinySA Ultra, multimeter); Emulation is supporting evidence except for ordering-only requirements (section 5.2). If the owner ever wants an Analysis alternative for a software hazard control, the RMM row SWE-192 is changed to disposition T with rationale and owner approval before SRR, and this paragraph is amended by CR to cite that row; until then any hazard-tracing `REQ-SW-*` without a Test closing case fails rule 7.3.6. A non-software hazard-tracing requirement (modules SYS, RX, TX, PWR, CTL, ME), which SWE-192 does not govern, uses Test where an instrument of section 6.1 can measure it; otherwise its method is Analysis, its `verification_note` begins `Analysis accepted per RSK-NNN`, and the risk register carries that `RSK-NNN` for the untested hazard control (hazard link rule, `docs/process/06-risk-and-decision-analysis.md` section 3).

## 4. Evidence classes (test case `type`)

The `type` field of `docs/test_cases/schema.json` names the form of the product and the environment in which evidence is produced. This is the SE HB §6.1 notion of product form (simulated, breadboard, engineering unit, final unit), collapsed to six classes. Charter section 9 and the schema both name the class Simulation; Analysis is reserved for the verification method (section 3). `05-configuration-and-data-management.md` section 5.3 and `docs/templates/adr.md` section 4.3 use the schema names.

| Class | Product form | Environment and tools | Phase available | Methods it may implement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simulation** | Model of the design (SE HB §6.1 "simulated") | LTspice batch runs with checker scripts in `hardware/sim/`, Python budgets in `tools/`, S-parameter models; tool versions from `tools/toolchain.lock.md` | B onward; re-run on the CDR design data | Analysis |
| **HostUnit** | Firmware crate `cwht-core` compiled for the host against `cwht-hal-mock` (functional equivalent of a breadboard: function only, not form or platform; crate names per `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 1.2) | `cargo test` (or `cargo nextest run`) in `firmware/`, coverage via LLVM source-based coverage (`cargo llvm-cov`). Unit tests (SWE-062) with the repeatability rules of SWE-186: mocked clock and no wall-clock time, fixed seeds for any randomized input printed on failure, no dependence on test order, one command from `tools/toolchain.lock.md`, pinned toolchain and `Cargo.lock`; the suite runs twice and the two result sets must be identical (07 section 9.2, gate G3). A HostUnit report whose rerun on the same tag differs is a `Procedure-correction` NCR against the test | B onward; re-run on every tagged firmware release | Test (software logic only, see section 5.2) |
| **Emulation** | Whole firmware binary for RP2350 on an emulator (SE HB §6.1 form "simulated"). Not a SWE-073 high-fidelity simulation: the SWE-073 note says a high-fidelity simulation typically has the exact processor, processor performance, timing, memory size and interfaces of the target system, and the emulator does not model time (ADR-011). Optional and secondary (charter section 9, SI-026): scenarios assert event ordering in TIMER0 microseconds and never durations, frequencies or timeouts; credit is limited to the peripherals in the per-peripheral credit scope of the emulator's tool validation record `docs/cm/tool-validation/TV-NNN-emulator.md`, summarized in `tools/toolchain.lock.md` (the scope that ADR-011 and `docs/research/emulator-accreditation-and-timer-irq.md` call `ACC-EMU-001`, a research proposal name and not a charter section 6 identifier; charter issue C1 of section 18). No emulator is selected at this date (lock row "RP2350 emulator: not selected"; selection ADR due PDR) | Emulator scenarios in `firmware/emu/` (`cwht-emu`, 07 section 9.4), scripted stimuli on GPIO, timers, serial; if the emulator fails accreditation the 07 section 9.4 item 3 fallback applies (dev-board runs, Bench for timing) | C onward; re-run on every tagged release | Test, Demonstration (event ordering only; never timing; supporting evidence for everything else, section 5.2) |
| **Inspection** | Design data, records, or the delivered assembly | `kicad-cli` ERC/DRC run by its lock path `/Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/MacOS/kicad-cli` (`tools/toolchain.lock.md` section 1; it is not on `PATH`), `jsonschema`, `tools/traceability.py`, rendered images, driver-to-datasheet traceability review (CS-25, CK-CODE-E4), calipers and loupe at receipt | Every phase | Inspection |
| **Bench** | The delivered unit (PCBWay assembly, serial `CWHT-A-NNN`) on the owner's bench. Dev-board cases on a bare Pico 2 (07 section 9.1), marked as below, are risk reduction and emulator accreditation reference behavior and never close a requirement | NanoVNA, tinySA Ultra with calibrated attenuator (ADR-021), Pico-based logic capture, 50 ohm BNC dummy load, adjustable bench supply, multimeter (section 6.1); test fixtures printed on the Bambu H2C | D, after TRR (dev-board cases: B onward, no TRR, 01 section 7.1) | Test, Demonstration |
| **OnAir** | The delivered unit in its enclosure, antenna connected, owner operating under 47 CFR Part 97 | Real 2 m band, a second station (friend's radio or the second cwht unit), the owner's straight key and iambic paddle (SI-034) | D, after the emission requirements of section 6.3 are closed and the on-air delta TRR is approved (section 13) | Demonstration |

**Dev-board cases (the setup-line convention named by charter section 3).** Until a schema CR adds a credit field to `docs/test_cases/schema.json`, a case run on a bare Pico 2 development board has `type` Bench and carries, in its `setup`, the line `Article: pico2-devboard-N.` (N is the number written on the board), the line `Credit: false (dev-board run, charter section 3).` and the credit row line `Credit row: SUPPORT.` (section 5.2), plus the `Configuration:`, `Safety:` and `Environment:` lines of rule 7.3.7. Its reports carry `article: pico2-devboard-N` and `credit: false`. A dev-board case is always supporting evidence and never a closing case, whatever its status (rule 7.3.12). It needs no TRR (01 section 7.1). `docs/templates/test_cases.example.json` case `TC-EXAMPLE-006` shows the form.

**Reused components (SWE-211).** Components compiled into the image and not written for cwht are Rust `core` (OSS, shipped with rustc) and the owner's rustos `api` and `pico2` crates (reused from the rustos repository); `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 17.1 is their register, listing the features cwht uses and the cases that exercise each feature. SWE-211 requires them to be tested to the level required to accept custom code. For cwht custom code that level is: requirement-based cases, 100 percent line and region coverage with SWE-189 dispositions, and, for code reached from the safety-critical components of 07 section 14.1, the MC/DC table and the complexity limit of section 12. The reused components meet it as follows:

| Component | Level applied | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| rustos `api` (traits and types, host-buildable) | Same as custom code: feature cases, plus the section 12 coverage measured in the HostUnit run | `TC-SW-COV-001` report, `api` rows |
| rustos `pico2` (target drivers) | Feature cases (register sequences exercised in Emulation within the accredited scope, on the dev board as supporting evidence, and on the delivered unit); coverage measured where the emulator exports it, otherwise the SWE-190 complementary inspection of every uncovered driver path (section 12); an MC/DC table for every driver decision reached from a 07 section 14.1 component (GPIO, TIMER alarms, PWM, ADC, watchdog, critical section) | `TC-SW-COV-001` report, `pico2` rows and MC/DC tables |
| Rust `core` | Feature level only: every `core` feature the register lists as used has a covering HostUnit or Emulation case; structural coverage and MC/DC of `core` are not measured | Register feature list with covering case IDs. The missing structural coverage is relief from SWE-211, recorded as disposition T in `docs/process/rmm.json` row SWE-211 with owner approval at SRR (alignment item A2); until approved it is listed as proposed tailoring in the SRR package (01 section 3.2 row S6) |

A feature without a covering case is not used; a release whose register has such a feature is not tagged (section 10.5). Auto-generated code (`build.rs` constant-table generators only; no SVD-generated crate is used; RMM row SWE-146, 07 section 17.4) is tested as project code by the same cases. Dev tools that are not embedded (emulator, cargo tools, `picotool`) are validated under SWE-136 by their TV records, not under SWE-211.

**Tool accreditation (SWE-136, SWE-070).** Every class needs an accredited tool set before its output is credit-bearing: `tools/toolchain.lock.md` (configuration item of the CM plan Table 4-1 row 27; maintained by Claude) lists each tool, its version, and the sanity check that was run (LTspice reproduces a known RC response within 1 percent; `kicad-cli` DRC reports a seeded violation; the emulator runs a reference binary and reports the known cycle count; the coverage tool reports 100 percent on a fully exercised reference crate; the NanoVNA reads the dummy load at better than 30 dB return loss across 144 to 148 MHz and an open at 0 dB; the tinySA Ultra reads its internal calibration output within its stated tolerance and the NanoVNA generator level through the attenuator whose S21 was measured that session, ADR-021; the Pico-based logic capture reports the period of a Pico-generated reference pulse train within one sample). A report produced with an unlisted tool version is not accepted. Prerequisites per class, each recorded with its version, install source and path in `tools/toolchain.lock.md` before any credit-bearing run (the lock is the single record of tool state; this document restates none of it): coverage needs the `cargo-llvm-cov` binary and the LLVM tools, both with the tool validation record `docs/cm/tool-validation/TV-NNN-cargo-llvm-cov.md`; Inspection needs `kicad-cli` at its lock path; Simulation needs the LTspice batch wrapper `tools/run_sim.py` (written by Claude before the first Simulation case is `Active`, PDR); Emulation needs the emulator selected by the PDR ADR and its TV record with the per-peripheral credit scope; Bench needs the tinySA Ultra TV record of ADR-021 and a TV record for the Pico-based logic capture, both before the TRR that opens the campaign using them. A tool absent from the lock, or listed without an accredited TV record, produces developer evidence only (CM plan section 9.1). MC/DC follows `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 9.6 and section 12 of this document: no accredited Rust MC/DC instrumentation exists at this date, so independently reviewed independence-pair tables plus stable-toolchain line and region coverage (MSR-13) plus nightly branch and condition coverage (MSR-14) are the Rev A method (RMM row SWE-219, disposition T). Nightly toolchains are not permitted for release or credit-bearing evidence builds; MSR-14 on the pinned nightly is a required, non-credit measure (`tools/toolchain.lock.md` section 1; charter section 10).

## 5. When verification credit is taken

### 5.1 Principles (charter section 9)

1. **Run for the record is on the delivered unit.** SE HB §6.1: run-for-record verification is usually performed on the final controlled unit, credit on lower-fidelity units needs explicit project approval and a likeness argument (the unit is sufficiently like the final unit in the areas verified), and buying off a "shall" on a prototype is typically not acceptable. On cwht the approved lower-fidelity credit is limited to the section 5.2 software rows (HostUnit; Emulation for ordering-only requirements); the likeness argument is the platform-independence statement in each case's `setup` field and the approval is the owner's PDR approval of the V&V plan (section 14). The cwht Unit 1 (`CWHT-A-001`) is an engineering unit that becomes the final product (SE HB §6.1 permits this when traceability over components and handling is kept; `docs/vv/adp/CWHT-A-001/as-built.md`, the unit as-built record of the CM plan Table 4-1 row 38, holds that record).
2. **Analysis closes only requirements whose method is Analysis.** A Simulation case against a Test-method requirement is risk reduction (SI-010, "must work at first power-on") and is listed as supporting evidence in the matrix, but the requirement stays `Active` until the Bench or software Test runs.
3. **Pre-build software evidence closes software requirements only under section 5.2.** Otherwise it too is supporting evidence.
4. **Inspection closes at the point the inspected item is baselined:** design-level inspections (DRC, ERC, schema, traceability) at CDR on the product baseline; as-built inspections at receipt of the unit; document inspections at the review that baselines the document.
5. **Analysis credit is taken at CDR and confirmed at SAR.** The analysis must use the CDR design data (final part values, layout parasitics where modeled). At SAR the physical configuration audit (charter section 8) confirms the as-built values match; if a CR changed an analyzed value after CDR, the analysis is re-run before SAR.

### 5.2 Credit rules by method

**Firmware release identification.** A credit run on software uses a tagged, released commit with a version description: tag `release/FW-vX.Y.Z` with `firmware/releases/VDD-vX.Y.Z.md`, or a release candidate `release/FW-vX.Y.Z-rcN` with its own VDD `firmware/releases/VDD-vX.Y.Z-rcN.md`, per the CM plan `docs/process/05-configuration-and-data-management.md` sections 4.3, 7.3 and 8.1 (SWE-187). Reports record the version as `vX.Y.Z+<short SHA>` in `firmware_version`. (`docs/process/01-lifecycle-and-reviews.md` sections 2 and 7.3 and `07-software-engineering-plan.md` sections 3 and 13 use the same identifiers; the CM plan is authoritative for identifiers, charter section 8.)

**Credit row line.** Every case states which row below applies in one `setup` line `Credit row: <key>.`, using the key of the first column; for keys `T-SW-LOGIC` and `T-SW-ORDER` the line is followed by the likeness argument (why host or emulator execution is representative). A case that closes some of the requirements it cites and supports others carries the key of its closing row; the method rule makes it supporting for the rest. The procedure reviewer checks the key (section 8.3 item 2), and the planned tool check `CLOSING_CLASS` reads it (rule 7.3.3).

| Row key | Requirement `verification_method` and kind | Closing case (`verification_method` equal to the requirement's, `type` in this column) | Supporting evidence only | Credit event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `A` | Analysis | Simulation | Bench measurement that corroborates the analysis (recorded as a Test case against a companion Test requirement, or as an `expected_artifacts` plot in the analysis report) | CDR run on product baseline; confirmed at SAR |
| `I` | Inspection | Inspection | none | CDR (design data), receipt (as-built), baselining review (documents) |
| `T-HW` | Test, hardware or system requirement (module SYS, RX, TX, PWR, CTL, ME) | Bench on the delivered unit | Simulation, HostUnit, Emulation, dev-board cases | TRR-authorized Bench run on `CWHT-A-001`, witnessed by owner |
| `T-SW-LOGIC` | Test, software requirement (module SW, SW-<SUB>), platform-independent logic | HostUnit | Simulation; Emulation (event ordering); dev-board cases | The credit run on a tagged release (SWE-187) whose VDD names it: the requirement becomes `Verified` on that report (section 5.3; charter section 9). It becomes `Closed` only when, in addition to the section 5.3 Closed conditions, the PCA-05 line of a delivered unit's `docs/vv/adp/<unit>/as-built.md` names that release (rule 7.3.4, `CLOSED_NOT_INSTALLED`). On a later release the requirement keeps `Verified` while that release's regression report `TC-SW-REG-001-r<N>.md` (section 10.5) has `credit: true` and `result: Pass`; it returns to `Active` when that report fails or is missing |
| `T-SW-TARGET` | Test, software requirement that depends on target timing, peripherals, interrupts, DMA, clocks or power states | Bench (Pico-based logic capture, tinySA Ultra, multimeter; section 6.1) | HostUnit with the simulated clock, Simulation, Emulation (event ordering only, accredited scope), dev-board cases | TRR-authorized Bench run on `CWHT-A-001`, witnessed by the owner, on the tagged release named in the VDD; `Closed` needs the PCA-05 line as in `T-SW-LOGIC`. Installing a later release on the unit returns the requirement to `Active` until its Bench case has a credit report on that release, unless that release's regression selection (section 10.5, recorded in its `TC-SW-REG-001` report) shows that the requirement's `design_refs` are untouched and the rustos commit is unchanged, in which case the credit carries forward |
| `T-SW-ORDER` | Test, software requirement that states only an event order (for example, PA-enable rises only after the T/R command and the settle event) | Emulation, only for peripherals inside the emulator TV record scope (ADR-011, 07 section 9.4); otherwise Bench | HostUnit, Simulation | As `T-SW-LOGIC`; if the emulator is not accredited at PDR (07 section 9.4 item 3 fallback, recorded in the PDR decision memo) the case is re-typed Bench by CR with key `T-SW-TARGET` and the emulator run is `credit: false` |
| `D` | Demonstration | Bench or OnAir on the delivered unit | Emulation demonstration, dev-board cases | Owner-operated run on the delivered unit |
| `SUPPORT` | any | never closing | A case whose `verification_method` differs from the requirement's; every dev-board case (section 4) | none |
| `VAL` | validation (module `VAL`) | never closes a requirement; its result sets the validation rows (section 7.2) | none | the gate named by its `Phase:` line |

"Platform-independent logic" means the requirement can be stated and checked entirely in terms of the inputs and outputs of a Rust module behind the HAL trait boundary, with time supplied by a mocked clock. Keyer element timing, iambic mode logic, straight-key debounce state machine, menu and tuning-step logic, and command sequencing (SWE-134 e, h) qualify. GPIO edge latency, PA-enable-to-RF timing, ADC sampling and encoder interrupt handling do not; they close at Bench with the Pico-based logic capture (row `T-SW-TARGET`). Emulation rule (charter section 9, SI-026, ADR-011): an Emulation scenario expresses time only in TIMER0 microseconds and asserts order, never a duration, frequency or timeout; a scenario that asserts a duration is a `Procedure-correction` NCR against the case.

### 5.3 Status semantics

Test case `status` (schema enum): `Draft` (written, not reviewed) → `Active` (reviewed per section 8.3, ready to run) → `Passed` or `Failed` (result of the most recent run-for-record) or `Blocked` (cannot run: instrument absent, prerequisite NCR open, TRR not held). A `Failed` case returns to `Active` only when its NCR is dispositioned and retest is authorized (section 10). A retired case is status `Blocked` with a `setup` that starts `Retired by ` (02 section 11.3); it is excluded from closing and supporting counts.

Requirement `status` (schema enum):

- `Draft` → `Active` (baselined at SRR for L1, PDR for L2).
- `Verified`: every live closing case (section 7.1: not retired, not a dev-board case, not a `TC-VAL` case, row key a closing key of section 5.2) is `Passed` with a report whose front matter has `credit: true` and `result: Pass`; for `REQ-SW-*` that report's `firmware_version` is a release tag named in its VDD, and on a later release the row rule of section 5.2 holds; no NCR with status other than `Closed` cites the requirement (rule 7.3.4).
- `Closed`: `Verified`; the validation rows that depend on it (rows of the `TC-VAL` cases that cite it) are `Validated`; the Bench smoke set of section 11.2 stages 4 to 6 is `Passed` on the delivered unit; the owner has accepted at SAR (`docs/reviews/SAR/decision-memo.md` exists, and the status change is made in the commit that records the signed memo, 01 section 8.4 row 5); and, for `REQ-SW-*`, the PCA-05 line of a delivered unit's `docs/vv/adp/<unit>/as-built.md` names the release of the credited report (charter section 9). The PCA-05 line reads `Installed release: vX.Y.Z+<short SHA>; picotool verify match; YYYY-MM-DD`; Claude writes it the day the owner flashes the unit at section 11.2 stage 4 (CM plan section 7.2 row PCA-05 and section 8.3 item 2).
- Retired: status `Closed` with tag `retired` and a `rationale` that starts `Retired by ` (charter section 6; 02 section 11.3). A retired requirement gets no matrix row (it is listed in the report's retired list), is exempt from the `Verified` and `Closed` checks of rule 7.3.4, and a live case that cites it fails rule 7.3.2.

Claude updates the JSON the day a report is filed; `tools/traceability.py` fails when a status does not match the evidence on disk (rules 7.3.2 to 7.3.4; implementation status in section 7.4).

### 7.3 Rules the tool enforces (charter section 7 plus V&V additions)

1. Unique IDs across all requirements and cases; module prefix of every case ID equals the file's `module`. Files under `docs/templates/` are never read (the tool globs `docs/test_cases/**/test_cases.json` only).
2. Every requirement has a parent or a self-derived rationale; every `Draft` or `Active` requirement has at least one closing case; every case cites at least one existing requirement. A live case that cites a retired requirement fails. A retired case (status `Blocked`, `setup` starting `Retired by `) is excluded from closing and supporting counts and, when it cites a live requirement, names its superseding case (`superseded by TC-...`) (02 section 11.3).
3. A case is closing only when its `verification_method` equals the requirement's and its `Credit row:` key is a closing key of section 5.2 for the requirement's module and the case's `type`: `A` with Simulation, `I` with Inspection, `T-HW` with Bench for modules SYS, RX, TX, PWR, CTL and ME, `T-SW-LOGIC` with HostUnit, `T-SW-TARGET` with Bench and `T-SW-ORDER` with Emulation or Bench for modules SW and SW-<SUB>, `D` with Bench or OnAir. Every other citing case is flagged supporting in the matrix. `TC-VAL` cases are never closing.
4. A requirement with `status: Verified` meets the `Verified` conditions of section 5.3: for every closing case a report with `credit: true` and `result: Pass` (for `REQ-SW-*`, on a release tag with a VDD, and on a later release the section 5.2 row rule), and no NCR with status other than `Closed` cites it. A requirement with `status: Closed` also meets the `Closed` conditions of section 5.3: dependent validation rows `Validated`, the SAR decision memo present, the Bench smoke set of section 11.2 stages 4 to 6 `Passed`, and for `REQ-SW-*` the PCA-05 line naming the credited release. Retired requirements are exempt.
5. Every `TC-VAL-NNN` case names at least one existing `OPS-NNN` or `MOE-NNN` after `Validates:` and one token after `Phase:` in its `setup` (section 1).
6. Every hazard-tracing `REQ-SW-*` (section 3: own `hazard_ids` non-empty, or named in `docs/safety/hazards.json` in a hazard's `requirement_ids` or a control's `control_req_ids`) has a closing case of method Test (SWE-192; no exception). Every other hazard-tracing requirement has a closing case of method Test, or has method Analysis and a `verification_note` beginning `Analysis accepted per RSK-` naming a risk that exists in `docs/risk/register.json`. A disagreement between `hazards.json` and a requirement's `hazard_ids` (`HAZARD_INVERSE`) blocks the readiness declaration of every gate.
7. Bench and OnAir cases have an `instruments` list drawn from section 6.1 (which includes the tinySA Ultra of ADR-021), or for OnAir from the second station and keys of the section 4 OnAir row, and a `setup` containing the lines `Article:`, `Configuration:`, `Safety:` and `Environment:`.
8. Every report matching `docs/vv/reports/TC-*-r*.md` has front matter whose `test_case` exists, whose `requirement_ids` are a subset of that case's `requirement_ids`, and, for a `TC-VAL` case, whose `validates` list is a subset of the tokens after `Validates:` in the case's `setup`; every NCR's `test_case_ids`, `retest_cases`, `regression_cases`, `requirement_ids` and `hazard_ids` exist; every entry of an `artifacts` list in a report or NCR names a file that exists with the listed SHA-256. Files in `docs/vv/reports/` that do not match `TC-*-r*.md` (`vv-report.md`, `part97-compliance.md`, `receipt-inspection-<n>.md`) carry no case front matter and are not read.
9. Software requirements to non-conformances (NPR 7150.2D §3.12.1 Table 1, Class A): the report lists, per `REQ-SW-*`, every NCR that cites it.
10. `tools/traceability.py --regression <design_ref...>` prints the regression set of section 10.5 (every `Passed` Bench case whose requirements' `design_refs` intersect the arguments, plus every `TC-ATP-NNN` case).
11. Test and verification procedures stay consistent with the requirements (SWE-071): a live case whose status is `Active`, `Passed` or `Failed` and that cites a requirement whose `description`, `verification_method`, `verification_note` or `tbr` changed in a commit later than the commit in which the case last became `Active` is flagged `CASE_STALE`; the case returns to `Draft` under section 8.2.
12. A case whose `setup` carries `Article: pico2-devboard-` is never closing, and it fails when its `setup` lacks `Credit: false` or carries a `Credit row:` key other than `SUPPORT` (`DEVBOARD_CASE_CLOSING`; section 4).

The output `docs/vv/traceability-report.md` has sections: summary counts and closure burndown (requirements by status, cases by status, NCRs by severity and status), verification matrix, validation matrix, rule violations, and the requirement-to-NCR list. It is an entrance product of every review (charter section 7) and its closure percentages are TPMs in `docs/plan/tpm.json`.

### 8.2 Authoring rules

- One case verifies one to a few closely related requirements; a case that needs more than 25 procedure steps is split.
- Acceptance criteria are objective: a number with tolerance, a logged event, an observable state, stated as one decision rule so that no reading is neither Pass nor Fail (for a measured limit, "Pass if the reading plus the instrument accuracy is within the limit; otherwise Fail"). "Works correctly" is rejected by the reviewer.
- Automated cases (`Simulation`, `HostUnit`, `Emulation`, `Inspection` by tool) have an `automation_ref` that a fresh checkout can run with one command from `tools/toolchain.lock.md`; the `procedure` still lists the human-readable steps the automation performs. HostUnit cases obey the SWE-186 repeatability rules of section 4.
- Every case that involves keying exists in two variants or has two procedure blocks: straight key, and iambic paddles (SI-018).
- Step order at the end of every procedure, stated once here: the penultimate step records instrument calibration state and tool or firmware versions in the report; the final step is the stop-and-open-an-NCR instruction (Bench and OnAir) or `On any assertion failure stop and open an NCR` (automated classes).
- Cases are written by the test author from the requirement, its rationale and the ICDs, before the design is reviewed against them; the author records the `Credit row:` line of section 5.2 in `setup`, and Bench and OnAir cases also carry the `Credentials:` line of section 8.1.
- Evidence of one requirement is not booked twice: coverage and MC/DC evidence belongs to `TC-SW-COV-001` (section 12) and never forms part of the acceptance criteria of a functional case.
- Cases are placed under configuration control at SRR (L1) and PDR (L2) with the requirements; after that, edits go through CR (charter section 7) except editorial fixes logged in the commit.
- **Requirement changes (SWE-071).** Every `CR-NNN` that changes a requirement lists the cases citing it (the output of the verification matrix row). The test author updates those cases and the affected `docs/vv/plan.md` sections before the CR is closed. Each updated case returns to `Draft` until the section 8.3 review makes it `Active` again; rule 7.3.11 (`CASE_STALE`) flags a case that was not updated. A requirement changed through an NCR `Design-change` disposition follows the same rule (section 10.4).

### 8.3 Procedure review checklist (used by the independent reviewer before `Active`)

1. Cites at least one existing, live requirement; method matches the requirement or the case carries `Credit row: SUPPORT.`
2. Class is allowed for the method (section 4 table); the `Credit row:` key is stated and correct for the requirement's module and kind (section 5.2); a dev-board case carries the section 4 lines and key `SUPPORT`; a `TC-VAL` case carries key `VAL`.
3. Acceptance criteria are numeric or observable, form one decision rule, and the requirement's tolerance exceeds the instrument or tool uncertainty stated in `instruments` or `setup`.
4. Steps are executable in order by the owner without Claude present (Bench, OnAir) or by one command (automated).
5. Instruments are in section 6.1 (which includes the tinySA Ultra of ADR-021), or for OnAir the second station and keys of the section 4 OnAir row; anything else is a `Blocked` case with the alternative named.
6. `Safety:` and `Credentials:` lines present for Bench and OnAir; no transmit step without the dummy load or a reference to the OnAir authorization in `docs/reviews/TRR-Dn/decision-memo.md` (section 6.3).
7. Expected artifacts are sufficient to reproduce the pass/fail decision from the report alone.
8. Keying cases cover both key types (SI-018).
9. Hazard-tracing requirements (section 3, including those named only in `hazards.json`): a `REQ-SW-*` has a closing case of method Test (SWE-192; a case set that leaves such a requirement without one fails review); a non-software hazard requirement is verified by Test or carries the `Analysis accepted per RSK-NNN` note with an existing risk (rule 7.3.6).
10. Case author differs from the author of every design element or code module the case verifies (charter section 2); the reviewer records both authoring invocations in the INSP record (section 2).
11. A `TC-VAL` case carries the `Validates:` and `Phase:` lines of section 1.

## 10. Discrepancies and non-conformances

### 10.1 Stop rule

SE HB §5.3.1.2.2 and §5.4.1.2.2: when a discrepancy is observed (any variance from the required or expected outcome, configuration or result), the activity stops and a discrepancy report is generated. On cwht: the operator stops the step, leaves the setup intact where safe (SE HB §5.3.1.2.3 recommends analyzing while the configuration is still intact), photographs the setup and instrument readings, and Claude opens `docs/vv/ncr/NCR-NNN.md` from `docs/templates/ncr.md` before any further step of that case runs. Automated classes stop the run at the failing assertion and open the NCR from the log.

### 10.2 Classification

The first analysis decides whether the discrepancy is a **product non-conformance** or a **procedure or conduct discrepancy** (wrong setup, uncalibrated instrument, mis-stated criterion, tool defect). A procedure discrepancy is still recorded as an NCR (SWE-201 includes defects in tools) with disposition `Procedure-correction`; the case is corrected by CR or editorial fix, the report records the correction, and the run repeats. Only product non-conformances affect requirement status.

### 10.3 Severity (SWE-202 minimum classes, mapped to cwht)

| Severity | SWE-202 class | cwht meaning | Immediate effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 Safety or regulatory** | loss of life or loss of vehicle | Injury or burn risk, fire or battery hazard, RF exposure above the limits of 47 CFR 1.1310 or 2.1093 as evaluated in `docs/design/analysis/rf-exposure-evaluation.md`, emission outside 47 CFR 97.305/97.307 limits, damage that destroys the unit, violation of a SWE-134 provision in any of the safety-critical components of `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 14.1 (the single authoritative list, charter section 10), or an unintended transmission | All powered testing stops until the owner approves the disposition; process assessment mandatory (SWE-204); risk register entry |
| **S2 Mission** | mission success | A `KDR` or `Baseline` requirement is not met, or an `OPS-NNN` scenario cannot be completed, with no operational workaround (for example, keyer does not work with paddles, or with a straight key: SI-018) | Case `Failed`, requirement blocked from `Verified`; process assessment mandatory (SWE-204) |
| **S3 Workaround** | visible to the user with operational workaround | User-visible deviation with a workaround acceptable to the owner for the current revision | Case `Failed`; disposition may be `Use-as-is` via CR that records the workaround in the operations handbook |
| **S4 Other** | other | Cosmetic, documentation, tool or fixture defect without product effect, `Goal` requirement not met | Case result stands as recorded; the owner schedules the fix in the disposition; the NCR cites a requirement only when the defect affects it |

S1 and S2 are "high severity" for SWE-204. The NCR author proposes severity. The independent software-assurance reviewer confirms it for any NCR touching firmware; the owner confirms it for every other NCR. The owner may raise but not lower a proposed or confirmed severity without a rationale line in the NCR.

### 10.4 Dispositions

| Disposition | Meaning | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| `Rework` | Bring the item to the baseline design (resolder, reflash, reprint) | Retest of the failed case plus regression per section 10.5 |
| `Repair` | Depart from the baseline design to make the item acceptable (jumper wire, part substitution) | `CR-NNN` recording the as-built deviation; retest and regression; physical configuration audit note at SAR |
| `Use-as-is` | Accept the non-conformance | `CR-NNN` that changes or waives the requirement with impact assessment (charter section 7); for S1 never; for S2 only with an owner decision memo |
| `Scrap` or `Return-to-vendor` | Unit or part is not usable | Vendor NCR correspondence filed under the NCR; new unit enters at receipt inspection |
| `Procedure-correction` | The product conforms; the case, fixture, tool or conduct was wrong | Corrected case, corrected tool entry in `tools/toolchain.lock.md` if a tool, rerun |
| `Design-change` | Root cause is the design | `CR-NNN`, re-analysis (section 5.1 principle 5), updated cases (SWE-071, section 8.2), retest and regression |

**COTS, OSS and reused components (SWE-203).** Components in scope (the SWE-203 note lists operating systems, run-time systems, device drivers, code generators, compilers, math libraries and build and CM tools): rustos `api` and `pico2`, Rust `core`, any dev-tool crate in the third-party register (07 section 17.1; no HAL, PAC or SVD-generated crate is used), the `build.rs` table generators (RMM row SWE-146), the Rust toolchain and cargo tooling, the emulator, LTspice, KiCad, `picotool`, the Python venv packages.

- **Reported defects (the mandatory assessment).** At every release tag the software lead reviews the upstream-reported defects of every component in 07 section 17.1 (rustc release notes for the pinned version, RustSec advisories through `cargo audit`, and the issue trackers of rustos, the emulator and each pinned dev-tool crate) and records the review in the VDD reuse-assessment section (07 section 12 item 2 and section 13 row "Reused components"; RMM row SWE-203; 01 section 7.5 row SWE-203). Each reported defect reachable from cwht code opens an NCR with `cots_assessment_required: true`.
- **Defects found by the project.** An NCR whose root cause lies in one of the components records an assessment section (component, version, upstream issue reference, workaround, and whether the component stays in the build) in NCR section 3 and sets `cots_assessment_required: true`.
- **Code audit.** For an S1 or S2 NCR of either kind, the assessment includes a code audit of the component paths the firmware executes (the functions and register sequences reached from cwht code, or the generator templates that produced the code), performed by an independent reviewer invocation and recorded in NCR section 3 ("Code audit performed": reviewer invocation, audited commit or version, paths audited, defects found), completed before the next release tag (SWE-203 note: mandatory code audits for critical defects).

Version pinning changes are CRs against `tools/toolchain.lock.md`.

### 10.5 Retest and regression (SWE-191)

- **Retest:** the failed case reruns for credit after the disposition is implemented; the new report cites the NCR.
- **Firmware regression set:** on every tagged firmware release, the entire HostUnit and Emulation suite reruns (one command, the gate script of `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 8.4), coverage is re-measured (section 12), and the cyber cases (USB firmware loading, key-input command handling; charter section 12 tailoring) are included so that no security regression passes unnoticed. The regression run is recorded as `docs/vv/reports/TC-SW-REG-001-r<N>.md` (case `TC-SW-REG-001` in `docs/test_cases/sw-reg/test_cases.json`, citing every `REQ-SW-*` the suite re-verifies; 07 section 9.3). A release with any failing automated case, or with a third-party register feature lacking a covering case (section 4, SWE-211), is not tagged.
- **Firmware release on a delivered unit:** on every firmware release installed on a delivered unit, Claude runs `tools/traceability.py --regression <changed firmware paths>`, where the arguments are the files under `firmware/` that differ between the previously installed release tag and the new one (`git diff --name-only <old tag> <new tag> -- firmware/`), and records the command and its output in the new release's `TC-SW-REG-001` report. A change of the pinned rustos commit counts as touching every design element. Every Bench case closing a `REQ-SW-*` whose `design_refs` intersect the arguments, plus the ATP stage 4 to 6 smoke set of section 11.2 (every `TC-ATP` case is in the printed set), runs in the delta TRR campaign of 01 section 7.6 (a). Status effects are those of section 5.2 rows `T-SW-LOGIC` and `T-SW-TARGET`.
- **Hardware regression set:** after `Rework`, `Repair` or `Design-change` on the unit, rerun receipt inspection of the touched area, every `Passed` Bench case whose requirements' `design_refs` intersect the touched design elements, and the full `TC-ATP-NNN` set (section 11). `tools/traceability.py --regression <design_ref...>` prints the list (rule 7.3.10, implemented; section 7.4) and Claude records it in the NCR `regression_cases` field.
- **Validation regression:** if a `Design-change` alters anything the owner touches or hears, the affected `TC-VAL-NNN` cases rerun.

### 10.6 Closure

An NCR closes when its disposition is implemented, the closure evidence of the table below is filed, the process assessment (S1, S2) is recorded with its resulting process change or a statement that none is needed, and the owner's closure line is entered. `tools/traceability.py` refuses `Verified` on any requirement with a non-closed NCR (rule 7.3.4, `VERIFIED_WITH_OPEN_NCR`, implemented; section 7.4).

## 11. Acceptance testing and first power-on

### 11.1 Acceptance philosophy

SE HB §5.3 (sidebar "Differences between Verification, Qualification, Acceptance and Certification"): verification is performed once per design; acceptance is a smaller subset performed on each unit to show that workmanship conforms to the verified design; an acceptance data package ships with each unit. SE HB §5.4.1.2.4 note: for a single deliverable the validation process completes acceptance. On cwht:

- **Unit 1 (`CWHT-A-001`)**: the full verification and validation program is the acceptance. SAR accepts the unit on the complete matrices (NPR 7123.1D App. G Table G-11: product verification results, product validation results, compliance with acceptance criteria, as-built documentation).
- **Subsequent units for friends (SI-019, `CWHT-A-002` onward)**: the acceptance test procedure (ATP) is the set of cases in module `ATP`, `docs/test_cases/atp/test_cases.json`, IDs `TC-ATP-NNN`. Each ATP case cites the requirements it re-verifies per unit. The ATP is written by CDR (charter section 3: acceptance criteria are CDR products; SWE-034) and contains at minimum: receipt inspection against renders and BOM (recorded in `docs/vv/reports/receipt-inspection-<n>.md`, section 11.2 stage 0); pre-power continuity and rail-short checks; staged first power-on (section 11.2); rail voltages and receive, transmit and sleep currents; antenna-port return loss (NanoVNA); output power into the dummy load by the section 6.2 method; receiver functional demonstration on a known signal; keyer demonstration with straight key and with paddles at two speeds (SI-018); sidetone and headphone audio level; battery charge start and termination; enclosure fit and control feel (Inspection and Demonstration); firmware hash equals the released VDD hash (Inspection, SWE-063); and the loaded code and data acceptance below. An acceptance data package `docs/vv/adp/<unit>/` holds the ATP reports, photographs, the as-built record `as-built.md` (CM plan Table 4-1 row 38) and the VDD.
- **Loaded code and data acceptance (SWE-193; nominal and off-nominal per the SWE-193 note).** Cases `TC-SW-BOOT-NNN` (`docs/test_cases/sw-boot/test_cases.json`) and `TC-SW-CFG-NNN` (`docs/test_cases/sw-cfg/test_cases.json`; modules per `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 9.7) are written by the test author by CDR. Their credit run is the Bench run at section 11.2 stage 4 on each unit, after TRR, as part of the ATP (SWE-193 acceptance tests at TRR per 07 section 3.1). Before TRR their non-USB parts (image integrity check and safe-state entry, configuration restore and default substitution) run in Emulation within the accredited scope, on the release candidate, as `credit: false` supporting evidence (row key `SUPPORT`); the USB bootloader load is target behavior outside the emulator scope. The platform-independent logic behind them (CRC and range checks, default substitution) is closed by its own `REQ-SW-CFG-*` and `REQ-SW-BOOT-*` HostUnit cases under row `T-SW-LOGIC`. The acceptance content: (a) firmware image load over the USB bootloader: a valid image is accepted, boots and reports its version and hash on the serial banner and `picotool verify` matches; a corrupt, truncated or wrong-target image is rejected and the unit reports the SWE-134 a safe state (PA disabled, TX inhibited) with a fault code; (b) persisted configuration (keyer speed, iambic mode A or B and straight-key mode, sidetone frequency and level, frequency calibration, last frequency): nominal values are restored on restart; out-of-range or corrupt values are replaced by defaults and a logged event is emitted, so that the radio always starts with a usable keyer for either key type (SI-018).
- Any ATP failure is an NCR (section 10); a friend's unit is handed over only with all ATP cases `Passed` and no open S1 or S2 NCR.

### 11.2 First-power-on procedure concept (SI-010)

The detailed procedure is the first-power-on case of module `SYS` (`docs/test_cases/sys/test_cases.json`; id allocated at authoring per 02 section 11.1, no id is reserved here) and its ATP twin, written by CDR from the power budget analysis, which supplies every current limit and expected rail value with tolerance. The concept, fixed here:

| Stage | Action | Go criterion (values from the CDR power budget) | Instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Receipt | Inspect assembly against rendered layout, BOM and polarity marks; measure PCB and enclosure critical dimensions; photograph both sides; record in `docs/vv/reports/receipt-inspection-<n>.md` (one per vendor delivery, `docs/process/01-lifecycle-and-reviews.md` section 7.3 row 9) and link it from `docs/vv/adp/<unit>/as-built.md` | No missing, rotated or wrong-value parts visible; dimensions within drawing tolerance | Loupe, calipers (OQ-VV-002), camera |
| 1 Cold checks | Power off: continuity of ground, resistance from each rail to ground, antenna port DC isolation, key and headphone jacks to their nets | No rail below the budgeted minimum resistance; antenna port isolated | Multimeter |
| 2 Passive RF | NanoVNA sweep of the antenna port with the unit unpowered and the PA path unbiased | Return loss and filter passband shape within the Analysis prediction band | NanoVNA, dummy load for calibration |
| 3 Rails only | Bench supply at nominal voltage, current limit at the budgeted no-firmware quiescent value; PA supply disconnected by its jumper or fuse | Each rail within tolerance; supply current below limit; no part warm to the touch after 60 s | Bench supply, multimeter |
| 4 Firmware load | Flash the tagged release over USB in bootloader mode; observe serial telemetry; run the `TC-SW-BOOT` and `TC-SW-CFG` acceptance cases (section 11.1); Claude writes the PCA-05 line in `as-built.md` the same day (section 5.3) | Boot banner with expected version hash; SWE-134 a safe-state report (PA disabled, TX inhibited); corrupt image rejected; configuration defaults restored | USB serial |
| 5 Receive | Headphones and antenna substitute (dummy load or short whip) connected; tune across the band | Noise floor audible, volume and tuning respond, LCD shows frequency; receive current within budget | Bench supply, multimeter |
| 6 Keying, no RF | Straight key then paddles connected; PA still disconnected | Sidetone follows the key; keyer produces correct elements at the set speed; TX request logged but PA-enable stays low (SWE-134 h prerequisite check) | Serial log |
| 7 Transmit into load, reduced supply | PA jumper in; bench supply at the reduced voltage from the budget with a current limit at the budgeted low-power draw; key-down 2 s bursts into the dummy load | Transmit current within the low-power band; RF probe (characterized by the RF probe characterization case, section 6.2) shows power in the predicted band; no rail collapse | Bench supply, multimeter, RF probe, dummy load |
| 8 Transmit into load, full supply | Nominal supply; ConOps key-down duty | Power, current and rail values within tolerance; PA and enclosure surface temperature below the stop criterion of the hazard analysis PA thermal limit (thermocouple reading if OQ-VV-003 confirms one, NTC telemetry otherwise, alignment item A11) | Same, plus the multimeter thermocouple (OQ-VV-003) |
| 9 On air | Only after section 6.3 conditions are met and the owner has signed the OnAir authorization in `docs/reviews/TRR-Dn/decision-memo.md` | Two-way contact with a second station using straight key, then paddles (`TC-VAL` scenarios) | Second station |

A discrepancy at any stage stops the procedure (section 10.1). Stage 0 is a TRR entrance product (01 section 7.3 row 9) and precedes TRR. Stages 1 and 2 are unpowered; they run for the record after TRR and may be run earlier as risk reduction (`credit: false`, recorded in the receipt inspection report) at the owner's option. Stages 3 to 8 occur only after TRR (section 13). Stage 9 occurs only after the on-air delta TRR (`TRR-Dn`, 01 section 7.6 (c)); it is a validation activity and a Demonstration credit event.

## 12. Software coverage as verification evidence (SWE-189, SWE-190, SWE-219, SWE-220)

- **Scope:** every crate compiled into the image: `cwht-core`, `cwht-app`, rustos `api` and `pico2` (07 section 17.1), and Rust `core` at feature level only (section 4, reused components).
- **Credit-bearing measure (MSR-13):** line and region coverage per crate from `cargo llvm-cov` over the nextest run of the tagged release on the locked stable toolchain (`tools/toolchain.lock.md` section 1), from the HostUnit suite. Code that is not host-buildable (the `pico2` drivers) is measured from Emulation where the emulator exports coverage; otherwise every uncovered driver path gets the SWE-190 complementary inspection below, recorded per path in the same report. Emulation coverage is reported as "not measured" when the emulator does not export it.
- **Required non-credit measure (MSR-14):** branch coverage of all image crates and condition coverage of the safety-critical components on the nightly pinned in `tools/toolchain.lock.md` (07 section 9.6 item 3), attached to every `TC-SW-COV-001` report and labelled nightly. It never closes a requirement by itself (CS-03; charter section 10).
- **Targets, in the measured units:** 100 percent line and region coverage for every image crate (the structural coverage of SWE-189 as `llvm-cov` measures it on Rust), with every uncovered line or region dispositioned into one of the four SWE-189 categories (requirement missing, test missing, extraneous or dead code, deactivated code) and acted on: a missing requirement becomes a CR, a missing test becomes a case, dead code is removed, deactivated code is listed with its activating configuration. For the safety-critical components of `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 14.1 (the single authoritative list, charter section 10): 100 percent MC/DC (SWE-219), shown by the independently reviewed independence-pair table plus MSR-13 plus MSR-14 (07 section 9.6), including a table for every `pico2` driver decision reached from those components; the independence-pair tests of a driver decision run where the driver executes (HostUnit against a register mock where the driver is host-buildable, otherwise Emulation within the accredited scope or the delivered unit), and the table names the class of each test.
- **MC/DC method:** `docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` section 9.6 records that tool-instrumented MC/DC is not available for Rust at this date, so the MC/DC table per safety-critical decision (each condition shown to independently affect the outcome by a named test), reviewed by an independent agent and included in the `TC-SW-COV-001` report, is the Rev A method, dispositioned T in RMM row SWE-219 and approved by the owner at SRR with the RMM (07 section 9.6; `docs/reviews/SRR/decision-memo.md`). A shortfall against 100 percent is relieved as 01 section 8.6 records it (owner decision at SRR): the RMM row SWE-219 stays the relief for the method, and the shortfall is a product waiver `W<n>` approved with rationale by the owner as Technical Authority in the CDR, TRR or SAR decision memo (the SWE-219 note: deviations from 100 percent are reviewed and waived with rationale by the TAs' approval; 07 section 9.6 item 4), entered in CSA item 12 and listed in VDD section 7 (05 section 2); a shortfall without a waiver fails the case. If accredited instrumentation becomes available, a CR adopts it.
- **Evidence:** the case `TC-SW-COV-001` ("Coverage and complexity of the tagged release", `docs/test_cases/sw-cov/test_cases.json`, type `HostUnit`, method Test, citing the `REQ-SW-*` that state the coverage and complexity requirements) produces one report per release, `docs/vv/reports/TC-SW-COV-001-r<N>.md` from the verification report template with `firmware_version` carrying the release, the summary table (MSR-13 and MSR-14 side by side, per crate including `api` and `pico2`), the uncovered-code disposition table and the complementary inspection records; raw `lcov.info`, the nightly output and the MC/DC tables live in the report's artifact directory `docs/vv/reports/TC-SW-COV-001-r<N>/`. (`docs/process/07-software-engineering-plan.md` sections 1.2, 1.3, 8.2 and 9.5 and the RMM row SWE-219 use the same name; this document governs evidence naming per 07's own precedence rule.)
- **Analysis of results (SWE-190):** coverage is computed from the execution of the tests that ran for the tagged release, never from an instrumented development build; the report links the gate log that produced it. Where 100 percent cannot be reached by execution, the complementary analysis or inspection of the uncovered code is recorded in the same report with the reviewer's invocation.
- **Complexity (SWE-220):** the same report lists every function with cyclomatic complexity above 15 and the owner's waiver reference, or states there are none.
- **Review points:** coverage is presented at CDR (plan and first measurement), at TRR and each `TRR-Dn` (release under test), and at SAR (delivered firmware). It is an entrance product of TRR (01 section 7.3 row 13).

## 15. Test data retention

- **What is retained:** every report and its `expected_artifacts` (logs, CSV, plots, photographs, serial captures), every NCR with its evidence, coverage raw data, LTspice checker outputs, emulator scenario logs, as-run procedures with redlines, TRR and SAR packages, calibration notes per session, instrument datasheet accuracy pages cited by reports.

Text data is preferred (`.txt`, `.csv`, `.json`, `.md`); plots as `.png` or `.svg`; photographs as JPEG at 1600 px on the long side (target 500 KB or less); video is not stored in the repository: it is kept in the owner's storage and its SHA-256 and location are recorded in the report.
- **Naming:** retained logs use the suffix `.log.txt` (a plain naming convention that every viewer opens as text); serial captures are `serial-<stage>.txt`. `.gitignore` ignores `*.log` and `*.raw` elsewhere but un-ignores them under `docs/vv/` (lines `!docs/vv/**/*.log` and `!docs/vv/**/*.raw`), so no evidence file is dropped by git. An LTspice `.raw` waveform is retained only when a report cites it, in the report's artifact directory with its SHA-256; a cited waveform larger than 10 MB is not committed, and the checker exports the cited traces to `.csv` instead. Uncited `.raw` files are not retained; the checker's CSV or text summary is.
- **Integrity:** the report and the NCR list every artifact file they cite with its SHA-256 in the `artifacts` front matter list (`"<path> sha256=<hex>"`); `tools/traceability.py` verifies that each file exists with that hash (rule 7.3.8, `REPORT_ARTIFACT` and `NCR_ARTIFACT`, implemented; section 7.4).
- **How long:** for the life of the repository. The as-built baseline tag `baseline/sar` includes all evidence for Unit 1; each friend's unit ADP is committed before hand-over. Nothing in `docs/vv/` is deleted; superseded reports keep their run number and are referenced by the newer run.
- **Software records** (NPR 7150.2D chapter 6 and SWE-065 a to d): test plan (`docs/vv/plan.md`), procedures (`docs/test_cases/`), tests (`firmware/` test code and `firmware/emu/` scenarios), reports (`docs/vv/reports/`), and the non-conformance records (`docs/vv/ncr/`) are the software test records set.
