## 2. Governing text and terms

- NPR 7150.2D App. D defines Classes A to F; App. C Table 2 marks with X which SWE requirements each class invokes; SWE-139 requires compliance with every X for the project's class; SWE-020 requires classifying each system and subsystem at the highest applicable class; SWE-021 requires updating plans when the classification changes; SWE-125 and SWE-176 require maintaining and retaining the RMM (one or several matrices, SWE-125) and the classification records.
- Tailoring (NPR 7150.2D section 2.2): relief from an X requirement, approved by the Authority named in Table 2. The Table 2 "Center" authority is the Center Director or the Center Director's designated ETA, the designated SMA TA and the CHMO-designated Health and Medical TA (App. C.3); the Center CIO or designee has joint responsibility with the ETA on the section 3.11 requirements (App. C.3); HQ authorities hold SWE-032 and SWE-141. A request for relief carries the rationale, a risk evaluation and reference to supporting material (NPR 7150.2D section 2.2.5 Note), and the authority judges whether the project's risks, mitigations and requests for relief are acceptable (SWE-126 a.(4)); the RMM row fields `tailoring_rationale` and `residual_risk` hold these (section 6.1). Section 2.2.1 also states that the responsible manager formally accepts the tailoring risk and that, for tailoring involving human safety risk, the actual risk taker(s) need to formally agree to assume the risk; on cwht the owner is both, and the SWE-022, SWE-023 and SWE-219 tailoring is accepted in that capacity (section 6.4 item 3, section 9). Approved tailoring is then reflected in the plans that control the software (SWE-121; mirroring procedure in section 6.4 item 4). SWE-150 (engineering, CIO and SMA authorities agree on the tailoring) is met by the owner holding all three capacities and signing in each (section 9). Customization (form, consolidation, formality) needs no relief and is recorded in the SEMP (charter section 1).
- Safety-critical determination (SWE-205) is made against the criteria of NASA-STD-8739.8. The standard itself is not in the reference corpus, but its definition of safety-critical software is: `docs/references/md/swehb/7-02-classification-and-safety-criticality.md` section 1.2 reproduces NASA-STD-8739.8B para 3.2. Software is safety-critical if it is determined by and traceable to a hazard analysis and meets at least one of: (a) causes or contributes to a system hazardous condition or event; (b) controls functions identified in a system hazard; (c) provides mitigation for a system hazardous condition or event; (d) mitigates damage if a hazardous condition or event occurs; (e) detects, reports and takes corrective action if the system reaches a potentially hazardous state. The same topic states that the determination includes verification of safety-critical software (applied in section 4.3.1) and that all safety-critical software is Class D or higher, as App. D Class E item a.3 also does. This record applies criteria a to e (section 4). The SWEHB scrape in `docs/references/md/swehb/` also holds, in full, the minimum content of the software assurance plan (topic 5.17) and of the safety plan (topic 5.18), and holds the topic pages of the design-for-safety checklist (6.1, PAT-006) and the general software safety requirements checklist (6.2, PAT-007) as pointers to downloadable files whose content is not in the corpus. What remains unavailable is the standard's enumerated requirement and tasking set; SWE-022 and SWE-023 are therefore tailored (section 6.3) and are re-dispositioned by `CR-NNN` when the owner obtains the standard (section 6.4 item 8).

## 4. Safety-critical determination (SWE-205)

### 4.1 Method

1. Hazards of the system as operated are identified and carried in `docs/safety/hazards.json` (`HZ-NNN`, charter section 6) by the hazard analysis (`docs/safety/hazard-analysis.md`; sources: ConOps scenarios `OPS-NNN`, every SI-NNN in `docs/requirements/l0-stakeholder/stakeholder-inputs.md`, the research reports, the risk register).
2. For each hazard, `hazards.json` states the firmware role in the terms of NASA-STD-8739.8B para 3.2 (`firmware_role.criteria`: a causes or contributes, b controls a function identified in the hazard, c provides mitigation, d mitigates damage, e detects, reports and takes corrective action, or none), the flag `firmware_role.safety_critical`, the components involved (`firmware_role.components`) and the SWE-134 items that apply (`swe134_items`).
3. A firmware component is safety-critical when (i) it is named in `firmware_role.components` of a hazard carried in `hazards.json` whose `firmware_role.criteria` is non-empty, or (ii) this record, applying criteria a to e to a hazard carried in `hazards.json`, finds that the component meets at least one criterion for it although `hazards.json` does not yet name it. A type (ii) finding states the hazard, the criterion and the reason in section 4.3, stays traceable to the hazard through this record, and is listed in section 6.5 for the hazard analysis author, who adds the component to `firmware_role.components` in the next `hazards.json` change. Components that meet none of the criteria for any hazard are not safety-critical. The SWE-134 note (components that control, mitigate or contribute to a hazard, and software used to command hazardous operations) is applied alongside to fix where SWE-134 a to l apply; on cwht it yields the same set.
4. Separately, identify mission-critical components using the NPR 7150.2D App. A definition of Mission Critical Software: "Software that can cause, contribute to, or mitigate the loss of capabilities that are essential to the primary mission objectives." The primary mission objective is making a CW contact within the 2 m amateur allocation, 144.000 to 148.000 MHz (SI-024), with either a straight key or an iambic paddle (SI-018, a core requirement). A component is mission-critical when its failure can cause or contribute to losing that capability (not only when it defeats it outright) or when it mitigates such a loss. SWE-134 a to l apply to mission-critical software as well as safety-critical software; SWE-219 and SWE-220 apply to safety-critical components only.
5. `hazards.json` is the source of record; sections 4.2 and 4.3 are hand-transcribed from it by the author of this record and cite its version. No generator exists. The author re-transcribes them whenever a hazard's `firmware_role` or `swe134_items` changes (section 6.4 item 9), and the determination is re-run at PDR (architecture known) and CDR (design known), as `hazard-analysis.md` section 1 also states.

Determination made by the owner as SMA TA with Claude as lead SE; independent concurrence per section 3.3.

### 6.4 Maintenance

1. **Trigger.** The RMM is updated for every life-cycle review (SRR, PDR, CDR, TRR, SAR), for every `CR-NNN` that changes a plan or process artifact named in an implementation field, and whenever a row's status changes from Planned to In place. It is an entrance product of every review (charter section 3).
2. **Procedure.** Claude edits `docs/process/rmm.json` only (never `rmm.md`), runs the four validation commands of section 6.1, and attaches the rendered `rmm.md` and the `--check` output (counts and id lists) to `docs/reviews/<REVIEW>/package.md`. Any change of disposition (FC to T, T to NA, or the reverse) is listed in the package under "proposed tailoring" with the rationale text.
3. **Approval and signature.** The owner's approval is recorded in `docs/reviews/<REVIEW>/decision-memo.md`: section 7 "Tailoring approved" of `docs/templates/decision-memo.md` carries one row per T and NA SWE id with the approved commit hash, and section 7.1 "RMM approval" carries every line of the section 9 signature block (approved hash, T, NA and lien rows, the ETA and SMA TA line, the CIO/SAISO designee line, the HMTA line, the risk-taker line and the classification concurrence line). The independent reviewer confirms with `git diff <approved hash> -- docs/process/rmm.json` that only `meta.approval` differs. The baseline record (05 section 4.4 step 2) then lists the hash of this `rmm.json` version, and `baseline/srr` is placed on the baseline-record commit per 05 section 4.4 steps 3 and 4. Never editorial: any change to a "shall" statement, a number, a unit, an ID, a status field, a schema, source code (other than comments and `rustfmt` whitespace), a netlist, a footprint, a BOM line, a CAD dimension." Before SRR every change is Log class with a `Refs:` trailer.
