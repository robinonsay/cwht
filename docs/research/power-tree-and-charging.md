# Power tree, 2S charging and protection for cwht (assignment: power-tree)

**Status:** research report for PDR trade studies. **Date:** 2026-09-25. **Author:** Claude (research subagent). **Owner:** Robin.

Extends, does not repeat: `2m-cw-transceiver-reference-designs.md` (PA line-up, F24/F25 and RISK 8 "battery voltage droop"), `pcbway-fabrication-and-assembly.md` (F15 THT soldering, F16 Pico 2 as a reflowed module, REQ PCB-ASM-08 single-side placement), `keyer-and-key-interfaces.md` (RP2350 pad ratings and erratum E9). Stakeholder inputs used: SI-003 (5 W), SI-005 (headphones), SI-006 (volume control), SI-007 (Pico 2), SI-009 (turnkey assembly), SI-010 (works at first power-on), SI-013 (bench gear), SI-022 (micro-USB for firmware and charging), SI-023 (2 x 18650 in a holder).

## 1. Question

Design-research the cwht power subsystem: charging two series 18650 cells (SI-023) from the Pico 2 module's micro-USB 5 V VBUS (SI-022), stating the USB 2.0 current budget and the charge rate it allows; candidate boost or buck-boost 2S charger ICs (TI BQ25887, BQ25792, MPS MP2672, MP2639B, Analog LTC4162 family, others) with lifecycle, dated stock and price, programming interface, thermal/NTC handling, charge inhibit and status outputs; 2S protection (balancing, over-current, under-voltage; protection ICs with FETs, or protected cells plus a PCM) and 18650 holders that PCBWay can place or the owner can screw in; cell voltage and temperature sensing for the RP2350 ADC (Pico 2 ADC reference and input range); power rails (PA rail from the pack and the ALC implication, the Pico 2 VSYS feed and its on-board regulator, a clean 3.3 V analog rail, an audio rail); power-path behaviour (operate while charging, inhibit charging during transmit); quiescent and receive current estimates; a battery-life model for a 2S 3000 mAh pack at 1:9 and 1:4 TX:RX; safety provisions for the hazard analysis. Recommend a baseline topology and list the owner decisions.

## 3. Findings

### A. The input: USB budget through the Pico 2 micro-USB

**F1. USB 2.0 budget is 100 mA unconfigured, 500 mA configured; BC1.2 charging ports give 1.5 A.** "The current limits are 2.5 mA when in suspended state, 100 mA when connected, and 500/900 mA when connected and configured for higher power in accordance with USB 2.0/3.0 specifications." BC1.2 defines SDP (standard host port, 500 mA), CDP and DCP, the latter two supplying "a current of 1.5 A"; a DCP "incorporates a short or a resistor up to 200 Ω between data lines" and cannot enumerate a device. Source: https://emlogic.no/2023/11/understanding-usb-battery-charging-1-2/ . Microchip's developer page states the same unit-load rules (100 mA at connection, 500 mA once configured, 1.5 A for DCP/CDP): https://developerhelp.microchip.com/xwiki/bin/view/applications/usb/power-delivery/battery-charging/ (search snippet; page fetch timed out).

**F2. The micro-B connector's power contacts are rated about 1.8 A; the Pico 2 publishes no rating for its own connector or VBUS trace.** Amphenol LTW: Micro USB 2.0 "Up to ~1.8 A" charging current, about 10,000 insertion cycles ( https://amphenolltw.com/news-events/different-types-of-micro-USB-ports.html ); the Hirose ZX-series note gives 1.8 A on power pins 1 and 5 and 1.0 A on signal pins (search snippet, https://www.mouser.com/pdfdocs/HiroseZXSeriesConnectors.PDF , not fetched). Pico 2 datasheet: "VBUS is the micro-USB input voltage, connected to micro-USB port pin 1. This is nominally 5 V", specification "VBUS 5 V ± 10%", and "VBUS is the 5 V input from the micro-USB port, which is fed through a Schottky diode to generate VSYS" (D1). The datasheet gives no current rating for the connector, D1 or the VBUS copper to castellated pin 40. Source: https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/pico/pico-2-datasheet.pdf (sections 2 pin descriptions, 3 mechanical/electrical table, 5.4 Powerchain). Consequence: under SI-022 every milliamp of charge power passes through the Pico 2 module's connector, D1 is bypassed for charging (the charger takes VBUS from pin 40, not VSYS), but the module's VBUS copper is an unrated series element. Confidence on a > 500 mA budget through the module: Low until measured.

**F3. Charge rate that each budget allows (derived).** A boost charger from 5 V to a 2S pack at about 8.0 V average during constant current, efficiency 0.90 (TI quotes 93.4 % for BQ25887 at 5 V in, 7.6 V battery, 1 A; https://www.ti.com/product/BQ25887 ):

| Input budget | Input power | Charge current | C-rate for 3000 mAh | Time to about 85 % (CC) | Total with 1 h CV tail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| USB 2.0 configured, 500 mA | 2.5 W | about 280 mA | 0.09 C | about 9 h | about 10 h |
| BC1.2 DCP or CDP, 1.5 A | 7.5 W | about 840 mA | 0.28 C | about 3 h | about 4 h |
| Micro-B contact limit, 1.8 A | 9.0 W | about 1.0 A | 0.34 C | about 2.5 h | about 3.5 h |

Samsung INR18650-30Q allows a 1.5 A standard charge (4.0 A max) with a 100 mA cut-off at 4.2 V ± 0.05 V, so none of these rates stresses the cell; the 500 mA USB case is a slow overnight charge (Samsung SDI INR18650-30Q specification, Blue Robotics mirror https://bluerobotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/INR18650-30Q-Data-Sheet.pdf ).

**F4. Nobody on the board can run BC1.2 detection without a design decision.** BQ25887 has no D+/D- pins; its input current limit defaults from the PSEL pin: "PSEL HIGH sets the input current limit to 500 mA and PSEL LOW sets the input current limit to 3 A" with the Input Current Optimizer enabled only for PSEL LOW (BQ25887 datasheet section 8.3.2.2.1 and Table in 8.3.3). BQ25792 does have D+/D- detection ("Detects USB BC1.2, SDP, CDP, DCP, HVDCP and non-standard adapters"), but on the Pico 2 the USB data pair runs from the connector through 27 Ω resistors to the RP2350 and is exposed only on the module's underside test points (pcbway report F16); wiring it out to a second detector is a signal-integrity risk. The RP2350 offers a firmware route: "USB DP/DM pins can be used as GPIO" via `USBPHY_DIRECT` bit `USBPHY_AS_GPIO` (RP2350 datasheet, GPIO differences list and USB register map), so firmware could drive D+ and read D- before enumerating to detect a DCP short (< 200 Ω per F1). This is a plausible but unverified method (Low confidence). The safe default is 500 mA (PSEL high) and 1.5 A only after a positive DCP test or an explicit owner menu setting.

### C. 2S pack protection

**F11. A charger IC is not a pack protector; the PA path bypasses it.** BQ25887 protects the charge path (VREG accuracy, battery OVP, JEITA, 12 h timer, thermal) but has no element in the discharge path (F5, "Power path: No"); MP2672A's battery FET is in the SYS path but its protections are charger-side (F6). Discharge under-voltage, discharge over-current, load short-circuit and a second, independent over-voltage detection therefore need a pack-protection function. Options with data:
- **ABLIC S-8252 series, 2-cell protection IC with external N-channel FETs.** Per-cell overcharge detection 3.550 to 4.600 V (5 mV steps, ±20 mV at 25 °C), overdischarge 2.000 to 3.000 V (10 mV steps, ±50 mV), discharge overcurrent 0.050 to 0.400 V (±10 mV, sensed across the FETs at the VM pin), load short 0.500 to 0.900 V, charge overcurrent -0.400 to -0.050 V, internal delay timers (no external capacitors), VM and CO pins rated 28 V absolute maximum, current 8.0 µA max operating, 0.1 µA in power-down, SOT-23-6 or SNT-6A. Variant S-8252AAO-M6T1U: overcharge 4.250 V detect / 4.100 V release, overdischarge 2.500 V detect / 3.000 V release, discharge overcurrent 0.200 V, short 0.500 V, charge overcurrent -0.100 V, 0 V charging inhibited, power-down available. Datasheet Rev 4.0: https://www.ablic.com/en/doc/datasheet/battery_protection/S8252_E.pdf . Stock (oemstrade, 2026-09-25 about 11:52 CDT): Mouser 3,390 at $1.66 (qty 1), DigiKey 3,000 (tape and reel, $1.2335 at 3,000): https://www.oemstrade.com/search/S-8252AAO-M6T1U .
- **ABLIC S-82C2B/C, newer 2-cell protector with an external sense resistor:** discharge overcurrent 3 to 400 mV in 1 mV steps at ±3 mV, charge overcurrent -400 to -3 mV, 3.0 µA typ / 6.0 µA max, power-down 50 nA, CTL pin for external charge/discharge control (S-82C2B) or PS pin power saving (S-82C2C), SNT-8A / HSNT-8. Datasheet Rev 1.1: https://www.ablic.com/en/doc/datasheet/battery_protection/S82C2B_C_E.pdf ; family page https://www.ablic.com/en/semicon/datasheets/power-management-ic/lithium-ion-battery-protection-ic/s-82b2a-b/ ("MOSFET Configuration: External", "Cell Balancing: Not included"). Stock not checked.
- **TI BQ29209, 2-series secondary over-voltage protector with 15 mA balancing:** fixed 4.30 V or 4.35 V thresholds, ±25 mV from 0 to 60 °C, balancing starts at a 30 mV cell difference, ICC < 3 µA, VSON-8 (DRB); OUT pin drives an external FET or a fuse blow circuit. Datasheet Rev C 2016 (local extraction) and product page "ACTIVE": https://www.ti.com/product/BQ29209 . Stock (oemstrade, 2026-09-25 about 11:50 CDT): DigiKey 3,954 at $1.14, Mouser 6,434 at $1.07: https://www.oemstrade.com/search/BQ29209DRBR .
- **No 2S protector with integrated FETs was found.** ABLIC's integrated-FET parts (S-821A/B family) are 1-cell devices ( https://www.ablic.com/en/semicon/products/power-management-ic/lithium-ion-battery-protection-ic/s-821a-821b/ ). Two external N-channel FETs in the pack negative lead (common-drain pair, as the S-8252 application circuit shows) are the standard 2S arrangement; a dual-FET package with RDS(on) around 10 to 20 mΩ keeps the drop at 1.3 A below 30 mV and lets the S-8252 0.200 V threshold correspond to about 5 to 10 A (derived; FET selection is an ACTION).
- **Protected cells instead of on-board protection:** protected 18650s carry a 1S PCM and measure about 68 to 70 mm versus 64.85 ± 0.15 mm for the INR18650-30Q (Samsung specification; length statements from https://www.keeppower.com/blog/protected-vs-unprotected-18650-battery/ and https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=287624 ). They protect each cell individually but do not balance, and they do not fit holders sized for 65 mm cells. Because SI-023 makes cells user-replaceable and SI-019 hands radios to friends, the design cannot assume protected cells; on-board protection is mandatory and protected cells become an optional extra.

**F12. Reverse insertion and mixed cells.** Holders do not prevent reversed insertion (F13). BQ25887 asks for 300 Ω in series with MID "For protection of bottom cell reverse plug in" (F5); the S-8252 datasheet notes that with a charger connected in reverse, current flows into the IC through the VM path and asks for as large a series resistance as possible (S-8252 section on external components, item on reverse charger connection). With a 4-terminal holder arrangement (F13) the firmware can read each cell through its own divider before enabling any rail; the protection IC's FETs stay off unless both cells are within window, which also blocks the reversed-cell case (one cell at -4.2 V gives a pack voltage near 0 V and a mid-tap out of range). The Pico 2 datasheet's own caution applies: "Bare, unprotected cells are dangerous and can catch fire or explode if over-discharged, over-charged or charged / discharged outside their allowed temperature and/or current range."

### E. Sensing with the RP2350 ADC and the charger ADC

**F14. RP2350 ADC and the Pico 2 reference.** RP2350 datasheet Table 1438: input range 0 to ADC_AVDD, ENOB 9 min / 9.5 typ bits, 12 resolved bits, input impedance ≥ 100 kΩ; 500 kS/s SAR with about 1 pF sampling capacitance ("DC measurements have no need to buffer"); the maximum input is bounded by IOVDD, not ADC_AVDD; four external channels on GPIO26 to 29 in the QFN-60 used on Pico 2, channel 4 the temperature sensor (Vbe 0.706 V at 27 °C, -1.721 mV/°C, about 40 µA extra, "very sensitive to errors in reference voltage": 1 % reference error is over 4 °C). Source: https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2350/rp2350-datasheet.pdf (sections 12.4.3, 12.4.6, 14.9, Table 1438). Pico 2: ADC_VREF is the 3.3 V SMPS output through 201 Ω into 2.2 µF with about 150 µA ADC draw giving "an inherent offset of about 150 μA × 200 = ~30 mV" that varies with sampling and temperature; remedies offered are forcing the SMPS into PWM mode (GPIO23 high), measuring a grounded channel as a zero reference, or an external 3.0 V shunt reference (LM4040) that limits the range to 0 to 3.0 V and draws about 1.5 mA through the 200 Ω; GPIO29/ADC3 already measures VSYS/3 and GPIO24 senses VBUS. Source: Pico 2 datasheet sections 2, 5.3, 5.4. Derived divider design at 3.3 V full scale (LSB 0.806 mV, ENOB step 4.56 mV): pack through 200 kΩ / 100 kΩ (k = 0.333) reads 2.80 V at 8.4 V, 2.42 mV of pack per LSB, 13.7 mV effective step, 28 µA at 8.4 V, 67 kΩ Thevenin; mid-tap through 47 kΩ / 100 kΩ (k = 0.680) reads 2.96 V at 4.35 V, 1.18 mV per LSB, 6.7 mV effective step, 32 kΩ Thevenin. The 30 mV reference offset is a 0.9 % gain error, about 76 mV at the pack, so the RP2350 ADC alone cannot set a 4.2 V ± 0.05 V charge or a 3.0 V/cell cut-off without calibration.

**F15. The charger's 16-bit ADC is the precision path; the RP2350 ADC is the independent check.** BQ25887 reports VBAT, VCELLTOP/VCELLBOT (MID), ICHG, IBUS, VBUS, TS and die temperature over I2C (F5); BQ25792 likewise (F7); MP2672A has none (F6). For the hazard analysis this gives two dissimilar measurements of each cell (charger ADC and RP2350 divider), which is what a Class A single-fault argument wants: firmware compares them and refuses to charge or transmit on disagreement beyond a threshold (about 100 mV, derived from the 76 mV gain error above; the ACTION is to calibrate the RP2350 path against the charger ADC at each USB connection).

**F16. Temperature sensing: NTC and placement.** BQ25887 and BQ25792 both recommend the Semitec 103AT-2 (10 kΩ ± 1 %, B 3435 K, 3.8 x 2.4 x 8.5 mm radial), Mouser 137,636 in stock at $0.58 (oemstrade, 2026-09-25 about 11:53 CDT, https://www.oemstrade.com/search/103AT-2 ). The JEITA windows in F5/F6 only work if the thermistor senses the cell, and neither Keystone holder has an NTC pocket; a radial 103AT-2 bent against the cell body inside the holder, or an SMD 10 kΩ NTC on the PCB under the cell with thermal contact through the holder floor, are the two placements (thermal coupling unverified: Low). A second NTC or the RP2350 on-die sensor covers the PA heatsink for the transmit duty limit (reference report DECISION 16 thermal path).

### F. Rails

**F17. PA rail from the pack: 6.0 to 8.4 V, and the ALC implication.** The reference-designs report derived Pout ∝ VDD², about 3.3 W at 6.0 V and 6.4 W at 8.4 V for a 5 W at 7.4 V design, and about 1.1 A at 7.4 V for 5 W at 60 % efficiency (its F25). Three responses: (a) accept droop and write the requirement as "5 W at 7.4 V, ≥ 3 W at 6.0 V"; (b) a regulated PA rail (boost or buck-boost to a fixed 7.2 V) which adds a switcher and its harmonics next to the receiver; (c) closed-loop ALC through the envelope-shaping node the keyer already needs (reference report DECISION 18: drain-supply modulator, driver supply or gate bias), with the RP2350 reading pack voltage and a forward-power detector and clamping the drive so Pout stays at the set value from about 7.2 V upward and follows V² below. Option (c) needs no extra converter and also caps the 6.4 W excursion at full charge, which otherwise raises harmonic levels toward the 25 µW limit (part97 report F2). Recommendation: (c) with (a) as the written requirement floor.

**F18. Pico 2 feed: VSYS 1.8 to 5.5 V through the datasheet's P-FET OR-ing; 3V3 pin load below 300 mA.** Pico 2 datasheet: VSYS "can vary in the allowed range 1.8 V to 5.5 V"; the RT6150 buck-boost "generates a fixed 3.3 V output for the RP2350 device and its IO (and can be used to power external circuitry)" but "it is recommended to keep the load on this pin less than 300 mA"; 3V3_EN is pulled to VSYS by 100 kΩ and shorting it low de-powers the RP2350; GPIO23 selects PFM (default) or PWM (lower ripple, worse efficiency); a second source should feed VSYS through a Schottky or, better, a P-FET whose gate is driven by VBUS so the external source disconnects when USB is present (Figure 9; example Diodes DMG2305UX, Vt ≤ 0.9 V, 100 mΩ at 2.5 V) with an optional Schottky across the FET for the hand-over; Figure 10 shows the same arrangement fed from a charger's system output. Source: Pico 2 datasheet sections 2, 5.4, 5.5, 5.6. Pico 2 module current from the datasheet tables: hello_usb 12.74 mA at 5 V (63.7 mW), CoreMark single core 9.38 mA, USB boot idle 5.47 mA, peak during boot 14.26 mA; RP2350 alone at 150 MHz single core 11.0 mA on VREG_VIN plus 0.45 mA IOVDD (Table 1446); peripherals add 1 to 38 µA/MHz each and USBCTRL 1.25 mA (Tables 1443 to 1444). Budget 40 mA at 5 V for the module with both cores, USB and PIO active (derived).

**F19. System 5 V bus: a low-noise synchronous buck is the efficient choice, and stock is the weak point.** TI TPS62913: 3.0 to 17 V input, 0.8 to 5.5 V output, 3 A, 2.2 MHz or 1 MHz fixed frequency with external clock synchronization, optional spread spectrum, "<20 µVRMS (100 Hz to 100 kHz)" noise and "<10 µVRMS after ferrite bead" ripple, quiescent shown as 0.005 A typical on the product page (a real cost of about 5 mA in receive; Medium confidence on that figure), VQFN-HR 2 x 2 mm, lifecycle Active, datasheet Rev B 2021-03-23. Source: https://www.ti.com/product/TPS62913 . Stock (oemstrade, 2026-09-25 about 11:52 CDT): DigiKey 0 at $2.76, Mouser 0 at $2.76, LCSC 7,153: https://www.oemstrade.com/search/TPS62913RPUR . The SYNC input matters for a CW receiver: harmonics of a 2.2 MHz free-running switcher fall every 2.2 MHz across 144 to 148 MHz (the 66th to 67th harmonics, derived), and syncing the converter to an RP2350 PWM output lets firmware move the birdie away from the tuned frequency, while spread spectrum would smear it into a noise-floor rise. An LDO-only chain (pack to 5 V LDO) avoids the switcher but burns (7.4 V - 5 V) x 0.25 A = 0.6 W in receive, about 30 % of receive power (derived).

**F20. 3.3 V analog rail: LDO from the 5 V bus.** TI TPS7A20: 1.6 to 6.0 V input, 300 mA, 7 µVRMS noise, PSRR 95 dB at 1 kHz and 60 dB at 100 kHz, 140 mV dropout at 300 mA (3.3 V), 6.5 µA quiescent, SOT-23-5 and smaller packages, fixed outputs 0.8 to 5.5 V, Active, datasheet Rev H 2024-07-02. Source: https://www.ti.com/product/TPS7A20 . Stock for TPS7A2033PDBVR (oemstrade, 2026-09-25 about 11:52 CDT): DigiKey 0 at $0.35, Mouser 0 at $0.37, TME 311, LCSC 229,875: https://www.oemstrade.com/search/TPS7A2033PDBVR . 60 dB PSRR at 100 kHz falls to roughly 20 to 30 dB by 2.2 MHz (typical LDO roll-off; derived, Low), so the switcher's fundamental still needs an LC or ferrite-bead stage before the LDO, which the TPS62913 datasheet's "after ferrite bead" figure presumes. ### G. Power-path behaviour

**F22. With BQ25887 the radio always runs from the pack; USB alone runs only the Pico 2.** Because BQ25887 has no power path (F5), with USB attached and no cells inserted the charger's BAT node has no battery ("If no battery is connected, the STAT pin blinks"), the 5 V buck has no input, and only the Pico 2 is alive through its own D1 from VBUS: firmware loading with cells removed works and the transmitter cannot be energised, which is a useful safety property for SI-022. Receiving while charging is electrically fine (the pack supplies the load, the charger supplies the pack) but the charger sees battery current = charge current - load current, so termination at 150 mA is disturbed by a 200 mA receive load: either inhibit charging while the receiver is on, or accept that "charge complete" is reported only when the radio is off or idle. Transmitting while charging is undesirable for three reasons: the 1.5 MHz boost ripple on the pack appears on the PA drain and produces AM sidebands at ±1.5 MHz that count as spurious emissions; the input budget (2.5 W) cannot carry transmit power in any case; and the charger's DPM loops respond to the 1.3 A key-down steps. Hardware interlock: the TX enable line pulls the CD pin high (converter off, I2C and ADC still alive) through a diode-OR with the firmware charge-enable GPIO, and firmware re-enables after a hang time. With MP2672A (F6) the NVDC SYS output would run the radio from USB with no cells present, but the PA current would pass the internal battery FET (ISYS ≤ 2 A absolute maximum) whose resistance was not captured; that path is not recommended for the 1.3 A key-down pulses (Low confidence on the FET data, so treat as a RISK if MP2672A is chosen).

### H. Current budgets and battery life

**F23. Receive and transmit current budgets and the battery-life model (derived).** Inputs: Pico 2 module 40 mA at 5 V (F18); LO 30 mA (Si5351 class) or 39 mA (LMX2571, reference report F20) or 120 to 170 mA (ADF4351, F18 there); LNA 30 mA (low-current MMIC) or 60 mA (PGA-103+ at 3 V) or 97 mA at 5 V (reference report F35); mixer, IF and audio chain 40 mA; LCD 5 mA plus backlight 0, 20 or 40 mA; headphone amplifier 4 mA plus 10 mA drive; 5 V buck quiescent 5 mA (F19); charger and protector quiescent 12 + 8 µA (F5, F11); TX period: PA quiescent 250 mA (RD07MUS2B IDQ, reference report F24) for the whole TX period plus 1.2 to 1.5 A key-down at 45 % key-down duty (English text at about 20 WPM, derived). Pack current from the 5 V bus assumes 0.90 buck efficiency at 7.2 V nominal. Capacity: 3000 mAh nominal (INR18650-30Q at 0.2 C to 2.5 V; 3040 mAh typical), 90 % usable to a 3.0 V/cell firmware cut-off (Low confidence; the 30Q curve was not digitised).

V_NOM=7.2; CAP=3.0; USABLE=0.90; EFF=0.90; KEY=0.45
def pack(i5): return i5*5.0/(V_NOM*EFF)
for name,(i5,ibias,ipa) in {"low":(0.180,0.150,1.20),"expected":(0.260,0.250,1.30),"high":(0.420,0.300,1.50)}.items():
    irx=pack(i5); itx=irx+ibias+KEY*ipa; ah=CAP*USABLE
    print(name, round(irx,3), round(itx,3), round(ah/(0.1*itx+0.9*irx),1), round(ah/(0.2*itx+0.8*irx),1), round(ah/irx,1))
for i_in in (0.5,1.5,1.8):
    ichg=5.0*i_in*0.90/8.0; print(i_in, round(ichg*1000), round(0.85*3.0/ichg,1))

 Sensing: BQ25887 ADC over I2C (VBAT, VCELLBOT, ICHG, TS); RP2350 ADC0 pack/3, ADC1 mid-tap x 0.68, ADC3 VSYS/3, GPIO24 VBUS
 Interlocks: TX_EN -> CD (charge off during TX); firmware charge-enable; rails enabled only after cell checks
