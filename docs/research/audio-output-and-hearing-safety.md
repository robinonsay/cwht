# Headphone audio chain and hearing safety for cwht (assignment: audio)

**Status:** research report for PDR trade studies and the preliminary hazard analysis. **Date:** 2026-09-25. **Author:** Claude (research subagent). **Owner:** Robin.

Stakeholder inputs carried into this report: SI-005 (operate with headphones and a key plugged in), SI-006 (volume is one of the three controls), SI-018 (straight key and iambic paddle, built-in keyer, so sidetone is mandatory), SI-019 (radios handed to friends, so third-party hearing exposure matters), SI-026 (application logic runs on a host through the rustos api traits, so audio processing belongs in testable firmware), SI-031 (PCBWay places all SMT; owner hand-solders only through-hole and simple pads), SI-033 (new peripheral drivers go upstream in rustos), SI-034 (8 h battery life at 1:9 TX:RX). Related reports: `keyer-and-key-interfaces.md` (F8 sidetone norms, SW-KEY-07, CTL-KEY-06 cross-plugging, D4, D11), `rustos-toolchain-proof.md` (PWM slice and ADC allocation, RP2350-E9 pull-up rule, WP-05), `2m-cw-transceiver-reference-designs.md` (D13 receiver architecture, which decides whether receive audio is born analog or digital), `part97-regulatory-basis.md` (RF-03: sidetone is audio-only, never on the carrier).

## Question

What should the cwht headphone audio chain be, with numbers: (1) the audio source (RP2350 PWM with RC or active reconstruction filter, an I2S DAC such as PCM5102A or UDA1334A driven by PIO, or a fully analog product-detector chain with the sidetone mixed in); (2) headphone amplifiers for 3.3 V or 5 V rails (TPA6132A2, LM4880, PAM8908, NJM4556A) with output power into 32 ohm and quiescent current; (3) mute and click suppression at T/R transitions; (4) sidetone injection and level control; (5) AGC in the audio domain; (6) hearing-safety norms (EN 50332, IEC 62368-1, WHO/ITU H.870, NIOSH, OSHA, ITU-T P.360) and typical earbud sensitivities, to derive a maximum-output requirement and a hazard control; (7) jack detection and behaviour with no headphones. Recommend a baseline and list the owner decisions (speaker or headphones only; maximum level policy).

## Findings

### A. Audio source options

**F1. The RP2350 has no DAC; PWM is the zero-cost source, and its numbers are adequate for CW.** RP2350 datasheet (build 2025-02-20): twelve PWM slices, each with a "16-bit counter", an "8.4 fractional clock divider", "Two independent output channels, duty cycle from 0% to 100% inclusive", "Wrap and level registers are double buffered and can be changed race-free while PWM is running", and "Interrupt request and DMA request on counter wrap" (section 12.5). Only eight slices reach the Pico 2's pins: the four extra slices are "available on GPIOs 32 through 47 in the QFN-80 package" (12.5.1.1). System clock "up to 150 MHz" (section 8). The only "DAC" in the document is the resistor DAC of the HSTX DPI/VGA example. Derived at 150 MHz: TOP = 1023 gives a 146.48 kHz carrier with 10-bit resolution; TOP = 2047 gives 73.24 kHz at 11 bits. Ideal quantization SNR is 62.0 dB (10-bit) or 68.0 dB (11-bit) over the full Nyquist band; confined to a 3 kHz audio band the in-band figure is about 76 dB and 79 dB respectively under a white-noise assumption (derived; a pure tone at low level produces tonal quantization products instead, so first-order dither or error-feedback noise shaping in firmware is the standard fix). A full-scale 3.3 V PWM sine is 1.167 Vrms (derived), far above any headphone level, so a resistive attenuator is part of the reconstruction network and sets the hardware ceiling (F21, F27). Feeding the slice's CC register from DMA on every wrap (146.5 kS/s, each 48.83 kS/s sample repeated three times, or true 3x upsampling) costs no CPU time per sample. Third-party practice: Adafruit's RP2350 PWM audio guide drives a PAM8302 speaker amp straight from the PWM pin and notes "there's not much point in using high bit rates" and that "updating a display, or having intense flash activity ... can result in distorted sounds or noise during playback" (a CircuitPython scheduling effect that DMA feeding avoids, but a reminder that PWM audio shares ground and supply with the digital section); Arduino-Pico documents that PWM gets "about 11 bits". Sources: local RP2350 datasheet; https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-feather-rp2350/pwm-audio-2 ; https://arduino-pico.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pwm.html .

**F2. I2S over PIO is proven on RP2040/RP2350 and has a Rust path, but SI-033 makes it a rustos work package.** pico-extras `audio_i2s.pio` transmits 16 bits per sample with BCLK and LRCLK on side-set pins, "Data is output at 1 bit per clock", and "Fractional divider will probably be needed to get correct bit clock period" (https://github.com/raspberrypi/pico-extras/blob/master/src/rp2_common/pico_audio_i2s/audio_i2s.pio). embassy-rp publishes `PioI2sOut` ("Pio backed I2S output driver") for the rp235xa flavour with `new(common, sm, dma, irq, data_pin, bit_clock_pin, lr_clock_pin, sample_rate, bit_depth, ..., program)` and `write(&[u32]) -> Transfer` (https://docs.embassy.dev/embassy-rp/git/rp235xa/pio_programs/i2s/struct.PioI2sOut.html). The `pio` crate (v0.4.0, 2026-09-01, MIT) is `#![no_std]` and targets "RP2040 and RP235x chips" with `pio_asm!` and `pio_file!` macros (https://docs.rs/pio/latest/pio/ ; source https://docs.rs/crate/pio/latest/source/src/lib.rs). rustos today has no PIO driver (`rustos-toolchain-proof.md` F-series: PIO registers documented, no driver), so I2S means a new upstream driver plus a PIO program, versus PWM which is already work package WP-05.

**F3. PCM5102A is an active line-level DAC, not a headphone driver, and costs 18 to 30 mA.** SLAS859C (May 2012, revised May 2015): "2.1-VRMS ground-centered" output via an internal charge pump, "Line Level Output Down to 1KΩ", recommended load 1 kohm min to 10 kohm; "Integrated High-Performance Audio PLL with BCK Reference to Generate SCK Internally" (no MCLK needed); XSMT "Soft mute (Low) / soft un-mute (High)" with a "two-level mute system for pop-free performance"; 16/24/32-bit I2S or left-justified. Supply at 3.3 V, 48 kHz: DVDD 7 mA typ (bipolar-zero data) to 8 mA (-1 dBFS), 12 to 13 mA max; AVDD + CPVDD 11 mA typ (bipolar zero) to 22 mA (-1 dBFS), 16 to 32 mA max; power-down 0.5 mA + 0.2 mA. TSSOP-20 (hand-solderable, relevant to SI-031). TI product page status ACTIVE on 2026-09-25, which also points to a newer TAD5242 with an integrated headphone amplifier (not evaluated). A headphone amplifier is still required after it. Sources: https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/pcm5102a.pdf ; https://www.ti.com/product/PCM5102A .

**F4. UDA1334ATS is obsolete at NXP; do not design it in.** Datasheet dated 2000-07-31 (supersedes 2000-02-09): 900 mV rms at 0 dBFS (scales with supply), IDDA 3.5 mA + IDDD 2.5 mA, PLL for 256x/384x 48 kHz system clock, MUTE pin, de-emphasis. Distributor listings show "Obsolete" (Mouser search excerpt; the Arrow page timed out), while Adafruit still sells its UDA1334A breakout (product 3678, "in stock" on 2026-09-25) from remaining chip stock. Confidence on lifecycle: Medium (secondary listing only). Sources: https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/UDA1334ATS.pdf ; https://www.adafruit.com/product/3678 .

**F5. The fully analog reference (QRP Labs QCX) shows what an op-amp chain needs and where its clicks come from.** G0UPL, FDIM 2018: the CW filter is the NM0S Hi-Per-Mite, "700Hz center frequency ... and 200Hz bandwidth", "a measured 18dB of gain"; "Sidetone is generated by the microcontroller and is a squarewave ... the sidetone injection is done at the input to the 200Hz CW filter, which cuts off any of the squarewave harmonics leaving a clean 700Hz sinewave"; sidetone level is set by duty cycle, "Maximum volume occurs at 50% duty cycle ... adjustable in 100 levels from 0 to 99 ... via a logarithmic function"; "The audio amplifier ... is preceded by a MOSFET switch which shorts the audio to ground during TX. A simple delay circuit at the MOSFET gate gives several milliseconds for the Rx/Tx 'Pop' to subside before switching back to Receive"; gain stage "about 41dB", final stage unity gain "for stability driving the earphones"; "for headphone use with popular 32-ohm earphones for example, an op-amp is capable of providing sufficient drive by itself" (the QCX uses an LM4562, whose datasheet drive is +/-26 mA); and "I recommend avoiding the LM386". The same paper states the AGC caveat of audio-gain receivers: "It is also harder to apply good Automatic Gain Control (AGC) at audio, than at IF-derived (and applied) AGC", and the T/R sequencing: envelope decay "approximately 5ms", "the microcontroller waits for 10ms after key-up before ... switching back to Receive mode", and "The Transmit/Receive switch and audio mute must also remain in 'Transmit' mode during this period." Source: https://qrp-labs.com/images/news/dayton2018/fdim2018.pdf (pp. 19 to 21, 33 to 35).

**F6. A hybrid path (analog receive audio digitized by the RP2350 ADC, everything after that in firmware) is feasible but marginal on ADC noise.** RP2350 ADC: "12-bit with 9.2 ENOB" in the feature list and Table 1437 "Effective Number of Bits ENOB 9 min, 9.5 typ", input resistance 100 kohm min (local datasheet). Derived: 9.5 ENOB is 58.9 dB SNR in the Nyquist band; sampling at 48 kS/s and filtering to a 300 Hz CW bandwidth adds up to 19.0 dB of process gain if the ADC noise is white, about 78 dB in the CW channel. That is enough for a receiver whose analog stages already provide gain and limiting ahead of the ADC, but the RP2350 ADC's non-white error (DNL, reference noise) makes the 19 dB an upper bound. This matters only if D13 selects an analog superhet (receive audio born analog); with a QSD or I/Q DSP receiver the audio is already digital.

**F7. Reading of the table.** At a 3.3 V rail the TPA6132A2 is the only candidate that (a) needs no output electrolytics (space, and no turn-on charging pop), (b) draws 2.1 mA, (c) has fixed, pin-selected gain with a +/-0.45 dB tolerance so the hardware ceiling of F27 is a datasheet number, and (d) advertises "Constant Maximum Output Power from 2.3 V to 5.5 V Supply, Simplifies Design to Prevent Acoustic Shock", which is exactly the hazard-control property needed. ### C. Mute and click suppression at T/R transitions

**F9. Analog-domain practice: short the audio during TX, release slowly.** QCX: MOSFET shorts audio to ground during TX and a gate RC delays the release "several milliseconds" after the 5 ms envelope decay plus the 10 ms hold (F5). G4TGJ (2019-09-04): two 2N7000 MOSFETs back-to-back "so that the body diodes do not allow clicks to get through" ("This will conduct if the audio signal is loud enough so that any loud click ... will still pass through even when the MOSFET is off"); mute is immediate via a diode discharge, un-mute is slow: "C1 will take about 100K x 3.3u x loge(5/(5-2)) = 168 ms to reach 2V and switch on Q1 and Q2". QCX manual (via `keyer-and-key-interfaces.md` F8): sidetone injection must not shift the audio DC bias or a click is heard at changeover. Sources: https://g4tgj.github.io/Audio-Switch/ ; FDIM 2018 paper.

**F10. Amplifier-level help.** TPA6132A2: "Active Click and Pop Suppression", 5 ms start-up, and in shutdown "Input-to-output attenuation 80 dB" with a 20 ohm output impedance, so the EN pin is a usable hard mute for faults and for the no-headphones state, but not for per-element keying (5 ms re-enable and a charge-pump restart per dit would be audible). The datasheet also asks for input coupling capacitors and to "Activate the TPA6132A2 after all audio sources" are settled to avoid turn-on pop. MAX98357A publishes click-and-pop as -72/-66 dBV.

**F11. Digital-domain practice removes the problem class.** If receive audio and sidetone are both samples inside the RP2350 (F1, F6), T/R muting is a gain ramp: fade the receive stream with a 2 to 5 ms raised cosine at key-down, hold it at zero through the transmit and hang interval that the keyer already computes (`keyer-and-key-interfaces.md` F9, refdesign F29/F31), and fade back over 5 to 20 ms. There is no analog switch, no body diode and no DC step; the only analog transient left is whatever the receive front end injects into the ADC during T/R, and the firmware simply discards those samples. The residual click source in a synthesized receiver is the LO: G0UPL warns that some Si5351A libraries reset the PLL on every frequency write, giving a "horrible CLICK in the receiver audio" while tuning (FDIM 2018, p. 17); the fix is in the synthesizer driver (write only the MultiSynth registers when stepping).

### D. Sidetone injection and level control

**F12. Sidetone norms are settled in the keyer report and are audio-only by regulation.** `keyer-and-key-interfaces.md` F8 and SW-KEY-07: 600 or 700 Hz default (owner decision D4), 300 to 1000 Hz range, level 0 to 99 Relative/Absolute, onset within 1 ms, 3 to 5 ms envelope, no DC step; `part97-regulatory-basis.md` RF-03: the sidetone must never modulate the carrier.

**F13. Two injection methods.** Analog: sum a PWM square wave through a high-value resistor into the CW filter input so the filter removes harmonics and the duty cycle sets level (QCX, F5; the G4TGJ phasing board feeds sidetone "via a 1M resistor" into the summing op amp of its CW filter). Digital: add a sine-table sample scaled by the sidetone gain into the output stream before the common limiter, with the raised-cosine envelope from the keyer. The digital method is a few lines of host-testable code (SI-026) and needs no components.

**F14. Level control options.** (a) Firmware gain on the sample stream: free, 1 dB steps or finer, ramped to avoid zipper noise, persisted in non-volatile storage (rustos report allocates "frequency, WPM, keyer mode, sidetone pitch, volume" to `NvStore`). The volume knob of SI-006 becomes an ADC-read potentiometer or the encoder in a press-and-turn mode; it never touches the signal path. (b) I2C headphone amp with volume (TPA6130A2, 64 steps from -59.5 dB). (c) Log-taper digital potentiometer with zero-crossing switching (DS1882: "up to +/-7 V signal levels", "zero-crossing detector allows the wiper position to change when there is no voltage across the potentiometer", "0.5 dB channel-to-channel matching"); its supply requirements exceed a 3.3 V rail and were not verified (Low). Only (a) keeps the hardware ceiling (F27) and the limiter (F18) in one place.

### E. AGC in the audio domain

**F15. What commercial CW rigs do.** Elecraft KX2 (Owner's Manual rev A8): per-mode AGC speed with "The default for CW mode is FAST", an AGC threshold menu, and a dedicated limiter: "AF LIM ... Adjustable AF output limiter for use when AGC is turned off (MENU:AGC MD)"; "If an uncomfortably-strong signal appears in your headphones or speaker with AGC off, reduce the threshold of the AGC-off audio limiter (MENU:AF LIM)"; the appendix repeats "to avoid painful audio volume on strong signals." Icom IC-705: "More than 530 mW (12 ohm load at 10% distortion)" internal speaker and "More than 200 mW (8 ohm load at 10% distortion)" external (https://www.icomjapan.com/lineup/products/IC-705/), figures that are speaker-class and show why a headphone-only pocket radio must not copy commercial audio power. Source: https://ftp.elecraft.com/KX2/Manuals%20Downloads/E740282%20-%20KX2%20Owner's%20Man%20A8.pdf .

**F16. Analog audio AGC parts.** The SSM2167 microphone preamp/compressor is used by homebrewers as an audio AGC (uBITX community reports, SSM2166 predecessor with "60 dB dynamic range" and an AGC voltage output usable for an S-meter); it comes only in a 10-pin MSOP and "targets one specific application ... extremely difficult to make it work acceptably well" outside it (search excerpts; the ADI product and datasheet pages timed out twice, so status, supply range and ratios are unverified: Low). A discrete alternative is a JFET or back-to-back MOSFET shunt attenuator driven by a rectified, hang-timed control voltage, the same devices as the mute of F9. G0UPL's caveat (F5) applies to both.

**F17. Digital AGC.** With audio in firmware, AGC is a gain state machine on the sample stream: fast attack (a 40 dB step brought to within 3 dB of target in <= 2 ms is a typical CW target), hang time in the 100 to 1000 ms range with the KX2 "FAST" default for CW, decay after hang, and a hard limiter downstream (F18) so that "AGC off" is still safe. Every parameter is host-testable with synthetic tone bursts (SI-026). No commercial parameter values were found published as numbers; the ranges above are design starting points, not sourced norms (Low).

**F18. A limiter is required regardless of AGC.** The KX2 keeps an AF limiter for AGC-off operation (F15); EN 50332/IEC 62368-1 (F19) treat the maximum as a property of the device, not of the volume setting. In cwht the limiter is a sample clamp with a short attack in front of the PWM/DAC, and the hardware attenuator (F27) caps whatever the limiter lets through.

### F. Hearing-safety norms and earbud sensitivities

**F19. EN 50332-1:2013 and EN 50332-2:2013 (CENELEC), limits carried by IEC/EN 62368-1 clause 10.6.** From the WHO/ITU situation analysis (C. Bertin) and T. Lund's ITU workshop slides: for a player sold with its headphones (Part 1) "PMPs must be equipped with user protection if they are capable of delivering an Sound Pressure Level (SPL) of 85 dB(A) when playing a static test signal including: an active warning when SPL (of the test signal) is above 85 dB(A); a maximum SPL no higher than 100 dB(A)". For a player and headphones sold separately (Part 2) the player is measured "at the player's maximum volume setting and the voltage at the player's headphone socket is measured across a 32 ohm load"; "When a player producing 150 mV or less is combined with headphones which require 75 mV or more to produce a sound level of 94 dB(A), the sound level of the combination must be 100 dB(A) or less"; Lund adds the warning threshold: "an active warning when voltage (of the test signal) is above 27 mV; a maximum voltage no higher than 150 mV". Any player "providing a headphone socket with more than 27 mV output, must be accompanied by a warning" symbol at least 5 mm high. Listen Inc.'s test-sequence page gives the method: "IEC weighted pink noise stimulus file (as specified by IEC 268)", "The RMS voltage is averaged over 30 seconds", "The result must be less than 150 mV", "The WBCV for each headphone must be greater than 75 mV". The 2013 EN 50332 parts "specify the test method. The actual limits are specified in other standards (EN 60065 or EN 60950-1)", whose features were "standard acoustic output level LAeqT not greater than 85 dB(A)", "higher output level has to be acknowledged by user every 20 hours", "maximum output from player and listening device not greater than 100 dB(A)", and a child/adult distinction; both have since been replaced by IEC/EN 62368-1, in which "Phase 1 and 2 implemented in IEC 62368-1, clause 10.6". Hearing Review (Kawamori, Best, Laureyns, 2020-05-19): 62368-1 requires "LAeq,T ... lower or equal to the relevant RS2 sound output value of 100 dBA", with 85 dBA or lower regarded as safe. The situation analysis summarizes: "all devices should have a default output limit of 85 dB. The user can choose to override the limit ... up to maximum 100 dB. If the user overrides the limit, warnings about the risks must be repeated every 20 [hours]". These are EU obligations for personal music players, not for US amateur equipment; cwht adopts them voluntarily as the best-documented consumer norm. Sources: https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-T/Workshops-and-Seminars/safelistening/Documents/Standards_for_safe_listening_devices_situation_analysis_report.pdf ; https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-T/Workshops-and-Seminars/safelistening/Documents/Abstracts-and-Presentations/S2P1-Thomas-Lund.pdf ; https://www.listeninc.com/resources/test-sequences/measuring-spl-of-portable-devices-to-en-50332-2/ ; https://hearingreview.com/hearing-loss/hearing-loss-prevention/risk-factors/standards-for-safe-listening-past-present-and-future .

**F20. WHO/ITU H.870 dose approach.** ITU-T H.870 "Guidelines for safe listening devices/systems": edition 08/2018 superseded, edition 03/2022 in force (https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-H.870). WHO's companion publication (2019-09-18): "Mode 1 for adults: 80 dB for 40 hours per week", "Mode 2 for children: 75 dB for 40 hours per week", a dosimetry function reporting "a percentage used of a reference exposure, also known as a user's 'sound allowance'", "automatic volume reduction" and "parental volume control" (https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241515276). Reference exposures quoted in secondary sources as 1.6 Pa2h (Mode 1) and 0.51 Pa2h (Mode 2); derived check: (20 uPa x 10^(80/20))^2 x 40 h = 1.6 Pa2h and 0.506 Pa2h for 75 dB, so the figures are consistent. For a radio used a few hours a week, an 80 dB(A) average is far below the limiter setting of F27, so a dose accumulator is an option, not a necessity.

**F21. US occupational references (context only; no US consumer rule exists).** NIOSH REL: "85 A-weighted decibels (dBA) averaged over an eight-hour workday" with "For each 3 dBA increase in noise level, NIOSH recommends reducing the exposure duration by half" (CDC page last updated 2024-01-30, https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/noise/about/noise.html). Derived durations from the 3 dB rule: 88 dBA 4 h, 91 dBA 2 h, 94 dBA 1 h, 97 dBA 30 min, 100 dBA 15 min, 103 dBA 7.5 min. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95 Table G-16 "Permissible Noise Exposures" (eCFR issue 2026-09-23): 90 dBA 8 h; 92 dBA 6 h; 95 dBA 4 h; 97 dBA 3 h; 100 dBA 2 h; 102 dBA 1.5 h; 105 dBA 1 h; 110 dBA 0.5 h; 115 dBA 0.25 h or less, "measured on the A scale of a standard sound level meter at slow response" (5 dB exchange). Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-23/title-29.xml?part=1910&section=1910.95 .

**F22. Acoustic shock limits (telephony headsets).** ITU-T P.360 (07/2006), clause 4.2 g): for longer-duration disturbances such as tones "the steady-state A-weighted sound pressure level should be below +31 dBPa(A) for the handset and +24 dBPa(A) for the headset 'slow' response" measured at the ear reference point; +24 dBPa(A) is 118 dB(A) SPL (0 dBPa = 94 dB SPL, derived); tones shorter than 0.5 s are assessed as impulses against "the applicable national or regional safety regulations, e.g., U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, 29CFR1910.95 ... and the Directive 2003/10/EC"; clause 4.3 references the 8 h TWA to regional limits and notes "In North America the highest Daily Noise Exposure is 115 dBA for 15 minutes exposure." The Jabra SafeTone white paper (2019-03-08) restates industry practice: 118 dB RMS as the accepted limit, "140 dB(C) is accepted as the absolute peak value limit", EU Directive 2003/10/EC "upper max exposure limit of 87 dB(A) (time weighted average ...)" with 85 dB(A) as the upper action value. These are ceilings for injury prevention, roughly 18 dB above the 100 dB(A) consumer maximum; a device that respects F19 respects P.360 by a wide margin. Sources: https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-P.360-200607-I!!PDF-E&type=items ; https://www.blueparrott.com/-/media/Documentation/Technical-Whitepapers/whitepaper_safetone__08_03_2019_final.pdf .

**F23. Earbud and headphone sensitivities.** Sony MDR-EX15LP manufacturer data (via fullspecs.net, secondary): 16 ohm, "100 dB" (dB/mW), 100 mW maximum input, 9 mm driver; measured by reference-audio-analyzer.pro: 16.9 / 17.1 ohm at 1 kHz, "121.6 dB/V SPL", "103.9 dB/mW SPL" (100 Hz to 10 kHz average). Apple EarPods measured: 42.0 / 41.4 ohm, 116.7 / 121.1 dB/V, 103.0 / 107.4 dB/mW (left/right). Sennheiser CX 300-II retail listing: 16 ohm, "113 dB" (basis unstated; Low). A general guide puts earphones at "80 to 125 dB SPL/mW" and full-size headphones at "90 dB SPL and 105 dB SPL/mW" (mynewmicrophone.com; Low). Smartphone headphone outputs measured by hobbyists reach "0.80 Vrms" typical and "up to 1.5 Vrms" into 32 ohm (diyAudio thread; Low), while EU-limited players stop at 150 mV. Note that the EN 50332-2 headphone assumption (94 dB(A) needs >= 75 mV, i.e. <= 116.5 dB/V) is met by the EarPods but exceeded by about 5 dB by the EX15LP as measured, so real earbuds can be louder than the standard's model. Sources: https://fullspecs.net/earphone/sony/mdr-ex15lp-specifications/ ; https://reference-audio-analyzer.pro/en/report/hp/sony-ex15lp.php ; https://reference-audio-analyzer.pro/en/report/hp/apple-earpods.php ; https://mynewmicrophone.com/the-complete-guide-to-headphones-sensitivity-ratings/ ; https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/a-test-how-much-voltage-power-do-your-phones-need.325461/ .

**F25. Unrestricted amplifier output is a hazard.** Derived full-scale voltages into 32 ohm and the SPL they produce (100 dB/mW phones / 121.6 dB/V earbuds): TPA6132A2 22 mW = 0.84 Vrms, 113 / 120 dB SPL; PAM8908 30 mW (3.6 V) = 0.98 Vrms, 115 / 121 dB; PAM8908 61 mW (5 V) = 1.40 Vrms, 118 / 124 dB; LM4880 85 mW (5 V) = 1.65 Vrms, 119 / 126 dB. ### G. Jack detection and no-headphones behaviour

**F26. A tip-switch jack gives plug detection for free.** Same Sky SJ-352X-SMT series datasheet (dated 04/15/2025; revision history 1.04 "brand update" 10/09/2019): 3.5 mm stereo right-angle SMT jacks, "switch options include: no switch, tip switch"; SJ-3523-SMT has no switch, SJ-3524-SMT adds pin 4 "tip switch"; ratings "rated input voltage 12 Vdc", "rated input current 1 A", "contact resistance 50 mOhm" max, insulation 100 MOhm at 500 Vdc, "voltage withstand 500 Vac" for 1 min, insertion/withdrawal force 0.3 to 3 kg, operating temperature -25 to 85 C, "life 5,000 cycles", UL94V-0, RoHS. The product page confirms "3 conductors, 1 internal switch" for SJ-3524-SMT-TR. Whether pin 4 is open or closed with no plug must be read from the drawing (not resolved in the text extraction; Low), and either polarity works with a GPIO input. The switch operates on the tip, so a mono TS plug is detected as well as TRS. Alternative DC-sense detection (a pull-up through the headphones) is impractical with a DirectPath amp whose output is a low impedance even in shutdown (20 ohm, F10). Sources: https://www.sameskydevices.com/product/resource/sj-352x-smt.pdf ; https://www.sameskydevices.com/product/interconnect/connectors/audio-connectors/jacks/sj-3524-smt-tr .

**F27. Hardware ceiling design (derived, example values).** With the TPA6132A2 at 0 dB gain (-0.95 to -1.05 V/V), a divider R1 = 10 kohm, R2 = 953 ohm (1 %) gives k = 0.0870 (-21.2 dB): full-scale PWM sine 1.167 Vrms becomes 101.5 mVrms; the worst possible digital pattern, a full-scale square wave at an audio rate, becomes 143.6 mVrms (+3.0 dB), still under 150 mV. Divider tolerance with 1 % resistors is about +/-0.1 dB and the amplifier gain +/-0.45 dB, so the ceiling is 100 mVrms +/-8 % by design. Reconstruction: C1 = 56 nF across the divider (Thevenin 870 ohm) gives a 3.27 kHz pole; a second R3 = 1 kohm, C2 = 47 nF stage gives 3.39 kHz; two poles attenuate the 146.5 kHz carrier by about 66 dB, so PWM residual at the amplifier input is 0.5 mV class and beyond any earphone's response; the amplifier's own input coupling capacitors (datasheet requirement) block the PWM's 1.65 V DC midpoint. The 3.3 kHz corner leaves the 300 to 1500 Hz CW band within about 1 dB. These values are a starting point for the LTspice analysis (ACTION A1), not a design release.

**F28. Behaviour with no headphones.** With a tip switch the firmware knows the jack state. Options: (a) shut the amplifier down (EN low, saves 2.1 mA, and the open jack carries no signal), show a "no phones" indicator, keep the keyer functional (the operator still sees keying on the LCD, and a plugged-in key still transmits); (b) additionally route audio to an internal speaker if one exists (F8); (c) do nothing. Insertion while a tone is playing: with two separately driven channels a TRS plug briefly shorts L to R and both to sleeve during insertion; DirectPath amplifiers with short-circuit protection tolerate it, and a 100 ms volume ramp after detection makes the event silent. Cross-plugging (CTL-KEY-06): a key in the PHONES jack shorts ring to sleeve permanently and tip to sleeve when pressed, which is a continuous output short on one or both channels; the TPA6132A2 and PAM8908 state short-circuit protection, the LM4880 text extracted does not, the NJM4556A has none. Headphones in the KEY jack put 3.3 V through the 10 kohm pull-up into 16 ohm (0.3 mA, inaudible, harmless) and read as "key closed", which SW-KEY-10 already turns into a "check plug" message instead of a transmission.

**F29. Mono plugs.** The KX2 accepts both: "The 3.5 mm PHONES jack accommodates headphones or one or two externally amplified speakers. Mono or stereo plugs can be used." For cwht the natural choice is to drive L and R from the two amplifier channels with the same mono sample stream (so stereo earbuds give both ears) and to accept that a TS plug shorts the R channel; the alternative, 10 to 33 ohm series resistors per channel, protects the short but costs 2.5 to 6 dB into 32 ohm and changes the ceiling arithmetic. The TPA6132A2 short-circuit current was not found as a number in the extracted text (ACTION A2).

### H. Power and assembly consequences

**F30. Receive-side current.** PWM source plus TPA6132A2: about 2.1 mA plus microamps in the passive network. I2S DAC path: PCM5102A 18 to 30 mA typical plus the amplifier, i.e. an extra 240 mAh over the 8 h of SI-034 (derived), a few percent of a 2S 18650 pack (cell capacity not sourced here). LM4880 3.6 mA, PAM8908 4 mA, NJM4556A 8 to 9 mA, TPA6130A2 4 mA. A class-D speaker amplifier idles at 2.4 to 4 mA but draws hundreds of milliamps at volume.

**F31. Assembly.** TPA6132A2 (3 x 3 mm WQFN) and PAM8908 (U-QFN3030) are hidden-pad packages: fine for PCBWay placement (SI-031) but not owner-repairable; PCM5102A (TSSOP-20), LM4880 (SOIC-8) and SJ-3524-SMT (gull-wing SMT) are hand-solderable. A do-not-populate TSSOP-20 footprint for the I2S DAC is therefore a realistic owner-installable fallback, whereas a spare headphone-amp footprint would not be.

## Implications for cwht

Tags: REQ-candidate (requirement to write), RISK-candidate (risk register), HZ-candidate (hazard analysis), DECISION-needed (owner or PDR trade), ACTION (task).

### Recommended baseline

The RP2350 owns the audio output. Receive audio (digital from a QSD/DSP receiver, or analog audio digitized by the RP2350 ADC if D13 picks a superhet), sidetone, T/R muting, AGC, limiter and volume are all sample-domain firmware on top of the rustos api traits (SI-026), and the physical output is one PWM channel (10 to 11 bits at 73 to 146 kHz, CC fed by DMA on wrap; work package WP-05 extended) into the passive divider-plus-two-pole network of F27, a TPA6132A2 at 0 dB gain on the 3.3 V rail driving both channels of an SJ-3524-SMT tip-switch jack. The amplifier EN pin is a GPIO with a hardware pull-down; the tip switch is a GPIO with pull-up and Schmitt trigger (RP2350-E9 rule). A do-not-populate PCM5102A footprint (I2S over PIO) is the documented fallback if PWM noise proves unacceptable on the bench. No speaker in rev A (D1 below).

### Requirement candidates

1. **REQ-candidate SYS-AUD-01 (headphone interface).** The radio shall provide one 3.5 mm 3-conductor headphone jack driving left and right with the same monophonic signal, accepting TS and TRS plugs, for headphone impedances of 16 to 64 ohm (F23, F29). [Inspection + Bench]
2. **REQ-candidate SYS-AUD-02 (hardware output ceiling, hazard control).** The headphone output voltage into 32 ohm shall not exceed 150 mVrms for any digital output pattern and shall be 100 mVrms +/-10 % for a full-scale 700 Hz sine, set by fixed passive components and fixed amplifier gain, independent of firmware state (F19, F24, F25, F27). Rationale: 100 mV bounds the SPL at 96.5 dB for EN 50332-2 boundary phones and 101.6 dB SPL (100.2 dB(A)) for the most sensitive earbuds measured, the consumer maximum of EN 50332/62368-1; 150 mV harmonizes the worst-case pattern with the EN player limit. [Analysis (LTspice) + Bench (AC RMS across a 32 ohm 1 % resistor with a firmware full-scale tone)]
3. **REQ-candidate SW-AUD-01 (default level cap and unlock).** Firmware shall cap the output at 30 mVrms-equivalent (about 85 dB(A) class per F24) until the operator acknowledges a high-level warning; the unlocked cap is the hardware ceiling; the acknowledgment shall expire after 20 h of cumulative operating time in the unlocked state and the cap shall revert at power-on unless persisted by explicit choice (F19). [HostUnit + Bench]
4. **REQ-candidate SW-AUD-02 (limiter).** A sample-domain limiter with attack <= 1 ms shall bound receive audio and sidetone to the active cap, including when AGC is off (F15, F18). [HostUnit]
5. **REQ-candidate SW-AUD-03 (T/R muting and fades).** Receive audio shall be faded to <= -60 dBFS with a raised-cosine ramp within 2 ms of key-down, held muted through transmit and the keyer's hang or break-in interval, and restored with a 5 to 20 ms fade; sidetone onset within 1 ms of key-down with a 3 to 5 ms envelope (SW-KEY-07); no DC step at any transition (F9, F11). [HostUnit + Bench]
6. **REQ-candidate SW-AUD-04 (AGC).** Audio AGC with attack bringing a 40 dB input step to within 3 dB of target in <= 2 ms, hang 100 to 1000 ms configurable, CW default FAST, and an AGC-off mode permitted only with SW-AUD-02 active (F15, F17; parameter values TBR). [HostUnit]
7. **REQ-candidate SW-AUD-05 (volume and sidetone level).** Digital volume in <= 1 dB steps, ramped at <= 1 dB per ms to avoid zipper noise, persisted; sidetone level 0 to 99 with Relative/Absolute behaviour (SW-KEY-07) mixed before the limiter (F13, F14). [HostUnit]
8. **REQ-candidate CTL-AUD-01 (amplifier enable and start-up).** The amplifier enable shall be pulled down in hardware, asserted only after the audio source has idled at mid-scale for >= 20 ms and the input coupling capacitors have charged, and de-asserted on brown-out or fault (F10, TPA6132A2 datasheet turn-on guidance). [Inspection + Bench]
9. **REQ-candidate CTL-AUD-02 (jack detection).** A tip-switch jack shall report plug presence to a GPIO (pull-up plus Schmitt, per RP2350-E9); with no plug the amplifier shall be shut down and the LCD shall indicate it; on insertion the output shall ramp from zero over 100 ms after a 50 ms debounce (F26, F28). [HostUnit + Bench]
10. **REQ-candidate CTL-AUD-03 (survivability).** Either or both headphone outputs shall survive an indefinite short to sleeve (mono plug, key cross-plugged) and ESD per CTL-KEY-04 without damage (F28, F29). [Analysis + Bench]
11. **REQ-candidate CTL-AUD-04 (reconstruction).** PWM carrier residual at the amplifier input shall be at least 50 dB below full scale and the passband flat within 1 dB from 300 to 1500 Hz (F27). [Analysis]
12. **REQ-candidate PWR-AUD-01.** Audio chain quiescent current in receive shall be <= 5 mA at 3.3 V (F30). [Analysis + Bench]
13. **REQ-candidate ICD-CTL-AUD.** Pin map: PWM output on a slice distinct from the backlight slice (rustos report), EN GPIO, DET GPIO; jack part number; network values; test points for the 32 ohm bench measurement.

### Hazards

- **HZ-candidate (excessive acoustic output at the headphones).** Causes: design gain, firmware fault (full-scale pattern), strong signal with AGC off, sidetone at maximum. Controls: SYS-AUD-02 (hardware ceiling), SW-AUD-01/02 (cap and limiter), CTL-AUD-01 (no output until firmware is ready). Severity: hearing injury to the owner or a friend (SI-019); the uncontrolled amplifiers reach 113 to 126 dB SPL (F25).
- **HZ-candidate (acoustic startle at power-on, plug insertion or T/R).** Controls: CTL-AUD-01, CTL-AUD-02 ramp, SW-AUD-03 fades, amplifier pop suppression (F10).

### Risks

- **RISK-candidate R-AUD-1 (PWM noise and coupling).** PWM audio shares supply and ground with the digital section; display SPI, flash and synthesizer activity can appear as hiss or ticks (F1). Mitigation: DMA-fed PWM, dedicated analog return, LTspice plus bench listening test; fallback is the DNP PCM5102A footprint (F31).
- **RISK-candidate R-AUD-2 (ADC path if D13 selects an analog superhet).** RP2350 ADC 9.0 to 9.5 ENOB (F6) may add audible noise; the analog stages then must deliver AGC or limiting before the ADC, or the audio stays analog with the QCX-style mute and injection (F5, F9).
- **RISK-candidate R-AUD-3 (earbuds louder than the EN model).** Measured earbuds exceed the EN 50332-2 headphone sensitivity assumption by up to 5 dB (F23); the 100 mV ceiling absorbs it, a 150 mV ceiling would not (105 dB SPL).
- **RISK-candidate R-AUD-4 (part availability unverified).** No distributor stock could be read (Method 6); TPA6132A2, PCM5102A, LM4880 show ACTIVE on TI pages; PAM8908 and MAX98357A lifecycle unverified; UDA1334ATS obsolete; NJM4556A DIP/SIP NRND.
- **RISK-candidate R-AUD-5 (mono plug short).** A TS plug shorts one channel continuously; protection current and thermal behaviour of the TPA6132A2 under a permanent short are not quantified (F29, ACTION A2).
- **RISK-candidate R-AUD-6 (tuning clicks).** Synthesizer driver resets on frequency writes produce audible clicks (F11); a driver requirement, tracked with the synthesizer trade.

### Decisions needed

- **DECISION-needed D1 (speaker or headphones only).** Recommendation: headphones only in rev A. A speaker needs a 5 V class-D amplifier (MAX98357A over I2S, 2.4 to 2.75 mA idle, or PAM8302A, 4 mA idle), a 15 to 20 mm driver (89 to 105 dB at 0.1 m class, F8), CNC grille features in the enclosure, and a second acoustic-hazard analysis; it also lets a group listen (SI-019). If wanted, reserve MAX98357A pads and a speaker cavity now and populate in a later revision.
- **DECISION-needed D2 (maximum level policy).** Pick one: (a) 100 mVrms sine ceiling with 150 mV worst-case pattern, 30 mV default cap with acknowledged unlock and 20 h expiry (recommended, SYS-AUD-02 and SW-AUD-01 as written); (b) 150 mVrms sine ceiling harmonized literally with EN 50332-2 players (louder with sensitive earbuds, up to 105 dB SPL); (c) no firmware cap, hardware ceiling only.
- **DECISION-needed D3 (audio source).** PWM baseline with a DNP PCM5102A footprint (recommended) versus I2S DAC from the start (+18 to 30 mA, new rustos PIO driver) versus analog op-amp chain (QCX pattern; more parts, analog mute, sidetone summing, less host-testable).
- **DECISION-needed D4 (no-headphones behaviour).** Recommendation: amplifier shutdown plus LCD indicator; keyer remains live so a plugged key still transmits (or, owner's choice, refuse to transmit with no headphones so the operator cannot miss a busy frequency).
- **DECISION-needed D5 (mono plug handling).** Accept TS plugs with a shorted channel under protection (recommended, pending A2) versus per-channel series resistors versus TRS-only with a manual note.
- **DECISION-needed D6 (dose accumulator).** Implement an H.870-style weekly sound allowance in firmware (adds non-volatile bookkeeping) or rely on the cap-plus-unlock policy only (recommended for rev A).
- **DECISION-needed D7 (sidetone defaults).** Already open as keyer report D4 (600 vs 700 Hz, Relative vs Absolute); the audio chain is indifferent.
