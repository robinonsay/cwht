# CW selectivity options for cwht: commercial crystal filters, tolerance-designed ladders, wide IF plus DSP, audio filters, I/Q (assignment: selectivity)

**Status:** research report for the PDR receiver trade study. **Date:** 2026-09-25. **Author:** Claude (research subagent). **Owner:** Robin.
**Extends:** `docs/research/2m-cw-transceiver-reference-designs.md` (F32 to F34, implication 15) and `docs/research/ltspice-batch-macos.md` (wrapper and checker). Nothing from those reports is repeated here except where a number is needed for a comparison.
**Evidence files:** `docs/research/sim/cw-selectivity/` (generator `xtal_ladder_mc.py`, batch driver `mc_batch.sh`, decks `out/*.net`, logs `out/*.log`, per-run metrics `out/*.csv`, summaries `out/*.json`) and `docs/research/figures/cw-selectivity/*.png`. Raw waveform files (`*.raw`, about 55 MB per case) are regenerated by the batch driver and are not kept.

## Question

Compare ways to obtain about 500 Hz CW selectivity with dynamic range adequate for a 2 m simplex handheld that PCBWay assembles without hand matching: (1) commercial crystal filters (KVG XF-9NB and other catalogue 500 Hz filters at 9 to 12 MHz: quote status, price, lead time); (2) a tolerance-designed crystal ladder from catalogue crystals, with a headless LTspice Monte Carlo of 4-pole and 6-pole ladders using catalogue parameter spreads, reporting bandwidth and ripple distributions; (3) a wider IF filter (ceramic or monolithic crystal, 2.4 to 15 kHz) followed by audio DSP on the RP2350, comparing the RP2350's own 12-bit ADC against an external I2S ADC or codec (PCM1808, WM8960, TLV320AIC3204 via PIO I2S), with an honest assessment of the RP2350 ADC's effective bits and the AGC-pumping issue; (4) analog op-amp audio filtering (QCX style); (5) direct conversion or low-IF I/Q with software filtering. For each: selectivity shape, ultimate rejection, dynamic-range consequences, current draw, part count, turnkey compatibility, firmware effort and risk to first-power-on success. Recommend two architecture candidates for the PDR trade study with the numbers needed to write L1 selectivity and MDS requirements as TBRs.

## Method

1. Read the two prior reports named above, the charter (`docs/process/00-charter.md` section 11) and the stakeholder inputs (SI-005, SI-009, SI-010, SI-013, SI-019, SI-024).
2. Web research with WebSearch and WebFetch (the session's search budget was exhausted near the end; the last catalogue checks were done by direct fetch only). Datasheets and papers that WebFetch could not parse were downloaded with `curl` into the session scratchpad and converted with `pdftotext -layout`, then searched with `grep`: KVG "Crystal filter 5.0 MHz to 21.4 MHz" sheet, ECS HC-49US crystal sheet, ECS monolithic crystal filter sheet, Abracon ABLS sheet, PCM1808 datasheet, Murata CERAFIL catalogue P05E, Cirrus WM8960 EOL-000028 notice, RP2350 datasheet (local copy in the rustos repo, build 2025-02-20), the QMX design paper (G0UPL, FDIM), the QCX design paper (G0UPL, FDIM 2018), the Hi-Per-Mite manual (4SQRP), the QEX Nov/Dec 2009 crystal ladder article (Steder and Hardcastle), and the WA5BDU and K8IQY crystal measurement papers.
3. Distributor stock and price: DigiKey, Mouser, Octopart, TrustedParts and findchips all returned HTTP 403 or empty results to automated fetches today; LCSC product pages, the TI store and one Polish distributor (hesta.pl) did respond. Search-snippet prices are marked as such (Low confidence). Times are local (Pacific), 2026-09-25.
4. Hands-on analysis (headless, charter rule 11.8): a Python generator (`xtal_ladder_mc.py`) synthesises a crystal ladder by the Dishal/Zverev k-q method (Butterworth or Chebyshev prototypes computed analytically; shunt coupling capacitors Ck = Cm f0 / (B k); end terminations R = 2 pi Lm B / q minus Rs; series tuning capacitors equalising the mesh resonances), emits an LTspice deck with `.step param run 1 N` and LTspice's `flat()` and `gauss()` random functions so every run draws fresh crystal and component values, runs LTspice 26.0.2 through the bundled CrossOver `wine` exactly as proven in `ltspice-batch-macos.md` (F3, F4), parses the stepped `.raw` with spicelib 1.6.3, and computes per-run insertion loss, -3/-6/-60 dB bandwidths, centre error, true peak-to-valley ripple, attenuation at fixed offsets and a yield against TBR limits. Each Monte Carlo case is 200 runs of a 6001-point AC sweep (about 10 s of wall time per case). Every `.log` starts with `LTspice 26.0.2 for MacOS`. Plots were rendered with matplotlib (Agg) and inspected (charter rule 11.3).
5. Derived numbers (noise floors, free-space path loss, ADC processing gain, image-rejection from I/Q mismatch, insertion-loss formula) are computed in this report and labelled derived.

Command lines used (from `docs/research/sim/cw-selectivity/`):

```

    --runs 2 --f-ppm 0 --cm-sigma-pct 0 --c0-min-pf 4.5 --c0-max-pf 4.5 --cap-pct 0 --res-pct 0   # nominal check
./mc_batch.sh 540 200 660      # nine Monte Carlo cases, 200 runs each, results in out/ and ../../figures/cw-selectivity/
```

## Findings

### A. Framing numbers

**F1. Noise floor, MDS and the signals a 2 m simplex group will actually see (derived).** Thermal noise in 500 Hz is -147.0 dBm; with a system noise figure of 5 dB the MDS is -142 dBm, with 7 dB it is -140 dBm (FT-290R class, reference report F14). Free-space path loss at 144 MHz is 55.6 dB at 100 m, 75.6 dB at 1 km, 89.6 dB at 5 km and 95.6 dB at 10 km (20 log10(4 pi d f / c)). A friend running 5 W (+37 dBm) with a 0 dBi antenna therefore arrives at -19 dBm from 100 m, -39 dBm from 1 km and -53 dBm from 5 km; a 50 W ERP repeater output arrives at -43 dBm from 5 km. The desired weak signal is 0 to 10 dB above MDS, so the receiver must tolerate in-band or near-band signals 90 to 120 dB above the wanted one. Off-channel signals more than about 100 kHz away are the job of the front-end filtering and blocking dynamic range (IC-705: 122 dB, reference report F5) and are outside this report. Within a few kHz, the selectivity chain and the LO set the limit: reciprocal mixing in 500 Hz is 85 dB at 10 kHz for a -112 dBc/Hz LO (Si5351 at VHF, unverified report) and 106 dB for -133 dBc/Hz (LMX2571 derived), reference report F15 and F17. A filter with more than about 85 dB ultimate rejection therefore buys nothing until the synthesizer decision (reference report implication 14) is made. No 47 CFR Part 97 provision read for this project constrains receiver selectivity; the transmitter constraints are in `part97-regulatory-basis.md`.

### B. Option 1: commercial crystal filters

**F2. KVG (Neckarbischofsheim, DE): current standard sheet has a 1.0 kHz CW filter at 9.000 MHz but no 500 Hz type; everything is quote-only.** The current "Crystal filter, examples of our product range from 5.0 MHz to 21.4 MHz" sheet (kvg-gmbh.de, `02_1_Crystal-filter-5.0-MHz-21.4-MHz.pdf`) lists at 9 MHz: XF-9B01-LF (8.9985 MHz, 6 dB at +/-1.2 kHz, 60 dB at +/-2.2 kHz, 500 ohm // 30 pF, insertion loss max 3.5 dB, case BF-01), **XF-90S52-LF (9.000 MHz, 6 dB at +/-0.5 kHz = 1.0 kHz, 60 dB at +/-1.3 kHz = 2.6 kHz, 500 ohm // 30 pF, insertion loss max 5.0 dB, BF-01)** and XF-9B02-LF (9.0015 MHz, as 9B01). Also listed: XF-100S46-LF (10.000 MHz, 3 dB at +/-0.125 kHz = 250 Hz, 60 dB at +/-0.35 kHz, 50 ohm, 10.0 dB loss), XF-107S47-LF (10.700 MHz, 3 dB at +/-0.50 kHz, 40 dB at +/-1.0 kHz, 50 ohm, 3.5 dB), XF-150S05-LF (15.000 MHz, 6 dB at +/-1.0 kHz, 60 dB at +/-3.0 kHz, 50 ohm, 6.0 dB). The KVG bandpass page states: 2 to 16 poles, hermetically sealed metal housing for wave or hand soldering, centre frequencies 0.4 to 200 MHz, Chebyshev or Butterworth, nominal input power 0 dBm, maximum 10 dBm, -55 to +105 C, RoHS and REACH; no prices, minimum quantities or lead times are published and the page directs customers to contact the company (info@kvg-gmbh.de, +49 7263 648-0). The XF-9NB (500 Hz, 8 poles, 6.5 dB loss, 60 dB at +/-1.1 kHz, ultimate > 100 dB, 500 ohm // 30 pF) and XF-9P (250 Hz) appear only on the legacy 9 MHz sheet mirrored at kn34pc.com (reference report F33); they are not on the current sheet. Used-market signals: one XF-9B sold for $21.50 and a pair was offered at EUR 101 (PicClick archives). Case BF-01 is 19.4 mm tall (KVG case drawing), a through-hole metal can: PCBWay can place it as a THT part but it dominates the enclosure height budget. Confidence: High for the sheet contents, Low for price and lead time (unquoted).

**F3. Inrad (International Radio, "Copyright 1972-2026"): catalogue 8-pole CW filters exist near 9 MHz at $118.** Crystal IF filter listing (inrad.net, cat=4): #111 (400 Hz, 9010.6 kHz, CW, 8 poles, shape factor 2.0, "250 Hz: < 9 dB; 500 Hz: < 7 dB; SSB/AM: < 6 dB" insertion loss, price **$118.00**), #112 (400 Hz, 9011.5 kHz), #110 (2100 Hz, 9011.5 kHz), #115 (400 Hz, 10749.3 kHz), #113 (2100 Hz, 10750.0 kHz), #701 (400 Hz, 8215 kHz), #119 (250 Hz, 8215.9 kHz), plus 455 kHz types (#1005 and #721 500 Hz, #116 400 Hz at $174.25). Stock and lead time are not shown; one item (#719) is marked DISCONTINUED, so the line is being thinned. Inrad filters are specified for a host radio's IF and their termination impedance is not on the web page (open item). A cwht IF of 9.0106 MHz is as easy for the Si5351 as 9.000 MHz, so #111 is a real catalogue option. Confidence: Medium (price seen on the product page today; availability unknown).

**F4. Other commercial paths are closed or wrong.** Yaesu YF-122C (Collins mechanical 500 Hz at 455 kHz) is discontinued and trades secondhand around $250 (qrper.com); Icom and Kenwood optional filters are the same story and all sit at 455 kHz or radio-specific IFs. Krystaly (Hradec Kralove, CZ) lists front-end crystal filters for 60 to 250 MHz and custom work on request; no 9 MHz CW standard type. Chinese marketplace 9 MHz filters exist but have no datasheet or lot control and cannot be placed by PCBWay turnkey from a distributor. Confidence: Medium.

### C. Option 2: tolerance-designed ladder filter from catalogue crystals

**F5. What a catalogue crystal guarantees, and what it does not.** ECS HC-49US (ecsxtal.com `hc_49us.pdf`): frequency tolerance +/-30 ppm at 25 C, stability +/-50 ppm (-10 to +70 C), shunt capacitance C0 7.0 pF max, ESR max **60 ohm for 9.000 to 12.999 MHz** (80 ohm for 7.000 to 8.999 MHz), drive level 0.5 mW, aging +/-5 ppm first year; part numbering ECS-freq-load-package, e.g. ECS-90-18-4XEN (DigiKey text: "9 MHz +/-30ppm Crystal 18pF 60 Ohms HC-49/US", search snippet, undated). Abracon ABLS (SMD HC-49/US): tolerance codes 1 = +/-10 ppm, 7 = +/-15, 2 = +/-20, 3 = +/-25, 4 = +/-30, blank = +/-50 ppm; C0 7 pF max; ESR max 60 ohm (9.000 to 9.999 MHz), 50 ohm (10.000 to 15.999 MHz); DLD change in ESR < 25 % of max. **Neither sheet specifies the motional capacitance Cm (or Lm) or a typical ESR**, and both are what a ladder filter is made of. At 9 MHz the datasheet ESR bound corresponds to an unloaded Q of only 1/(2 pi f Cm Rs) = 14,700 for Cm = 20 fF (derived); real HC-49 crystals measure far better (F6) but nothing obliges them to. A crystal marked for a 30 pF load resonates in series about f Cm / (2 (C0 + CL)) = 2.6 kHz below the marked frequency (derived for Cm 20 fF, C0 4.5 pF; the Land Boards 50-crystal batch of 9.000 MHz IQD LFXTAL064523 (30 pF) measured fs = 8.997799 MHz mean, i.e. 2.2 kHz low, consistent). The filter centre then lands about 2 kHz below the marked frequency plus a few hundred hertz of mesh shift; the BFO must be set to the measured centre, not to the marked frequency. Confidence: High.

**F6. Published measurements of crystal parameter spread and the matching rules.** Land Boards (IQD LFXTAL064523, 9 MHz, 50 pieces): series resonance 8.997668 to 8.997937 MHz, **total spread 269 Hz (+/-15 ppm), standard deviation 36.34 Hz (4 ppm)**. Giangrandi (10 MHz HC-49): Cs 27.132 fF, Ls 9.343 mH, Cp 6.385 pF; selection within 100 Hz; groups of four measured 95 Hz and 136 Hz apart; "even if all the crystals look identical ... they are not really identical, always producing higher ripple in the pass-band than planned". QEX Nov/Dec 2009 (Steder and Hardcastle): 4.9 MHz crystals Cm 15.053 fF and 13.805 fF by two methods; "One cannot assume that crystals with equal frequencies and the same case style will show the same motional inductance. This can vary significantly from manufacturer to manufacturer and can even vary from batch to batch"; acceptable frequency tolerance "approximately +/-2 % of the design bandwidth ... around +/-50 Hz [SSB] but only +/-10 Hz for a CW filter", compensated in practice by per-crystal series capacitors. K8IQY (Atlanticon 2002): example Rs 13.0 ohm; C0 estimate 3.5 pF for HC-49; "The rule of thumb is to have the crystals matched within 10 % of the filter's bandwidth. So for a 500 Hz filter, the crystals should all be within a span of 50 Hz"; from a batch of 20 or more, sets of four are often within 20 to 25 Hz. WA5BDU: four measurement methods agree within 5 % on Lm and Cm; changing Lm/Cm by 5 % in a design changed the bandwidth "a bit" but kept the shape; Q "maybe 120,000 or so"; C0 about 220 Cm. Reference report F32: builders match to 20 to 100 Hz. **All of these are hand-selection practices; a turnkey build gets whatever comes off the reel.** Confidence: High for the quotations; Medium for generalising a single 50-piece batch.

**F7. Synthesis and nominal simulation: at 9 MHz a 500 Hz ladder is loss-limited by crystal Q before it is tolerance-limited.** Nominal crystal used: fs 9.000 MHz, Cm 20 fF (between the 15 fF and 27 fF published values), Lm 15.636 mH, C0 4.5 pF, Rs 15 ohm typical (Qu 58,900). Butterworth 4-pole, B = 500 Hz: k = 0.841/0.541/0.841, q = 0.765, Ck = 428/665/428 pF, end tuning capacitors 665 pF, loaded end Q 13,800, terminations 39 ohm at Rs 25 and **4.2 ohm at the datasheet-maximum Rs of 60 ohm (not buildable)**; Chebyshev 0.1 dB needs 19 ohm at Rs 25. Butterworth 6-pole: Ck = 308/595/696/595/308 pF, terminations 70 ohm at Rs 25, 35 ohm at Rs 60. A 4-pole 2.4 kHz Chebyshev needs Ck = 90/114/90 pF, 188 ohm terminations and a loaded end Q of only 4,200. LTspice nominal results (2 identical runs each, `out/n*_*.log`, LTspice 26.0.2 for MacOS):

Confidence: High for the arithmetic (checked two ways); Medium for the nominal Cm and Rs.

**F8. Monte Carlo results (200 runs per case, LTspice 26.0.2 for MacOS, `out/mc_*.json`, plots `figures/cw-selectivity/mc_*.png`).** Spread model per crystal: frequency error uniform +/-30 ppm (catalogue), uniform +/-10 ppm (Abracon code 1) or Gaussian sigma 4 ppm truncated at +/-15 ppm (same-lot, from the Land Boards batch); Cm Gaussian sigma 3 % truncated +/-10 %; Rs uniform 8 to 30 ohm; C0 uniform 3.5 to 5.5 pF; capacitors uniform +/-2 % (C0G), resistors +/-1 %. Reading the table: (a) with +/-30 ppm crystals the 500 Hz 4-pole bandwidth is a lottery from 380 to 630 Hz (5th to 95th percentile) and the centre wanders +/-150 Hz, so one unit in seven meets the strict TBR and one in two the relaxed one; (b) the 6-pole never meets the strict criterion because its median loss is 12 dB, and its loss, not its bandwidth, is what fails; (c) tight +/-10 ppm crystals or a single reel remove the bandwidth problem (449 to 514 Hz) and the ripple problem (0 dB) but leave the loss problem: half the units exceed 8 dB because Rs ranged 8 to 30 ohm; (d) **a 1.0 kHz 4-pole ladder is tolerance-proof with ordinary +/-30 ppm crystals (98.5 % strict yield, loss 3.5 to 6.6 dB)**, and (e) **a 2.4 kHz 4-pole ladder is completely tolerance-proof (100 %, loss 1.6 to 2.8 dB, ripple <= 0.33 dB)**. True passband splitting (peak-to-valley ripple) is rare in every case; the dominant effects are bandwidth scaling with Cm, centre shift with frequency error, and loss with Rs. In the nominal figures (`n4_540_rs15.png`) the passband is a smooth dome and the 60 dB skirts are asymmetric (upper side steeper, e.g. 67 dB at +2 kHz versus 60 dB at -2 kHz for the 4-pole), the classic ladder shape. Confidence: High that the simulation reproduces the model; Medium for the spread assumptions (Cm sigma 3 % and Rs 8 to 30 ohm are informed estimates, not catalogue guarantees).

**F9. Limits of the model.** No PCB strays, no leakage around the filter and no ground-return coupling are modelled, so the simulated attenuation beyond +/-5 kHz (90 to 107 dB) is not physical; well-built ladders reach 70 to 90 dB, commercial cans specify 60 dB at 2 to 3 bandwidths and 100 dB ultimate (F2). Temperature is not modelled (+/-50 ppm over -10 to +70 C moves all crystals together by up to 450 Hz at 9 MHz; the BFO must follow, F5). The same-lot case assumes PCBWay places all four or six crystals from one reel, which is likely but not guaranteed. With N = 200 the yield estimates carry about +/-7 percentage points (95 % interval at p = 0.5). LTspice's `flat()` and `gauss()` are pseudo-random; runs are repeatable only if `.options seed` is set (the batch does not set it). Confidence: High.

### D. Option 3: wider IF filter plus DSP on the RP2350

**F10. Wide IF filter candidates that survive turnkey assembly.** (a) The tolerance-designed 4-pole 2.4 kHz or 1.0 kHz ladders of F8 (four SMD HC-49/US crystals, six C0G capacitors, two resistors). (b) ECS 10.7 MHz monolithic crystal filters (ecsxtal `mono crystal filters.pdf`): ECS-10.7-7.5A (2 poles, 3 dB passband +/-3.75 kHz, ripple 0.5 dB, insertion loss 1.5 dB max, 20 dB stopband at +/-18 kHz, 1800 ohm // 5.0 pF, HC-49/U), -7.5B (4 poles, ripple 1.0 dB, 2.5 dB loss, 40 dB at +/-14 kHz, 1800 // 4.0, two HC-49/U), -7.5C (6 poles, 45 dB at +/-8.75 kHz, 65 dB at +/-12.5 kHz, SC-3), -7.5D (8 poles, 65 dB at +/-8.75, 90 dB at +/-12.5, SC-4); the 15 kHz family (ECS-10.7-15A/B/C/D, 3000 ohm) is similar. DigiKey search snippets say the 7.5A, 15A and 15B are in stock and the 7.5B shows 599 units (undated snippets, Low). These are through-hole HC-49/U cans, so PCBWay THT assembly applies; 7.5 kHz at 3 dB is 15 times wider than the CW channel and leaves the ADC and AGC exposed to everything within +/-4 kHz. (c) Murata 455 kHz ceramic filters are still in the CERAFIL P05E catalogue: CFWLB455KJFA-B0 (6 dB at fn +/-2.0 kHz min, 50 dB at fn +/-7.0 kHz max, stopband 55 dB min within fn +/-100 kHz, loss 7.0 dB max, 4 elements, order multiple 200), CFWLB455KHFA-B0 (+/-3.0 kHz, 55 dB, 6.0 dB), CFULA455KH1A-B0 (+/-3.0 kHz, 35 dB, 6.0 dB, 2000 per order); one distributor (hesta.pl) showed the KHFA at $12.00 with 76 in stock on 2026-09-25 about 12:10. A 455 kHz IF needs a second conversion (another mixer and LO output) and the Murata site is JavaScript-only, so lifecycle could not be read; rejected on complexity. (d) KVG XF-9B01/02-LF 2.4 kHz (quote, F2). Confidence: High for datasheet numbers; Low for stock.

**F11. RP2350 internal ADC: 9.5 effective bits and no INL/DNL specification yet.** RP2350 datasheet (local copy, build 2025-02-20) Table 1437: ENOB minimum 9, typical 9.5 bits; resolved bits 12; input impedance 100 kohm minimum; 500 kS/s from a 48 MHz `clk_adc` at 96 cycles per sample; section 12.4.1: the RP2040's DNL spikes at codes 0x200, 0x600, 0xa00 and 0xe00 (erratum RP2040-E11) are removed, "improving the ADC's precision by around 0.5 ENOB"; section 12.4.5 "INL and DNL: Details to follow" (no specification). Raspberry Pi forum thread "ADC DNL on Pico2": a Raspberry Pi engineer (jdb) states the INL/DNL is within specification for ENOB > 10; user gmx (1 million samples with injected noise) found 1 to 2 LSB errors at the former hole codes and estimated "10-11 ENOB in that big hole, and better anywhere else"; user hippy estimated "around 9 ENOB" from values sticking at 0x5FF and 0x7FF. On the Pico 2 module the reference is the filtered 3V3 rail (rustos toolchain report), which adds reference noise. Derived: SINAD = 6.02 ENOB + 1.76 = 59 dB at 9.5 bits over the Nyquist band; sampling I and Q alternately at 500 kS/s gives 250 kS/s per channel and a processing gain of 10 log10(125 kHz / 500 Hz) = 24 dB into a 500 Hz channel, so 83 dB if the noise were white and the ADC dithered, 76 dB at 48 kS/s. Practice: the Pi Pico Rx (101 Things, RP2040 ADC at 500 kS/s round-robin I/Q, CIC decimation, all-software AGC, 150 Hz CW bandwidth) claims 72 dB from 12 bits plus 20 dB for an oversampling ratio of 100 but states "the ADC has internal noise of about 20 dB" and that "an MDS of a little better than -100 dBm might be a more realistic figure" with an input range of -78 dBm to +14 dBm and no RF amplifier. **Honest estimate for cwht: 60 to 70 dB of in-channel dynamic range with the RP2350 ADC in a real board, with DNL artefacts appearing as discrete spurs rather than white noise.** Confidence: High for the datasheet, Medium for the forum figures, Low for the practical estimate.

**F12. External I2S ADC and codec options.** PCM1808 (TI, status ACTIVE): 24-bit delta-sigma stereo ADC, SNR 99 dB, dynamic range 99 dB, THD+N -93 dB, 8 to 96 kHz, I2S or left-justified, master or slave, single-ended 3 Vpp input with on-chip anti-alias low-pass and 0.91 Hz high-pass, 5 V analog and 3.3 V digital, ICC 8.6 mA typ (11 max) and IDD 5.9 mA typ (8 max) at 48 kHz, **power 62 mW typ, 81 mW max**, 14-TSSOP. Stock and price: LCSC C55513 showed **28,195 in stock at $0.7084 (qty 1), $0.5687 (10), $0.4274 (100), $0.337 (1000)** on 2026-09-25 about 12:05; the TI store was out of stock at the same time; a DigiKey search snippet says $1.81 with 4,497 in stock (undated, Low). **F13. AGC pumping: mechanism and the architecture consequence.** KB6NU quoting W3PFR: pumping occurs when a strong signal "gets through to the DSP front end and will reduce the gain of the receiver because the Hardware AGC is being activated by the strongest signal" inside the roofing filter but outside the DSP filter; "Using a more narrow roofing filter can eliminate this unwanted signal and eliminate the AGC pumping." The FTDX101D user group reports the same effect with signals inside the crystal filter but outside the DSP bandwidth. The textbook cure is the hybrid loop of US patent 5451948 (front-end AGC only to keep the ADC out of overload, back-end digital AGC after the DSP filter, with the front-end gain changes fed forward through a delay); G0UPL's QCX paper notes that AGC "at audio" is harder than IF-derived AGC, and the QMX avoids analog AGC entirely by using a 112 dB ADC and floating-point DSP. Consequence for cwht (derived): the strongest signal inside the roofing bandwidth must stay below the ADC full scale without any gain change, and the wanted signal at MDS must stay above the ADC noise in 500 Hz. With a 24-bit ADC (95 dB practical) the fixed-gain window spans -142 dBm to about -47 dBm at the antenna, which covers every scenario of F1 beyond 3 km and needs only a manual or slow 20 dB step attenuator for a friend closer than 1 km; with the RP2350 ADC (65 dB) the window ends near -77 dBm, so an analog AGC ahead of the ADC is unavoidable and every strong signal inside the roofing passband pumps the audio. Narrowing the roofing filter to 1.0 kHz (F8) instead of 2.4 kHz halves the exposure but does not remove it. Confidence: High for the mechanism; Medium for the numbers.

**F14. Firmware effort and emulation coverage for I2S plus DSP on rustos.** RP2350 PIO I2S receive is proven on the RP2040 by community code (malacalypse/rp2040_i2s_example: `i2s_sck`, `i2s_out_master` and `i2s_in_slave` PIO programs with double-buffered DMA, archived; forks flpvsk and alejzeis; a `rp2040-i2s` crate on crates.io) and by pico-extras for output. rustos today has no PIO, DMA or ADC driver and no ICD for them (rustos toolchain report, open item 4), so candidate B adds three new drivers plus the DSP chain (decimation, CW band-pass, digital AGC, sidetone mixing, audio output by PWM or I2S DAC). The QMX runs 48 kS/s I/Q DSP in floating point with IIR filters on a Cortex-M4F at 168 MHz (G0UPL FDIM paper); the RP2350's Cortex-M33 at 150 MHz with FPU is of the same class, and the DSP itself is pure Rust that runs identically in host unit tests with recorded IF audio. Emulation: rp2350js models PIO, DMA and the ADC (emulation report F12) but its I2S timing fidelity is unverified; the PIO program can be validated on the host only by a PIO simulator (open item). Confidence: Medium.

### E. Option 4: analog op-amp audio filter

**F15. Hi-Per-Mite (NM0S, 4SQRP) as used in the QCX.** Manual: centre 700 Hz, 3 dB bandwidth 200 Hz, gain 0 to 50 dB selectable, 5 to 13 V DC at **< 15 mA**; "three low-pass filters, and one high pass filter. Each filter section is fairly low-Q, and the frequencies of resonance are staggered" (all Sallen-Key; the schematic gives LP F0 = 700 Hz, Q = 3.15 and HP F0 = 1802 Hz, Q = 4.42); "narrow-bandwidth CW filtering without objectionable ringing". QCX (G0UPL FDIM 2018): the same design with a measured 18 dB gain, LM4562 dual op-amps (2.7 nV/sqrt(Hz)), sidetone injected before the filter so the square wave is cleaned to a sine, no AGC at all. Ultimate rejection is not specified by either source; four staggered second-order sections give of the order of 40 to 60 dB at twice the bandwidth (estimate, Low). Limits: it filters after the detector, so nothing protects the mixer, the IF amplifier or any AGC from adjacent signals; 5 % capacitors move the centre by about 5 %; the bandwidth is fixed by parts. Confidence: High for the quoted specifications.

### F. Option 5: direct conversion or low-IF I/Q with software filtering

**F16. I/Q directly at 144 MHz is the wrong place.** G4JNT's 144 MHz DC receiver reached 20 to 25 dB opposite-sideband rejection with a passive quadrature hybrid (reference report F12); the Si5351's phase-offset quadrature stops near 110 MHz (reference report F16); an FST3253 QSD needs quadrature clocks at the RF frequency, which the SoftRock designers avoided at VHF. An integrated demodulator such as the LT5517 (40 to 900 MHz, 4.5 to 5.25 V at 90 mA, NF 9.5 dB at 200 MHz, IIP3 +23.5 dBm, I/Q mismatch 0.3 dB and 0.7 degrees, $11.67 at 1k list) needs a 2 x LO input at 288 MHz (beyond the Si5351's 200 MHz) and its mismatch limits image rejection to about 35 dB (derived from 20 log10 of the vector error), at 90 mA. Rejected. Confidence: High.

**F17. The SoftRock Ensemble RX II VHF pattern maps onto one Si5351 PLL with no crystals at all.** WB5RVZ builders' notes: RF amplifier, diode mixer with the Si570 at 115.2 MHz, 28.8 MHz IF, quadrature clock generator dividing the LO by 4 (74AC74) to clock an FST3253 QSD with 0.047 uF sampling capacitors; centre = f(Si570) +/- f(Si570)/4; example 145.200 MHz wanted, 29.05 MHz into the QSD, 29.04 MHz quadrature clocks, 10 kHz I/Q output. For cwht (derived): PLL A VCO = 6 fLO = 24 fIF, fLO = 0.8 fRF = 115.2 to 118.4 MHz on CLK0 (even divider 6, VCO 691.2 to 710.4 MHz inside 600 to 900 MHz), fIF = 28.8 to 29.6 MHz on CLK1 and CLK2 with divider 24 and CLKx_PHOFF = 24 for 90 degrees (each LSB is a quarter VCO period and the MultiSynth must be in fractional mode, AN619 and PY2OHH), no flip-flop divider needed; the mixer image sits at 0.75 fLO = 86.4 to 88.8 MHz, 57 MHz below the wanted signal, for the 144 MHz band-pass filter to kill; the opposite-sideband image of a CW signal lies 2 x 700 Hz = 1.4 kHz away and is suppressed only by I/Q balance (30 to 40 dB in hardware; QMX-style software correction adds an unverified 10 to 20 dB). The whole selectivity is then DSP and needs the 24-bit ADC of F12 for the reasons in F13. Confidence: High for the SoftRock facts; Medium for the Si5351 mapping (untested).

## Implications for cwht

Tags: REQ-candidate, RISK-candidate, DECISION-needed, ACTION.

1. **REQ-candidate (L1 selectivity, TBR):** The receiver shall provide a CW passband of 500 Hz (+100 / -50 Hz) at -6 dB [TBR], a -60 dB bandwidth of at most 2.5 kHz [TBR], attenuation of at least 60 dB at +/-2 kHz from the passband centre and at least 80 dB beyond +/-5 kHz [TBR], and passband ripple of at most 2 dB [TBR]. Basis: F2 (KVG XF-9NB 60 dB at +/-1.1 kHz, ultimate > 100 dB), F3 (Inrad shape 2.0), F8 (6-pole ladder 2.4 kHz at -60 dB; 4-pole 3.7 kHz fails the -60 dB number), F1 (85 dB reciprocal-mixing bound with a Si5351 LO). Verification: Analysis (LTspice or DSP filter response), Bench (NanoVNA sweep of the IF filter; audio sweep of the DSP filter through the USB sound path), OnAir.
2. **REQ-candidate (L1 MDS, TBR):** MDS at most -140 dBm in 500 Hz (NF <= 7 dB) with a target of -142 dBm (NF 5 dB) [TBR]. Basis: F1, reference report F14. Verification: Analysis (noise budget), Bench (calibrated signal source is not on the owner's bench; use a known-level source or accept OnAir comparison with a reference receiver).
3. **REQ-candidate (L1 adjacent-channel dynamic range, TBR):** A signal of -60 dBm at +/-2 kHz [TBR] shall not reduce the audio output of a -130 dBm wanted signal by more than 3 dB and shall not produce audible AGC pumping. Basis: F1 (friend at 5 km is -53 dBm), F13. Verification: Analysis plus OnAir demonstration (a two-signal bench test needs two generators the owner does not have).
4. **REQ-candidate (BFO and centre calibration):** The BFO offset shall be adjustable over at least +/-500 Hz in steps of at most 10 Hz and stored per unit, because the filter centre is uncertain by +/-150 Hz (ladders, F8), by the marked-versus-series-resonance offset of about 2 kHz (F5) and by temperature (F9); commercial cans also carry a centre tolerance. Verification: Demonstration.
5. **REQ-candidate (for the DSP candidate):** The audio ADC shall provide at least 90 dB of in-channel dynamic range in 500 Hz [TBR] and the analog gain ahead of it shall be fixed (no AGC before the ADC), with AGC applied after the CW filter in firmware. Basis: F11 to F13.
6. **RISK-candidate (500 Hz ladder from catalogue crystals, high):** Monte Carlo yield of 14.5 % (strict) to 51 % (relaxed) with +/-30 ppm crystals and 0 to 11.5 % for a 6-pole; even +/-10 ppm or same-reel crystals leave half the units above 8 dB insertion loss because catalogue ESR is bounded only at 60 ohm (F5, F7, F8). Mitigation: do not baseline a 500 Hz ladder; if a ladder is used at all, make it 1.0 kHz or wider (F8) and put the 500 Hz selectivity in DSP.
7. **RISK-candidate (commercial filter supply, medium):** KVG's current sheet has no 500 Hz 9 MHz type and is quote-only; Inrad shows one discontinued item and no stock information; both are single-source through-hole cans (F2, F3). Mitigation: request quotes now (lead time and MOQ), buy the project quantity before CDR, and keep the PCB footprint compatible with both BF-01 (KVG) and the Inrad case (dimensions to be obtained).
8. **RISK-candidate (RP2350 ADC as audio sampler, high):** 9.5 typical / 9 minimum ENOB, no INL/DNL specification, hole artefacts at fixed codes, noisy Pico 2 reference; practical 60 to 70 dB in-channel dynamic range forces analog AGC ahead of the ADC and therefore pumping (F11, F13). Mitigation: external 24-bit I2S ADC (PCM1808, F12) or keep the RP2350 ADC for battery and volume only.
9. **RISK-candidate (codec availability, medium):** TI store out of stock for PCM1808 and TLV320AIC3204 on 2026-09-25; WM8960 is EOL. Mitigation: PCM1808 is deeply stocked at LCSC (28,195) and reported at DigiKey; order with the PCBWay kit; second source an equivalent (PCM1802 or TLV320ADC3xxx) at PDR.
10. **RISK-candidate (firmware scope, medium):** Candidate B adds PIO, DMA and I2S drivers to rustos plus a DSP chain and digital AGC; emulator fidelity for PIO I2S is unverified (F14). Mitigation: DSP as pure host-tested Rust; PIO program validated on hardware early with a loopback; keep candidate A's analog path as the fallback design.
11. **RISK-candidate (lot-to-lot Cm, medium, any ladder):** a 20 % Cm shift moves the bandwidth 20 % (F7). Mitigation: design bandwidth 1.0 kHz or wider so that +/-20 % still covers the DSP passband; record the crystal lot in the as-built record; measure one filter with the NanoVNA at receipt.
12. **DECISION-needed (PDR trade study, selectivity architecture).** Recommendation: carry two candidates. **Candidate A, "classic superhet":** single conversion to 9.0106 MHz (or 9.000 MHz), Inrad #111 400 Hz 8-pole (or KVG XF-90S52-LF 1.0 kHz on quote) between a low-noise post-mixer amplifier and an IF amplifier with analog AGC, product detector with the Si5351 as BFO, optional Hi-Per-Mite-class audio filter for a 250 Hz "narrow" position; the RP2350 does keyer, sidetone, BFO and display only. **Candidate B, "roofed SDR back end":** the same front end and mixer into a tolerance-designed 4-pole 1.0 kHz (or 2.4 kHz) ladder from +/-30 ppm SMD crystals, fixed-gain IF amplifier, product detector, PCM1808 at 48 kS/s over PIO I2S, DSP band-pass 250/500/1000 Hz with digital AGC and sidetone mixing, audio out by PWM or I2S DAC. Numbers for the trade: A gives 400 Hz at -6 dB and 800 Hz at -60 dB for $118 and zero firmware; B gives selectable bandwidth, a 95 dB fixed-gain window and a 100 % assembly yield for about $1 of crystals plus $1 of ADC, at the cost of three drivers and a DSP chain. Not recommended for the first build: a 500 Hz ladder (implication 6), the RP2350 ADC as the audio sampler (8), audio-only filtering (F15) and any I/Q architecture (F16, F17); the SoftRock-VHF I/Q pattern is the documented fallback if crystals or cans become unobtainable.

Recommendation: 1.0 kHz, because it halves the AGC exposure and its loss is still moderate; it is also the bandwidth KVG chose for its current 9 MHz CW standard.

Recommendation: keep the IF a Si5351 parameter and choose it with the filter purchase; the LO plan (reference report implication 13) is unaffected within this range.
