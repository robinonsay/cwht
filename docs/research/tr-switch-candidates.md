# T/R switching candidates for 5 W CW at 144 MHz (assignment: tr-switch)

**Status:** research report for the PDR T/R trade study; not a baseline. **Date:** 2026-09-25. **Author:** Claude (research subagent, independent invocation). **Owner:** Robin.

Builds on `2m-cw-transceiver-reference-designs.md` Findings F27 to F29 and Table 4 (T/R elements, keyer timing) and on `keyer-and-key-interfaces.md` F9 and F10 (break-in modes, hang times, 5 ms envelope). Stakeholder inputs that constrain this study: SI-003 (5 W), SI-009 and SI-028 (parts readily sourced from DigiKey, Mouser or PCBWay's distributors), SI-018 (straight key and iambic paddles), SI-023 (2S Li-ion, 6.0 to 8.4 V), SI-031 (PCBWay places all SMT; owner hand-solders THT only), SI-033 (keyer 5 to 50 WPM), SI-034 (battery life 8 h at 1:9 TX:RX; tinySA Ultra will be bought), SI-035 (full QSK versus semi break-in is an open owner decision).

## Question

For 5 W CW at 144.000 to 148.000 MHz, identify transmit/receive switching solutions PCBWay can assemble: (a) at least two relays with a stated carry rating of 10 W or more at 150 MHz, with switching time and coil current, with distributor stock and price dated; (b) at least two PIN diode solutions rated for the 22 V peak / 0.45 A peak RF of 5 W, with the bias current and supply they need, the quarter-wave or lumped-equivalent receiver-protection topology at 144 MHz and diode part numbers with datasheets; (c) a GaAs or SOI RF switch IC if any handles 5 W at 144 MHz. For each: insertion loss, isolation, switching time, receiver protection, sequencing needs, full QSK at 50 WPM or semi break-in only, and an LTspice modelling note. Recommend a rev A baseline and state the owner's full-QSK versus semi-break-in decision with its consequences.

## Findings

### A. Electrical stress at 5 W and derived design numbers

**F1. RF stress.** 5 W into 50 ohm: 15.8 V RMS, 22.4 V peak, 0.316 A RMS, 0.447 A peak. Standing-wave maxima with a mismatched antenna: VSWR 2, 29.8 V and 0.60 A peak; VSWR 3, 33.5 V and 0.67 A peak; open or short, 44.7 V and 0.89 A peak (V or I doubles). A quarter-wave line at 144 MHz is 521 mm in air (about 290 mm on FR-4 microstrip), so only the lumped pi equivalent is practical (Skyworks 200312E Figure 15): L = Z0/(2 pi f) = 55.3 nH (use 56 nH), C = 1/(2 pi f Z0) = 22.1 pF (use 22 pF, both shunt legs). Source for the equations: Skyworks 200312E equations (1), (14) to (16), (24) to (29).

**F2. Stored-charge criterion corrects prior F27.** Skyworks equation (1): the stored charge Q = I_F x tau must be much larger than I_RF/(2 pi f). At 144 MHz with I_pk = 0.447 A the minimum DC bias is 0.32 mA for tau = 1.55 us, 0.49 mA for 1.0 us, 0.99 mA for 0.5 us, 1.24 mA for 0.4 us (SMP1322 class) and 4.9 mA for 0.1 us. The prior report's "IF max 100 mA vs 0.45 A peak RF" comparison is therefore not the governing limit: in a forward-biased PIN the RF current may exceed the DC bias by orders of magnitude as long as the stored charge is not depleted. The governing limits are (i) dissipation, I_RMS^2 x R_S = 50 mW at 0.5 ohm, 100 mW at 1.0 ohm, 200 mW at 2.0 ohm, plus the DC bias power (about 0.8 to 1.0 V x I_F), and (ii) the R_S obtained at the chosen bias (loss and isolation). Skyworks' own worked example (200312E, equation 29, P_A = 12.5 P_D / R_S for a totally mismatched antenna): the 0.25 W SMP1322-011LF quarter-wave switch "may then be computed to handle 6.25 W with a totally mismatched antenna". In the quarter-wave topology no diode holds off the transmitter voltage (both are forward biased in TX), so the 22 V peak requirement becomes a current and dissipation requirement, and the reverse-voltage rating matters only for fault cases (bias lost while the PA is on: up to 44.7 V peak).

**F3. Receiver damage threshold.** Mini-Circuits PSA4-5043+ (the G4DDK Anglian LNA family from the prior report) absolute maximum input power: +17 dBm continuous, +23 dBm for 5 minutes maximum (datasheet, "Permanent damage may occur if any of these limits are exceeded"). Leakage at the LNA = 37 dBm minus isolation: 20 dB gives +17 dBm (at the limit), 30 dB gives +7 dBm, 40 dB gives -3 dBm, 60 dB gives -23 dBm. Design target derived here: at least 40 dB TX-to-LNA isolation, or at least 30 dB plus the LNA supply switched off during TX.

### B. Timing envelope from the keyer (SI-018, SI-033)

**F4. Listening window in full QSK.** With a 5 ms raised-cosine fall (keyer report F10) and a T/R switching time t_sw each way, the receiver hears (dit - 5 ms - 2 t_sw) of each inter-element gap:

| Speed | dit | t_sw 0.01 ms (PIN, MEMS, PIN IC) | t_sw 1 ms | t_sw 3 ms (HF3 typ.) | t_sw 5 ms (HF3 max) | t_sw 10 ms (G6Z, ARS max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 WPM | 48 ms | 43 ms (90 %) | 41 ms (85 %) | 37 ms (77 %) | 33 ms (69 %) | 23 ms (48 %) |
| 40 WPM | 30 ms | 25 ms (83 %) | 23 ms (77 %) | 19 ms (63 %) | 15 ms (50 %) | none |
| 50 WPM | 24 ms | 19 ms (79 %) | 17 ms (71 %) | 13 ms (54 %) | 9 ms (38 %) | none |

Relay operate time plus bounce is also a key-down to RF lead: the sequencer must hold the envelope until the contacts have settled (HF3: 5 ms + 3 ms max; G6Z and ARS: 10 ms + bounce, bounce not specified). For a straight key this is a fixed lead-in the operator feels; Elecraft's KX2 allows up to 20 ms but warns that "longer delays can add some timing variation in CW mode at higher code speeds" (keyer report F9). For paddles the keyer can hide the lead-in inside the element pipeline (WinKeyer "first extension"). Semi break-in hang of 8 dits (QRP Labs "Auto") is 384 ms at 25 WPM and 192 ms at 50 WPM.

**F5. Relay life under full QSK.** PARIS has 14 elements per word, so element rate = 14 x WPM / 60: 3.5 per s at 15 WPM, 5.8 at 25, 9.3 at 40, 11.7 at 50. Full QSK cycles the relay once per element: 12,600 / 21,000 / 33,600 / 42,000 cycles per hour of keying. Against mechanical life ratings (F6 to F9): Omron G6Z 1e6 gives 79 / 48 / 30 / 24 hours of keying; TE HF3 1e7 gives 794 / 476 / 298 / 238 hours; Panasonic ARS (standard) 5e6 gives about half the HF3 figures. At the owner's duty (SI-034: 1:9, 8 h a day, so 0.8 h keying per day) the G6Z would reach its rating in about 60 days of daily use at 25 WPM; the HF3 in about 600 days. In semi break-in the relay cycles once per transmission burst (say 30 per minute, 1,800 per hour): G6Z 1e6 gives 556 hours, HF3 1e7 gives 5,556 hours of keying. Sources: element arithmetic (python3), life figures from the datasheets cited below.

### D. PIN diode solutions

**F13. Topology: the quarter-wave switch is the only PIN topology that fits a 6.0 to 8.4 V rig.** Skyworks 200312E describes two antenna-switch families. (a) Quarter-wave switch (Figure 16, lumped version 16B): series diode D1 between PA and antenna node, shunt diode D2 at the receiver end of a quarter-wave (or 56 nH / 22 pF pi) section from the antenna node. "When switched into the transmit state, each diode becomes forward biased. The series diode appears as a low impedance to the signal heading toward the antenna, and the shunt diode effectively shorts the antenna terminals of the receiver"; "the RF current in both the series and shunt diode is practically identical"; "If RS is 1 ohm greater than 30 dB isolation and less than 0.2 dB insertion loss can be expected. This performance is achievable over a 10 percent bandwidth" (144 to 148 MHz is 2.7 %). "In the receive condition, the diodes are at zero or reverse bias." No diode holds off the transmitter voltage; D1 "need not have a high voltage rating since it is always forward biased in its low resistance state when high RF power is applied". (b) Broadband series-series switch (Figure 17): the receive-side diode must hold off the TX voltage: "In high power applications (> 5 W), it is often necessary to apply reverse voltage on D2 during transmit", that is a rail above the 22.4 V (44.7 V mismatched) peak, which cwht does not have. The same applies inside integrated series-shunt SPDTs (F20). Precedents: Yaesu FT-817 2 m at 5 W uses PIN diodes D3003 and D3005 (HSU277) as series isolation switches and D3035 as the shunt that "prevents TX power from getting past the quarter-wave network consisting of L3022 and related components" (KA7OEI circuit description); Elecraft KX3, 10 W CW: "Fast, silent, PIN-diode T-R switching - no relays" (Elecraft product page). This report therefore evaluates only quarter-wave (lumped) PIN solutions.

**F14. Solution P1 (baseline candidate): Skyworks SMP1322 series diode plus SMP1302-085LF shunt diode.**
- D1 series: SMP1322-011LF (SOD-323) or the SMP1322-016LF pair (two diodes in SOT-143, "specifically designed for low-current drain antenna T/R switches", datasheet Figure 1 is a quarter-wave T/R switch). Data sheet 200049M: reverse voltage 50 V; power dissipation 250 mW at 25 C lead temperature; RS 1.5 ohm max at 1 mA, 0.5 ohm typ at 10 mA (100 MHz); CT 1 pF max at 30 V (1.15 pF for -016/-017); VF 0.85 V at 10 mA; carrier lifetime 0.4 us; I-region 8 um; ESD class 1B; MSL1. Skyworks computes 6.25 W into a totally mismatched antenna for this diode in this topology (F2).
- D2 shunt: SMP1302-085LF (2 x 2 mm QFN with exposed paddle, BRO399): VR 200 V; IF 200 mA; CW dissipation 3 W (30 W for 1 us); RS 1.0 ohm typ / 1.5 max at 100 mA, 3 ohm max at 10 mA (100 MHz); CT 0.3 pF max at 30 V; carrier lifetime 700 ns typ; theta_JC 40 C/W; rated 50 W CW as a shunt element with adequate paddle heat-sinking. Smaller SMP1302 packages (-001LF SOT-23, -079LF SC-79) are the Skyworks example parts for "> 25 dB receiver isolation up to 400 MHz".
- Bias: 20 to 50 mA per diode (stored-charge margin at 50 mA: 40x for D1, 70x for D2), fed in series (D1 then D2, about 2 V total drop) from the pack through a PNP current source or a 80 to 130 ohm resistor; TX bias power 0.3 to 0.42 W at 50 mA; receive state 0 V (RFCs return both diodes to ground); no negative or high-voltage rail. Battery cost at the SI-034 duty: 5 mA average, about 4 % of the PA's average draw.
- Dissipation at 5 W (LTspice F17): 76 mW in D1 and 117 mW in D2 including DC bias, against 250 mW and 3 W ratings; at VSWR 3 the currents rise 1.5x and the RF part of the dissipation 2.2x (D1 about 150 mW, still inside its rating at 25 C lead temperature, Low margin at 60 C).
- Isolation and loss (Skyworks and LTspice): 0.09 to 0.17 dB TX loss, 31 to 40 dB isolation for RS2 1.5 to 0.5 ohm, 0.03 dB RX loss; single-stage leakage at the receiver port +2.6 dBm (F17), which is inside the LNA's +17 dBm limit but with only 14 dB margin, so a second shunt stage is specified (F19).
- Switching time: driver limited; the diodes themselves switch in about 1 to 3 carrier lifetimes (1 to 2 us) when reverse-pulled; less than 10 us total is realistic. Full QSK at 50 WPM: yes.
- Package and assembly: all SMT (SOD-323, SOT-143, 2 x 2 QFN with hidden pad) placed by PCBWay; nothing for the owner to hand-solder (SI-031).

**F15. Solution P2 (heavy, distortion-specified): MACOM MA4P1250-1072T MELF diodes (series and shunt).** MACOM MA4P MELF and HIPAX data sheet V16 ("designed for usage in switch and attenuator applications requiring high power handling and low distortion"): CT 0.8 pF typ / 1.2 max at 50 V; reverse voltage 50 V min; RS 0.5 ohm typ / 0.75 max at 50 mA and 100 MHz; RP 5 kohm min; VF 0.85 / 1.0 V at 50 mA; carrier lifetime 2 us min / 8 us typ; forward-bias harmonic distortion (2nd and 3rd) 80 dB min / 90 dB typ at PIN = 30 W, 100 MHz, IF = 50 mA; reverse-bias distortion 60 / 70 dB at 0 dBm; dissipation 6 W with no heat sink (15 C/W), 18 W on an infinite heat sink; leadless MELF (package 1072) on tape and reel, reflow compatible. **F17. LTspice results for the lumped quarter-wave switch (P1-class parameters).** Models: standard SPICE diode with TT set to the carrier lifetime and RS set to the datasheet RF resistance at the design bias: D1 `D(Is=1e-8 N=1.2 Rs=0.5 Cjo=1.5p Vj=0.6 M=0.35 TT=0.4u BV=50)`, D2 `D(Is=1e-8 N=1.2 Rs={0.5|1|1.5|3} Cjo=0.6p Vj=0.6 M=0.35 TT=0.7u BV=200)`; 56 nH / 22 pF pi section; 1 uH RF chokes; 1 nF DC blocks; 50 ohm PA, antenna and receiver ports; 50 mA bias per diode. Results (LTspice 26.0.2 for MacOS, 2026-09-25):
- TX state, small-signal, 144 MHz: TX to antenna insertion loss 0.086 / 0.127 / 0.167 / 0.281 dB and TX to receiver isolation 40.3 / 34.4 / 31.0 / 25.4 dB for RS2 = 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 3.0 ohm; at 148 MHz 0.089 dB and 40.5 dB (RS2 = 0.5 ohm), so the 4 MHz band is flat.
- RX state (both diodes 0 V, PA port terminated 50 ohm): antenna to receiver loss 0.027 dB at 144 MHz, 0.037 dB at 148 MHz; receive signal leaking into the PA port -22.6 dB (through D1's 1.5 pF zero-bias capacitance; the real off-state PA impedance must replace the 50 ohm termination in the design deck).
- TX state, large signal (44.72 V peak source behind 50 ohm, RS2 = 1 ohm): antenna 22.03 V peak, 4.85 W; receiver port 0.426 V peak = 1.8 mW = +2.6 dBm (34.3 dB below the antenna power, matching the AC result); D1 current +0.499 / -0.399 A, D2 +0.476 / -0.376 A (the 0.447 A RF swing rides on the 50 mA bias and the diodes conduct through the negative half cycle: D1 junction voltage never drops below +0.279 V, the stored-charge behaviour); D1 dissipation 75.5 mW, D2 117 mW (DC bias included).
- Harmonics reported by `.four`: below -130 dBc. This is an artefact: the standard SPICE diode with a large TT is nearly linear at RF and does not model PIN distortion; use vendor measured data (F18) and the tinySA instead.

**F18. Harmonic distortion of PIN switches and where the LPF must sit.** Measured vendor data: Skyworks SKY12208-478LF at +30 dBm TX drive, 5 V bias: 2nd harmonic -31 dBc at 50 MHz and -40 dBc at 250 MHz, 3rd -36 / -43 dBc; with 28 V bias -78 / -82 dBc and -92 / -85 dBc (data sheet 201671E Tables 4 and 5). Log-frequency interpolation to 144 MHz gives about -37 dBc (2nd) and -41 dBc (3rd) at 5 V bias and 1 W; at 5 W and low bias the relative levels degrade further (Low confidence extrapolation: roughly -30 dBc). MACOM MA4P1250-1072T: 80 dB min / 90 dB typ at 30 W, 100 MHz, 50 mA. Skyworks does not publish harmonic figures for the SMP1322 or SMP1302 in the documents fetched; the thin 8 um SMP1322 at low bias is the more distortion-prone element ("the thicker one generates less distortion", 200312E). Since 97.307(e) requires 53.0 dB harmonic suppression at 5 W (prior regulatory report F2), any PIN T/R element must be placed between the PA and the harmonic low-pass filter, with the LPF at the antenna port and therefore also in the receive path (0.3 to 0.5 dB extra RX loss, acceptable), or the switch element must be a relay or MEMS. 97.307(b) ("Emissions outside the necessary bandwidth must not cause splatter or keyclick interference", eCFR issue 2026-09-23) additionally forbids hot-switching transients, which the sequencing in F22 removes.

**F19. Receiver protection stack for the PIN options.** One shunt stage: 31 to 40 dB, leakage +2.6 to -3 dBm at the receiver port. Adding a second SMP1302-085LF shunt across the LNA input (a further quarter-wave or the LNA input match acting as the spacing) adds about 30 dB, or a limiter diode (Skyworks CLA series) clamps at about +10 dBm; with the LNA drain supply switched off during TX the device sees the leakage unpowered. Target: at least 40 dB margin below +17 dBm, that is leakage at or below -23 dBm, which two shunt stages achieve on paper (60 to 70 dB) and the NanoVNA can verify at low level (S21 of the assembled block in the TX-biased state).

### E. Switch ICs

**F20. GaAs or SOI FET switch ICs: none found rated for 37 dBm CW at 144 MHz.** pSemi PE42823 (UltraCMOS SOI SPDT): operating frequency 700 MHz to 6 GHz, max RF input power in TX mode 38.5 dBm CW, average input power 43 dBm (700 to 1800 MHz), P1dB 46 dBm, but not specified below 700 MHz (DOC-67417-4.02). Analog Devices ADRF5130: 0.7 to 3.8 GHz, 43 dBm max; HMC8038: 0.1 to 6 GHz but P0.1dB 35 dBm, below 37 dBm; Qorvo QPC2320: 0.02 to 15 GHz, 2 W. pSemi PE42723 (5 to 1794 MHz) is a cable-television switch with no 5 W rating found. Low-frequency power handling of FET switches is limited by the gate-bias network time constants and the off-arm voltage rating, which is why the vendors' low-frequency parts stop near 35 dBm. Conclusion (Medium confidence, search budget limited): no SOI or GaAs FET SPDT meets 5 W at 144 MHz; the integrated options are silicon PIN modules (F21) and MEMS (F22).

**F21. Skyworks SKY12208-478LF, silicon PIN SPDT in a 16-pin 4 x 4 x 1.5 mm QFN (data sheet 201671E, 29 Apr 2025).** Two bands by RF-choke value: 0.02 to 0.70 GHz or 0.05 to 2.7 GHz. Lifecycle: the data sheet carries no NRND note, but Mouser's hosted copy is named "SKY12208_478LF_201671E_discontinued.pdf" and the predecessor SKY12208-306LF is discontinued on Skyworks' site with -478LF named as its replacement; treat availability as at risk (Low confidence). ## LTspice modelling notes (per option)

**Relays (HF3, G6Z, ARS, G6Y).** No vendor SPICE models exist for these relays (TE, Omron and Panasonic publish plots only). Model the RF path as a voltage-controlled switch `.model RLY SW(Ron=0.1 Roff=1G Vt=2.5 Vh=0.1)` with 1 to 2 nH of series contact inductance; represent the open-contact isolation as a fixed series capacitor giving the datasheet number at 144 MHz (60 dB corresponds to 11 fF, 80 dB to 1.1 fF between 50 ohm ports), which is small enough that the relay is electrically ideal at 144 MHz. The useful relay model is temporal: drive the switch from a PWL source with the datasheet operate delay, a 1 to 3 ms bounce burst (alternating 0.1 ms segments) and the release delay, and run the T/R sequencer transient with the PA envelope to show the RF is off during every bounce interval (Analysis evidence for TR-02). Coil: series R and L (HF3 5 V: 178 ohm, inductance not published; measure with the NanoVNA or the bench supply step response) to size the driver and the clamp.

**PIN diodes (P1, P2, SKY12208).** Standard SPICE diode with `TT` = carrier lifetime reproduces the stored-charge behaviour (F17: diode stays forward biased through the -0.4 A half cycle). Its RF on-resistance equals `Rs` (the junction resistance is bypassed by the diffusion capacitance TT x I/(n Vt), about 150 nF at 10 mA), so set `Rs` to the datasheet RS at the design bias and `.step` it across the min/typ/max spread (0.5 to 3 ohm covers SMP1302 at 10 to 100 mA); set `Cjo`, `Vj`, `M` to reproduce CT at 0 V and at the receive-state reverse bias; `BV` from VR. Verify each model with a `.ac` deck at the bias point against the datasheet RS versus IF curve at 100 MHz before use. Skyworks and MACOM publish no SPICE decks in the documents fetched (the Skyworks app note gives the Rs, CT, RP, L equivalent circuit); Nexperia's SPICE model URL pattern for the BAP64-02 (assets.nexperia.com/documents/spice-model/BAP64-02.txt) exists but returned a bot challenge; Infineon's BAR64 family is EOL. Distortion is not predicted by this model (F17); harmonic compliance rests on LPF placement, vendor data and the tinySA measurement. The TX AC deck as run:

```
* cwht T/R PIN quarter-wave switch, TX state, small-signal AC 140-150 MHz (Skyworks Fig.16B topology, lumped lambda/4)
.param Rs1=0.5 Rs2=1.0 Ibias=50m Lq=56n Cq=22p
.model DSER D(Is=1e-8 N=1.2 Rs={Rs1} Cjo=1.5p Vj=0.6 M=0.35 TT=0.4u BV=50 Ibv=1u)
.model DSHN D(Is=1e-8 N=1.2 Rs={Rs2} Cjo=0.6p Vj=0.6 M=0.35 TT=0.7u BV=200 Ibv=1u)
Vtx tx0 0 AC 2
Rsrc tx0 tx 50
Cb1 tx txa 1n
D1 txa anta DSER
Cb2 anta ant 1n
Ib1 0 txa DC {Ibias}
Lrfc1 anta 0 1u
C1 ant 0 {Cq}
Lq ant rxn {Lq}
C2 rxn 0 {Cq}
D2 rxn 0 DSHN
Ib2 0 rxn DC {Ibias}
Cb3 rxn rx 1n
Rrx rx 0 50
Cb4 ant antp 1n
Rant antp 0 50
.step param Rs2 list 0.5 1 1.5 3
.ac lin 3 140Meg 148Meg
.meas AC iltx_ant_db FIND 20*log10(mag(V(antp))) AT 144Meg
.meas AC iso_tx_rx_db FIND 20*log10(mag(V(rx))) AT 144Meg
.meas AC iltx_148_db FIND 20*log10(mag(V(antp))) AT 148Meg
.meas AC iso_148_db FIND 20*log10(mag(V(rx))) AT 148Meg
.end
```

Reading the log: LTspice prints AC `.meas` results as (dB, phase) of the expression, so an expression that is already in dB is reported twice in dB; decode as value = 10^(printed/20) with 180 degrees meaning a negative value (for example "-21.336 dB, 180" is -0.086 dB). The RX-state deck swaps the source to the antenna port, sets both bias currents to zero and terminates the PA port in 50 ohm; the transient deck replaces the AC source with `SINE(0 44.72 144Meg)`, runs `.tran 0 2u 1.5u 0.05n` and measures V(antp), V(rx), I(D1), I(D2) and the diode powers with `.meas TRAN` plus `.four 144Meg 5 V(antp)`. The design decks must add: the real PA off-state impedance (LDMOS Coss plus output match) instead of 50 ohm, the chokes' self-resonance (1 uH ideal here), the LPF, the second shunt stage, and a `.step` of the antenna load over VSWR 1 to 3 at all phases (use a transmission-line stub) for the dissipation case.

**MEMS (MM5130).** Ideal switch with RON 0.6 ohm and COFF 15 fF plus a behavioural delay of 8.5 to 16 us on the gate command; no SPICE model found (Menlo provides measured S-parameters per the data sheet). The hot-switch rule is a sequencing check: in the sequencer transient, the switch command must occur only where the RF envelope is below 0.5 V peak.

## Implications for cwht

1. **REQ-candidate TR-01 (power and mismatch).** The T/R block shall pass 5.5 W CW (5 W plus 10 % ALC headroom) at 144.0 to 148.0 MHz into any load of VSWR up to 3:1 at any phase with every element within its datasheet ratings (peak 33.5 V, 0.67 A; relay carry 0.5 A RMS limit for G6Z and ARS is then marginal, F1, F6, F8). [Analysis (LTspice load sweep) + Bench (dummy load, tinySA)]
2. **REQ-candidate TR-02 (isolation and receiver protection).** TX-to-LNA-input isolation shall be at least 40 dB (leakage at or below -3 dBm) with the LNA supply removed during TX, and the LNA input shall never exceed +17 dBm under any single fault (bias lost, relay stuck) (F3, F19). [Analysis + Bench: NanoVNA S21 of the TX-biased block at low level]
3. **REQ-candidate TR-03 (cold switching and sequencing).** T/R state changes shall occur only while the RF envelope is below -20 dBc of full power (PIN, relay) or below 0.5 V peak (MEMS); key-down order: T/R to TX, wait t_op,max + t_bounce,max (HF3 8 ms; G6Z or ARS 11 ms; PIN or MEMS 20 us), PA bias on, raised-cosine rise; key-up order: envelope fall complete (5 ms), PA bias off, guard 0.2 ms (solid state) or 1 ms (relay), T/R to RX, receiver unmute after t_rel,max + bounce (F4, F12, F22; 97.307(b)). [Emulation of the sequencer + Bench]
4. **REQ-candidate TR-04 (fail-safe receive).** The unpowered state of the T/R element shall connect the antenna to the receiver (relay NC contact to RX; PIN and MEMS at zero bias in the receive configuration) so that a controller reset never leaves the PA connected to the LNA path with bias applied (F12, F13).
5. **REQ-candidate TR-05 (harmonic ordering).** If the T/R element is a PIN diode, it shall be located between the PA and the harmonic LPF, with the LPF at the antenna port and in the receive path; relay or MEMS elements may sit after the LPF (F18; 97.307(e) 53 dB).
6. **REQ-candidate TR-06 (supply).** The T/R element shall be driven from the 2S pack or the regulated rail without any additional rail above 12 V; average battery cost of T/R switching at the SI-034 duty shall be at or below 10 mA (PIN 5 mA, relay 3 to 4 mA at 10 % TX; a 28 V or 90 V rail would violate this unless justified) (F14, F21, F22).
7. **REQ-candidate TR-07 (assembly).** No T/R component shall require owner hand soldering of hidden pads; all T/R parts are SMT for PCBWay placement (SI-031); ESD class 1B (SMP1322) and class 0 (MM5130) parts shall be called out in the assembly notes.
8. **REQ-candidate TR-08 (break-in behaviour).** In full QSK the receiver shall be audible within 1 ms after the envelope fall plus switching time at every speed up to 50 WPM; in semi break-in the hang shall be selectable 6.1 or 8 dits or 1 to 999 ms and the first-element lead-in shall not exceed 12 ms (F4; keyer report SW-KEY-08).
9. **RISK-candidate (relay wear in QSK).** A relay used for full QSK reaches its mechanical rating in 24 to 79 hours (G6Z) or 238 to 794 hours (HF3) of keying (F5). Control: relays only in semi break-in, or solid-state element.
10. **RISK-candidate (lifecycle).** Panasonic ARE discontinued; Infineon BAR64/BAR63 EOL; NXP BAP64 no longer manufactured at NXP; SKY12208-478LF possibly discontinued (Mouser filename); Omron G6Z and G6Y lifecycle unverified (F6, F9, F10, F16, F21). Control: baseline only parts with verified active status (Skyworks SMP1322/SMP1302, MACOM MA4P1250, TE HF3) and record second sources in the BOM.
11. **RISK-candidate (PIN harmonics).** PIN switch distortion after the LPF would consume the 53 dB harmonic budget (F18). Control: TR-05, tinySA measurement (SI-034), P2 diodes if the margin is thin.
12. **RISK-candidate (single-stage leakage).** +2.6 dBm at the receiver port from one shunt stage leaves 14 dB to LNA damage and a bias-loss fault applies the full 22 to 45 V peak to the receive path (F17). Control: second shunt stage or limiter, LNA off in TX, bias supervised by the sequencer.
13. **RISK-candidate (MEMS hot switching and ESD).** MM5130 forbids switching above 0.5 V and is ESD class 0 (F22). Control: only with proven PA off-state leakage below +4 dBm and PCBWay ESD handling notes; not recommended for rev A.
14. **RISK-candidate (stock unverified).** No distributor stock or price could be observed in this session (Method 4). Control: Open item 1 before CDR procurement release (SI-020).
15. **DECISION-needed D-TR-1 (owner, SI-035): full QSK or semi break-in.** See the recommendation section for consequences.
16. **DECISION-needed D-TR-2: baseline element.** Recommendation: P1 (Skyworks SMP1322-016LF or -011LF plus SMP1302-085LF, two shunt stages) if QSK capability is wanted; TE HF3 54 (1-1462051-7) if the owner chooses semi break-in only.
17. **DECISION-needed D-TR-3: LPF position.** Recommendation: LPF at the antenna port after the T/R node (required for PIN, harmless for relay).
18. **DECISION-needed D-TR-4: second RX protection stage type.** Recommendation: second SMP1302-085LF shunt biased with the first; limiter diode as the alternative if board area is short.

## Recommendation for rev A

**Baseline: PIN quarter-wave switch P1.** Series diode Skyworks SMP1322-016LF (both diodes of the SOT-143 pair used as D1 and D2) or SMP1322-011LF (D1) with SMP1302-085LF as D2, a second SMP1302-085LF shunt at the LNA input, two lumped quarter-wave sections (56 nH, 22 pF, 22 pF each), 30 to 50 mA per diode from a PNP current source on the pack keyed by the sequencer, 0 V in receive, the harmonic LPF placed after the T/R node at the antenna connector, and the LNA supply switched off in transmit. Rationale: it is the only class of solution that supports iambic paddles at 50 WPM with full QSK (SI-018, SI-033); it is silent, has no wear-out mechanism, fails safe to receive, occupies a few square millimetres, uses parts that are active at Skyworks and cost in the low single digit USD class (unverified), follows Skyworks' own reference topology and the FT-817's production precedent at exactly 5 W on 2 m, and it needs no rail beyond the pack. Its design debts are explicit: LPF ordering (TR-05), the second protection stage (TR-02), and harmonic verification with the tinySA. If the PDR trade shows the receiver IMD or harmonic margin is thin, P2 (MACOM MA4P1250-1072T, 80 dB distortion specified) drops into the same footprint class with the same bias network at double the bias current.

**Fallback if the owner chooses semi break-in only or the PIN parts cannot be sourced by CDR:** TE Axicom HF3 54 (1-1462051-7, 6 V coil straight from the 2S pack, 23 mA, 50 W carry, 80 dB isolation, 1e7 operations, 3 to 5 ms) as an SPDT with the NC contact to the receiver; 8 ms lead-in, hang 8 dits. It is the only relay found that is both comfortably rated and hot-switch tolerant; the Omron G6Z (10 W exactly, 10 ms, 1e6, lifecycle unverified) and Panasonic ARS14A4H (10 W, 10 ms, needs the 5 V rail) are the second and third choices.

**The owner's decision (SI-035): full QSK versus semi break-in.**
- Full QSK means the receiver is live between every dit and dah: at 50 WPM the operator hears 19 ms out of each 24 ms gap (79 %); at 25 WPM 43 of 48 ms. It requires the solid-state switch (P1 or P2; SKY12208 or MEMS are not recommended for rev A), a receiver mute and AGC recovery inside about 1 ms after the envelope has fallen (firmware plus audio-path design), the second protection stage and the LPF ordering above, and 60 to 100 mA of diode bias during transmit (about 5 mA average on the battery, negligible against the PA). Its benefits are the ability to hear a station break in, no hang-time tuning, and silent operation. Once the hardware is QSK capable, semi break-in remains available as a firmware setting at no hardware cost.
- Semi break-in means the receiver returns only after a hang time (Auto 8 dits: 384 ms at 25 WPM, 192 ms at 50 WPM). It permits a relay: the simplest RF path (no bias network, no diode distortion, 80 dB isolation without a second stage), at the cost of a 15 x 7 x 10 mm body and 2.5 g inside a pocket radio, an audible click at every transmission start and end, an 8 to 12 ms first-element lead-in that a straight-key operator feels as a slight delay, and no receive between elements. Relay wear is not a concern in semi break-in (thousands of hours), and the relay path is immune to the PIN harmonic risk.
- Recommendation: choose QSK-capable hardware (P1) now and decide the default break-in mode in firmware later (keyer report D5). This keeps SI-018 fully served at 50 WPM, avoids a rev B respin if the owner later wants QSK, and costs less board area and mass than the relay. If the owner prefers the relay for its simplicity, accept that full QSK is then excluded for rev A and that the keyer must implement the lead-in and hang logic (semi only).
