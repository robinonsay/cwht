# Display and human-interface parts for cwht (display, encoders, volume, buttons, jacks, knobs, micro-USB wall access)

Assignment key: display-ui. Date of research: 2026-09-25 (observations time-stamped in CDT where they are perishable). Author: Claude (research agent), for PDR trade studies. Prior reports extended, not repeated: `pcbway-fabrication-and-assembly.md` (F15 to F18 assembly capabilities and sourcing), `keyer-and-key-interfaces.md` (F1 jack convention, CTL-KEY-01..06, D2, D11), `enclosure-cnc-and-openscad-pipeline.md` (A5, A6 wall and tolerance rules), `rustos-toolchain-proof.md` (LCD pin estimate, WP-08 SPI TX driver).

## 1. Question

Which human-interface parts should cwht baseline so that PCBWay can assemble the board (SI-009), the CNC aluminum enclosure can expose them (SI-008), the receiver is not degraded by display switching noise, and the radio is readable outdoors (SI-019): displays (Sharp memory LCD LS013B7DH03 and LS027B7DH01, ST7789 240x240 IPS TFT, SSD1306/SH1106 OLED, PCD8544, small e-paper) compared on current with and without backlight, sunlight readability, refresh, SPI pin count, connector type and PCBWay placeability, enclosure window, cost and dated availability, and RF-noise history; rotary encoders with push (Bourns PEC11R/PEC12R, Alps EC11) and their detent and pulse counts; volume as analog pot, digital pot or encoder; tactile buttons; 3.5 mm TRS jacks with switch contacts for key and headphones (Same Sky, formerly CUI Devices, and Switchcraft) that are PCB-mounted and panel-accessible; knobs; and micro-USB access through the enclosure wall (Pico 2 connector position and the opening needed). Deliver a baseline set with part numbers and the owner's decision list.

### B. RF-noise history of displays near a sensitive receiver

**F7. OLED (SSD1306/SH1106) is the documented offender; the mechanism is the on-module charge pump, and the coupling is often conducted.**
- uSDX SOTA build article (`https://antrak.org.tr/blog/usdx-sota-modular-all-mode-sdr-hf-transceiver-for-qrp-operations/`): "OLED displays are notoriously RF noisy due to their DC capacitive charge pumps which produces all kinds of spikes"; "this is an switching upverter which produces a lot of high frequency pulse which interferes with RX quality"; documented fix: remove the charge-pump capacitors (C3/C4 on the 128x64 module, C1/C2 on the 128x32) and feed the panel rail from an external 12 V DC: "By eliminating this charge pump we simply get rid off that unwanted interference by supplying 12VDC from TRX power input."
- Arduino forum, OLED versus a 433 MHz regenerative receiver (`https://forum.arduino.cc/t/oled-screen-interfering-with-rf-module/421913`): "When there is nothing displayed on the oled, the reception of RF is great. When there is 1 or 2 lines of text, the reception is good but apparently weakened"; moderator: "the way that interference is induced into your system looks like it is by conduction rather than radiation"; moving parts 50 cm apart helped little; decoupling and a star ground were the recommended fixes.
- (tr)uSDX field report (`https://qrper.com/2022/04/the-trusdx-five-band-qrp-transceiver-my-initial-impressions-after-a-cw-pota-activation/`): "the OLED display is very difficult to read outdoors. It's superb indoors, but outside any sunlight or reflection simply wipes it out."; the rig's noise floor "perhaps S5 or S6" is attributed to the audio chain, not the display.
- DL2MAN (tr)uSDX troubleshooting (`https://dl2man.de/troubleshooting/`): "OLED needs 100x to 1000x more Traffic (and uC cycles) to write the same information compared to LCD"; the CPU load "causes timing irregularities in CW keying"; workaround "Turn off CW Decoder and S-Meter. This prevents unneeded OLED writes." This is a keyer-timing hazard (SW-KEY requirements) independent of RF noise.
- The uSDX README (`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threeme3/usdx/master/README.md`) credits "a Hitachi HD44780 LCD display" and mentions no OLED; QRP Labs QSX uses a 16x2 LCD (search summary). The OLED variants are community builds.

**F8. TFT modules: two switching sources (gate-voltage booster, backlight PWM) plus a fast SPI clock; documented remedies are internal PWM and short cables.**
- ST7789V generates VGH/VGL with an on-chip booster clocked from its oscillator (F3); it runs whenever the panel is on, including with a static image.
- TFT_eSPI discussion 2194 (`https://github.com/Bodmer/TFT_eSPI/discussions/2194`): external pin PWM of the backlight "doesn't work well - AND it is very noisy"; using the module's internal PWM gives "the EMI/RFI greatly reduced because its not going through a potentially long cable"; external PWM was "very sensitive to the frequency vs. duty cycle". Focus LCDs FAN4211 (`https://focuslcds.com/journals/application-notes/backlight-control-using-tft-hdmi-modules/`): backlight PWM "frequency of the cycle is defined in a range from 31.37kHz to 137Hz", duty 50 to 90 %. A backlight PWM at f_pwm produces spectral lines at every harmonic; at 20 kHz that is a comb 20 kHz apart across the band (derived), so DC (linear) dimming or PWM followed by an LC filter into the LED is the quiet option.
- The uBITX community documents birdies with Nextion TFT displays (groups.io thread "Nextion Display on uBITX", search summary); the ubitx.net remedy page did not resolve (DNS failure on 2026-09-25). Medium.

**F9. Sharp memory LCD and PCD8544 have no on-module switching converter; e-paper switches only during refresh.**
- Sharp memory LCD: no backlight, no charge pump (VDD/VDDA are supplied externally, 3 V for DH03/DH05, 5 V for LS027, F2); the only periodic signals are EXTCOMIN at 1 to 60 Hz and the SPI burst during an update (fSCLK <= 1.1 MHz, 16.8 ms per full frame at 1.1 MHz, derived). Between updates the bus is idle, so the display is silent except for its VCOM edge at a few hertz. No noise report involving a Sharp memory LCD in a receiver was found (absence of evidence, Low).
- PCD8544 has an internal VLCD "voltage generator" (DC-DC multiplier, F5) that runs continuously; small current (240 uA) but still a switching source; no ham noise reports found (Low).
- E-paper: the SSD1681 booster (external inductor and MOSFETs) runs only during a refresh (2 s full, 0.3 s partial); between refreshes the panel is unpowered (0.003 mW, F6).

### Risk candidates
- RISK-candidate R-UI-01: The Pico 2's 12 MHz crystal (12th harmonic) and 48 MHz USB clock (3rd harmonic) land exactly on 144.000 MHz; a birdie at the band edge during checkout could be mistaken for a display or PA fault. Mitigation: predict and log expected spurs before bench tests; keep the USB PLL off when not enumerated; shield the module; verify with the tinySA (SI-021) or a second receiver. [F10]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-02: OLED or TFT chosen for looks would add a charge pump or booster plus backlight PWM next to a -140 dBm class receiver; history (uSDX, (tr)uSDX, uBITX Nextion) shows conducted birdies and CPU-load keyer jitter. Mitigation: baseline the memory LCD; if a TFT is ever adopted, use DC dimming and an LC-filtered supply. [F7, F8]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-03: Sharp memory LCD is unreadable in the dark; night operation (SI-019 "play radio") may need a light. Mitigation: owner decision on the Azumo film or bezel LEDs; the film costs about twice the panel. [F2]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-04: The Sharp brochure and application information disagree on operating temperature (-10 to +60 C versus -20 to +70 C). Mitigation: design to -10 C and confirm with Sharp or the distributor datasheet revision at CDR. [F2]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-05: The ZIF contact side (bottom versus top) and the exact 10-pin pinout orientation are the classic first-board error for FPC displays. Mitigation: overlay the Sharp outline drawing on the KiCad footprint at CDR, order the connector Sharp names if still active, and add a rendered check per charter rule 3. [F2]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-06: Distributor stock and price could not be verified by machine (403s); the panel could be out of stock at procurement release. Mitigation: owner checks DigiKey/Mouser at CDR and orders the panel and ZIF separately (consigned) if PCBWay's buyer cannot source Sharp. [F22, PCBWay report D-PCB-03]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-07: A mono (TS) plug in the key jack grounds the ring; the plug-detect circuit does not remove that hazard (keyer report F1). Mitigation: SW-KEY-10 interlock plus the F19 detect only as an enabler, not a safety. [F19]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-08: Encoder bushing length versus PCB-to-panel distance: the datasheet lists 7 mm and 10 mm figures for the side-facing configurations; if the front panel outer face sits more than about (bushing length minus 2 mm nut) above the encoder mounting surface, the nut cannot engage. Mitigation: read the exact bushing length of the chosen configuration from the drawing and fix the panel stack-up at PDR. [F12]
- RISK-candidate R-UI-09: The TPA6130A2 charge pump (300 to 500 kHz) is another switching source; its comb at the 300th to 500th harmonic is weak but conducted noise into the product detector is plausible. Mitigation: LC filter, ground plane, and a bench A/B; keep a capacitor-coupled amplifier as the fallback. [F15]
