## 1. Executive summary

- **Owner budget (status note section 10, governs).** Target USD 200 for the first complete radio; **USD 300 is the absolute maximum**, and the worst case including contingency must stay at or below it. **Sales tax is excluded.** Owned and not bought: 24 AWG magnet wire, a 2 m antenna with an SMA-male plug, through-hole resistors and capacitors (surface-mount passives are not owned), a USB power adapter. Answered later the same day (status note section 11): no 18650 cells or charger are owned, so they are priced; test instruments are outside the radio's cap, under a separate USD 300 cap on all new equipment (the tinySA Ultra counts there if it is not yet bought).
- **Recommendation (one sentence):** present **A4 (340 of 500) and A5 (335) together** for the owner's choice, because the ranking is not robust and the totals differ by 5 points (06 section 14.5); A4 has the higher total since revision 4, which restates A5's junction figure as an estimate of 87 to 119 C instead of a bound of 103 C (adversarial R-4), so C8 for A5 falls from 3 to 2, the score A4 already had.
  - **A4 (revision 2, repaired):** the revision-1 radio (JFET preamps and mixer, 500 Hz crystal ladder, analog audio, Morse menu, Adafruit Si5351A module with its 25 MHz crystal, the End-of-Life NXP AFT05MS004NT1 with a hand-derived match and a spare in the order, cells charged outside the radio, one Mouser order plus two JLCPCB 2-layer boards).
  - **A5, "hybrid H1":** A4 with four upgrades that the USD 300 ceiling pays for: a **Mitsubishi RA07M1317M 50 ohm PA module** (no hand-matched network, vendor-guaranteed harmonic and stability limits) in place of the end-of-life NXP AFT05MS004NT1, an **Epson TG2520SMN TCXO** (restores REQ-SYS-010 and the 1.2 kHz carrier guard), the **research-validated diode-ring mixer** in place of the JFET mixer, and a **spare GVA-84+ driver**; plus the INSP-110 and adversarial fixes that every alternative gets (a 160 MHz guaranteed divider for REQ-SYS-182, thermal compound, the 40 dB monitor port in the baseline, a second button, a drive low-pass filter, and a key-down sequence that waits 10 ms for the relay contacts before the envelope ramp). Revision 3 added, at no cost, a VGG clamp and a **vented PA bay** behind a printed double-wall bulkhead, with the cells moved to the far end of the main board, because the sink's inner face, the module and the RF board sit inside the case (section 7.3). Revision 4 adds, at no cost, a VGG clamp sized on the LDO's temperature range and 1 % resistors (3.46 V or less, finding-14) and a pre-order transmit clock-plan item in WP-PDR-20 (R-3), which applies to A4 as well.
- **Cost (section 8.4), sales tax excluded, 15 % contingency on every line:** **USD 204.89 low, 234.68 planning, 294.51 worst case.** The worst case passes the USD 300 screen with USD 5.49 to spare; it stacks every high estimate, a board duty of 92.5 % on the whole JLCPCB order (the base is unpublished) and a Mouser tariff line of 50 % on the China-origin share. The planning figure is **USD 34.68 above the USD 200 target**. The lower-cost alternative A4 (revision 2, repaired) plans at **USD 187.61** (worst 243.42) but keeps an end-of-life PA, misses REQ-SYS-012 at the 6.4 V pack end (about 3.2 W against 3.97 W) and does not show REQ-SYS-112; since revision 4 A5 does not show REQ-SYS-112 either (section 7.3).
- **What the USD 47.07 between A4 and A5 buys (section 8.2, risk per dollar, on the section 7.1 scores; revision 4):** A4 carries seven Red risks and A5 four. The module (USD 36.88) retires two of A4's Reds (no harmonic data with a PA match rebuilt on another substrate; the low-pack power shortfall) and lowers a third without retiring it (the junction, 16 to 12, still Red, because A5's figure is an estimate of 87 to 119 C, not a bound; R-4). It doubles the heat into a sink whose inner face is inside the case: the cell-heating Red rises from 10 to 15 and a fin and PETG Yellow (9) is added; and it brings the module-specific envelope-loop Yellow (6), which revision 3 left out of the reconciliation (finding-17). On the 7.1 scores the module nets **-2 points**: it trades RF and power risks for thermal ones, and its case rests on evidence quality (vendor-guaranteed harmonic and stability limits, scored in C5) rather than on the risk count. The end-of-life risk is Yellow in A4 (spare in the order) and is replaced by an equal single-seller risk. The TCXO (USD 4.15) retires the 30 ppm reference Red and restores a KDR (REQ-SYS-010): 8 points, about USD 0.5 per point. The diode ring (USD 2.60) and the spare driver (USD 3.44) retire no scored risk. Net **6 risk points for USD 47.07, about USD 7.8 per point** (revision 3 gave 16 points at USD 2.9; finding-17 alone makes it 10 points at USD 4.71; R-4 makes it 6).
- **What the owner gets with A5:** 5 W CW at the connector (4.0 to 4.6 W estimated at the 6.4 V pack end after key-down sag and filter loss, against the 3.97 W floor; Low), a 500 Hz crystal-ladder receiver with MDS about -140 dBm (Low), +/-2.5 ppm carrier accuracy, a PA junction estimate of 87 to 119 C under continuous key-down at 45 C ambient (not a bound; REQ-SYS-112 at risk, section 7.3), about 8 to 14 h battery life at 1:9, a case of about 153 x 70 x 42 mm (160 mm with the SMA) and 286 to 361 g, and one flagged hand-solder exception (the leadless 4-pad TCXO). No BGA, no reflow-only part, no hidden thermal pad. **With A4:** 5 W at 8.4 V but about 3.2 W at the 6.4 V pack end, a 30 ppm reference, the same receiver with a JFET mixer, a junction estimate of 100 to 135 C, about 142 x 70 x 42 mm and 277 to 347 g, and no flagged part.
- **What A5 gives up (section 8.8):** the display (Morse menu instead), in-radio charging, the conductive case coating, the tuning encoder and the stainless SMA jack. The heat sink's fins sit behind a printed finger guard; its inner face, the module and the RF board sit in a vented bay walled off from the cells, and at 45 C ambient under heavy sending the cell 60 C trip, not the PA, may be what ends a transmission (section 7.3).
- **Problem requiring a decision (one sentence):** which architecture, parts list and requirement set let the owner build the first complete cwht at his home bench within USD 300 worst case (target USD 200), with listed-price parts only.
- **Robustness verdict (section 6):** **Not robust** between A4 and A5. A4 leads by 5 points (340 against 335; revision 4 scores C8 for A5 2, not 3, adversarial R-4; revision 3 had A5 ahead by 10). A4 is first in 9 of 16 weight perturbations, A5 in 5 (C1, C2 and C6 at -10; C4 and C5 at +10) and A2 in 2 (C3 and C4 at -10); ten single Low-cell moves put another alternative first or level, among them A5 C8 +1 (A5 350 against A4 340), the move the WP-PDR-28 thermal model with the Boyd drawing tests. The choice is between the cheaper radio with requirement deltas, an end-of-life PA and half the heat in the box (A4), and USD 47 more for a vendor-guaranteed PA, the kept REQ-SYS-010 and 012 KDRs and fewer Red risks, with twice the heat (A5). The cost screen passes for A5 on a worst case that is Low confidence and has USD 5.49 of margin.
- **Four findings for the owner before deciding:**
  1. **The revision-1 PA device is End of Life** (NXP page, read 2026-09-25 and re-read by INSP-110 on 2026-09-27). Revision 1 said "active production"; that was wrong. A5 does not use it; A4 keeps it with a spare in the order.
  2. **The revision-1 prescaler was used above its published rating** (SN74LVC74A: TI page 100 MHz; datasheet 150 MHz minimum at 3.3 V, -40 to 85 C, with a 3.3 ns pulse-width minimum against a 3.38 ns half-period). Every alternative now uses the Nexperia 74LVC1G80, guaranteed 160 MHz minimum at 3.0 to 3.6 V over -40 to +125 C (datasheet Rev. 17, 12 November 2024), USD 0.15 at Mouser.
  3. **The module doubles the heat into the sink** (8.5 to 10 W against about 5 W for the AFT05), because it is less efficient. The junction figure is an estimate, not a bound: 87 to 119 C at the 45 C continuous corner against 110 C, depending on how much of the fin area works in outside air (adversarial R-4; WP-PDR-28 settles it with the Boyd drawing); the fins can reach 56 to 62 C at 25 C ambient, so they sit behind a printed guard and nothing PETG touches the sink. Revision 2 called this "heat outside the case"; that was wrong (adversarial R-2): the sink's inner face (about 70 to 99 C at the 45 C corner, revision 4), the module and the RF board are inside the box, and with them in the same air as the cells the interior reaches about 74 C at 45 C ambient under continuous key-down, above the cells' 60 C discharge limit and PETG's 69 C heat-deflection temperature. Revision 3 walls them into a vented bay and moves the cells to the far end; the estimate is then 54 to 56 C for the cell-bay air at that corner (Low), with pass criteria in WP-PDR-28 before the order (section 7.3).
  4. **Two transmit-clock claims were wrong (adversarial R-3).** Revision 3 said that USB off in transmit removes the 48 MHz x 3 product at 144.000 MHz; but the RP2350 ADC needs a 48 MHz clock and the ALC plausibility check reads the ADC in transmit, so that line stays, on top of the 12 MHz x 12 line on the same frequency. The 125 MHz transmit system clock, the 25 MHz reference's 5th and 6th harmonics (125 and 150 MHz) and the /8 prescaler's products at 126 to 130 and 162 to 167 MHz also fall inside the low-pass passbands. None has a price; a pre-order clock-plan item in WP-PDR-20 lists every transmit clock line from 118 to 175 MHz against a coupling budget at the GVA-84+ input (about -61 dBm for 25 uW at the SMA, -68 dBm for the 60 dBc target), and the tinySA sweep verifies it. It applies to A4 and A5 alike (section 7.3).
- **Owner's decision (section 10):** pending. Open owner questions, each with a recommendation, are in section 8.13.

- **Direct-conversion receiver.** 0 dB opposite-sideband response and a 3 dB double-sideband noise penalty; fails REQ-SYS-022 and 025. Kept only as a perfboard audio-chain prototype.
- **SA612 mixer, commercial CW filters (Inrad #111 USD 118, KVG on quote), LMX2571 synthesizer (WQFN with exposed pad, 0.5 mm).** Discontinued, over budget for their function, sold on quote, or reflow-only.
- **In-radio 2S charging (revision 2 re-check under the ceiling).** The USB-input 2S charger ICs found (CN3302 with HY2213 balancers and FS8205A switches) are LCSC-only; no leaded USB-to-2S charger IC with a Mouser listing was found in the power research. Not cheap and not safer; pruned for build 1 (owner question Q3, section 8.13).
- **A spare RA07M1317M in the baseline.** USD 33.25 capped; it pushes the A5 worst case to about USD 327.8, over the ceiling. Kept as a gate add-back (AB-A, section 8.4).
- **Perfboard or Manhattan for the RF sections.** Admitted for prototypes and non-RF sections only; a 144 MHz LPF, drive chain and front end need controlled ground and short returns that a 2-layer board with a solid pour gives for USD 2.

### 3.3 Weight rationale

Cost carries 25 (C1 20, C2 5), down from 35 in revision 1, because the owner's section 10 answer turns the firm USD 200 cap into a USD 200 target with a USD 300 absolute maximum: the ceiling is enforced by M1, and C1 now scores the distance above the target. The 10 points freed go to RF and emissions confidence (C5, 15 to 20; a legal limit, 97.307(e), and the radio's purpose) and to safety and thermal margin (C8, 10 to 15; REQ-SYS-112 is a KDR and the PETG case has a 69 C heat-deflection temperature, TS-011 M8), following the risk-per-dollar reading of section 2 item 9. Hand-solderability (C3, 15) stays high because it is the owner's stated reason for the SMD boundary. Build effort (C4, 10) and envelope (C6, 10, negotiable) follow; deltas and firmware scope (C7, 5) matter least because the Morse UI adds the same firmware to every alternative. No weight was set by the owner directly.

### 7.3 Feasibility: the PA and the receiver at 144 MHz with hand-solderable parts

**Through-hole at 144 MHz, honestly (transmitter and receiver research).**
- No through-hole VHF power device is in active production and stocked by a franchised distributor. RF Parts lists NOS parts with prices (RD06HVF1 USD 9.91, RD15HVF1 USD 14.91, 2N4427 USD 3.91), which meets the listed-price rule but is a finite supply; the RD06HVF1 gives 3.8 W at 7 V and 2.8 W at 6 V on its datasheet graph.
- The IRF510 has roughly 0 to 5 dB of power gain at 144 MHz; the BS170 and 2N7000 are DC switches here.
- The TO-92 J310 is listed (InterFET J310 at Mouser USD 5.83, 381 in stock; INSP-110 row 25); the SOT-23 MMBFJ310 (USD 0.23) is chosen on cost. The SA612 mixer is discontinued. A 146 MHz tuned circuit has no catalog through-hole coil.
- **Conclusion:** a 5 W 2 m radio from catalog stock needs a PA module or a handful of SOT-89 and SOT-23 parts, and 0805 or 1206 capacitors in the RF sections. Everything else is through-hole or a module.

**PA of A5: line-up and drive.**
- Si5351A CLK1 (3.3 V CMOS, drive 8 mA; into 50 ohm about +10.4 dBm at the fundamental, derived from a 1.65 Vpp square wave; up to about +12.9 dBm if the output resistance is 25 ohm) into a 3-pole drive LPF (fc about 170 MHz, one 1812SMS and two 1206 C0G), an input pad, the GVA-84+ (24.1 dB typical at 100 MHz, P1dB +20.4 dBm typical and +19.4 dBm minimum; datasheet Rev. F per the adversarial check), a 3 dB output pad, and the RA07M1317M module (Pin 20 mW nominal; stability specified for Pin 10 to 30 mW; 30 mW maximum rating).
- **Overdrive designed out:** the drive level is set by the Si5351 swing and two fixed pads, not by the GVA-84+ compressing. With an 18 dB input pad and GVA gain 22.5 to 25 dB, the module sees 10.5 to 14.4 dBm (11 to 28 mW) over the Si5351 range of +9 to +10.4 dBm; the GVA-84+ runs at about +16 dBm, 3.4 dB under its minimum P1dB. **LTspice check before the order (WP-PDR-21):** the drive chain with the Si5351 modelled as a 3.3 V square source of 25 to 50 ohm output resistance, the GVA-84+ S-parameters, the pads and the drive LPF; **pass: module input 10 to 30 mW at every corner (144 to 148 MHz, 25 and 50 ohm source, GVA gain 22.5 and 25 dB), 3f at the GVA input at least 25 dB below the fundamental.** If the 25 ohm corner exceeds 30 mW, the input pad grows by 1 dB and the low corner is re-checked.
- **Drive gating and key clicks.** Output power is set by VGG (datasheet graph at 7.2 V: about 0 W at 1.5 V, 2 W at 2.5 V, 4 W at 3.0 V, 7 W at 3.5 V; steep and nonlinear). A raised-cosine VGG would not give a raised-cosine envelope, so the ALC is a closed envelope loop: a 1N5711 detector on the LPF output side feeds an MCP6002 error amplifier whose reference is the firmware raised-cosine ramp (filtered PWM), and the loop drives VGG. **VGG clamp (revision 3, finding-14; resized in revision 4).** Datasheet maximum ratings (Jun. 2019, read 2026-09-27): VGG 4 V applies only at VDD 7.2 V or less with Pin 20 mW; Pout 10 W is rated at VGG 3.5 V or less; the stability guarantee covers Pout up to 8 W. Revision 2's 0.75 divider (3.83 V at VDD up to 8.4 V) was outside the first two conditions. Revision 3's 0.68 divider was sized on a 5.1 V LDO maximum that the datasheet does not give. The TI LM2940 datasheet (SNVS769J, section 6.5, 5 V option, read 2026-09-27) gives an output of 4.85 to 5.15 V at TJ 25 C and **4.75 to 5.25 V** over the recommended operating temperature range, and the MCP6002 output swings to its 5 V rail, so 0.68 reached 3.50 V at 25 C and 3.57 V over temperature, and about 3.68 V with 5 % resistors (INSP-110 iteration 3). VGG is now bounded by a **divider of ratio 0.654 from two 1 % resistors** of E24 values (for example 2.7 k over 5.1 k, or the same ratio at the impedance the WP-PDR-22 deck sets for the module's VGG input current): ratio 0.6493 to 0.6584 over both tolerances, so VGG is at most 5.25 x 0.6584 = **3.46 V** and at least 4.75 x 0.6493 = **3.08 V**, before the op-amp's output headroom, the module's gate current and any LM2940 dropout at the low pack end, all of which only lower it. The highest value is under the 3.5 V condition at every pack voltage and LDO temperature. The resistors must be 1 % (metal film): from the owner's assortment if it is 1 % (gate item 9), otherwise two 0805 1 % values inside the E2 row's USD 0 to 3.00 assortment-gap allowance, so no total changes. A reference independent of the 5 V bus was not taken: it adds a part, and the 1 % divider meets the condition. The datasheet gives nominal output at VGG 3 V typical and 3.5 V maximum, so a module at the high end of that spread may reach slightly less than saturation at the 6.4 V pack end with the clamp at its low end; that is carried in the REQ-SYS-012 risk (0.0 to 0.6 dB margin) and checked in WP-PDR-22 below. **Open-loop case.** If the loop opens (detector open, or the error amplifier at its rail), VGG sits at the clamp: with the clamp at 3.5 V or less the Pout 10 W rating condition holds, but at a full pack the module may exceed the 8 W stability range (revision 2's own figure is about 10.5 W at 8.4 V with VGG unbounded). Two bounds apply: the firmware plausibility check of REQ-SYS-156 (forward-power reading against the drive level) enters Fault-safe within 100 ms, and the 10 s hardware cutoff bounds the time. If the WP-PDR-22 run shows more than 8 W at the clamp at 8.4 V, the clamp is made pack-voltage dependent with the spare MCP6002 half and the spare 1N5711W already in the BOM (no cost), lowering it at high VDD while keeping the fixed-divider value at 6.4 V. **Key-down sequence (revision 3, adversarial R-1).** The Omron G5V-2 datasheet (read 2026-09-27) gives an operate time of 7 ms maximum and a release time of 3 ms maximum; its bounce-time distribution graph shows about 0.3 to 0.5 ms of operate bounce (graph read). The ALC detector is on the LPF output, after relay pole A, so revision 2's 5 ms settle could start the ramp with the contacts still open: the loop would see no output, integrate VGG to the clamp, and step to full saturated power when the contacts closed (a key click, and at 8.4 V outside the stability conditions). Sequence now: relay to TX at t0; at t0 + 8 ms GVA-84+ bias on and CLK1 enabled with VGG still held at 0 V (module off); at **t0 + 10 ms** (7 ms operate plus 0.5 ms bounce plus 2.5 ms margin) the clamps on the envelope reference and the VGG node release and the ramp starts. Until then the error-amplifier integrator is parked at 0 V: the reference clamp holds the reference at 0 V and a bias resistor (owned) on the inverting input gives the error amplifier a small negative idle error, so its output rests at the 0 V rail, not wherever the offset drifts, and there is no wound-up integrator to release. As a check on a late or failed contact, firmware compares the detector reading with the reference at mid-ramp and enters Fault-safe if the reading lags (the REQ-SYS-156 range table, an L2 design item). The 10 ms lead-in is inside REQ-SYS-161's 12 ms and gives 1 ms sampling + 2 ms make filter + 10 ms = 13 ms against REQ-SYS-160's 15 ms. A4 uses the same relay and takes the same sequence. At key-up: the ramp takes VGG under 1.2 V (module output about 0 W), then after 1 ms CLK1 is disabled and the GVA-84+ bias switched off, then the relay returns to RX after the hang time. So the abrupt CLK1 edge happens only with the module off (backwave at most about -17 dBm estimated, under the 25 uW spur limit and never on the air in RX). The hardware clamps of REQ-SYS-055, 180, 181 and 092 are open-collector pulls on the envelope reference and VGG node (on at reset), and CLK1 enable is the second, independent condition of REQ-SYS-120. **LTspice check before the order (WP-PDR-22):** the envelope loop with the VGG transfer as a table from the datasheet graphs (Pout versus VGG at 7.2 V; Pout versus VDD at VGG 3.5 V) at 6.4 and 8.4 V, the G5V-2 pole A modelled as a switch that closes at 7.5 ms (operate plus bounce, with 0.5 ms of bounce) and, as a fault case, at 12 ms, and the integrator's idle bias; **pass: 10 to 90 % rise and fall inside the set 3 to 8 ms (REQ-SYS-014), 26 dB bandwidth at most 350 Hz with continuous dits at 50 WPM (REQ-SYS-015, TC-SYS-013 method), overshoot at most 0.2 dB with the nominal contact timing, loop phase margin at least 45 degrees, VGG never above 3.5 V with the LM2940 output at 4.75 to 5.25 V (and in dropout at the 6.4 V end under key-down sag), the divider resistors at +/-1 %, the MCP6002 output headroom and the module's VGG input current modelled (revision 4, finding-14); the integrator output at 0 V at ramp start; with the contact closing at 12 ms (fault case), module output at most 8 W at every instant and the mid-ramp plausibility check tripping; open-loop case (detector open) at 8.4 V: module output at the clamp at most 8 W, or the pack-dependent clamp adopted; at 6.4 V the lowest clamp (3.08 V: LM2940 at 4.75 V and the lowest divider ratio) still gives at least 3.97 W at the SMA with the table's high-VGG module.**
- **RF off (REQ-SYS-183, -57 dBm).** In receive and in every ended or inhibited state, CLK1 is disabled, the GVA-84+ is unpowered and VGG is at 0 V, so nothing at the carrier frequency is generated; the relay's receive pole grounds the RX input. Designed out; **bench check before first on-air use:** tinySA at the monitor port with the radio in receive, inhibited and ended states: at most -97 dBm at the port (-57 dBm at the antenna).
- **Stability.** The module's stability is vendor-specified (spurious below -60 dBc) for VDD 4.0 to 9.2 V, Pin 10 to 30 mW, Pout up to 8 W by VGG control and load VSWR 4:1. The design keeps VDD at 5.5 to 8.4 V, Pin at 11 to 28 mW (LTspice check above), and VGG positive only while the drive is on (sequence above). The GVA-84+ is unconditionally stable (datasheet, per the adversarial check). The remaining exposure is board-level feedback around about 45 dB of gain; **bench check before first on-air use:** with the dummy load and at 6.0 and 8.4 V, (a) VGG at 0 V and CLK1 on: no output above -30 dBm at any frequency from 1 MHz to 1.5 GHz; (b) at each power step: no non-harmonic output within 20 dB of the 25 uW limit (the tinySA sweep of the harmonic check below).
- **Output power at the connector at the 6.4 V pack end (answers adversarial C2 for A5).** REQ-SYS-012 measures the pack in receive; at key-down the pack delivers about 2.0 A (module about 1.8 A plus the 5 V bus). ### 8.7 Morse-code audio user interface (owner direction 8)

**Controls.** Two push buttons (B3F-1052: MENU and ALT), two owner potentiometers on ADC inputs (pot 1: fine tuning window of +/-5 kHz around the entered centre, about 10 to 20 Hz resolution with hysteresis; pot 2: volume), the key or paddle on the key jack, and the power switch. The Pico 2 on-board LED, behind a light pipe, shows transmit and blinks fault codes.

**Sequence (the owner's words, implemented).**
1. A short press of MENU enters the menu. Transmit is disarmed: the key line goes to the decoder, and only the sidetone path sounds.
2. The radio sends the menu letters in Morse through the sidetone path, at the keyer speed, with the tone level held under the headphone ceiling (REQ-SYS-071).
3. The operator sends one letter on the key or paddle to pick an item, for example: F frequency, P power step, S keyer speed, K key mode (straight, iambic A, iambic B), T tone pitch, C call sign, B battery and status read-out, X exit.
4. For a number, the operator sends digits (for F: kHz within the band, for example 144050); the paddle can also step a value (dit down, dah up) with a step size set in the menu.
5. The radio reads the value back in Morse.
6. The operator sends R to confirm or N to reject (the value is not applied on N). No entry for 20 s (TBR) exits without change.

**Rules.**
- Two menu levels at most from menu entry (REQ-SYS-062).
- The 5 W step needs the read-back and an R (REQ-SYS-063); a first entry after power-on starts at a lower step.
- A long press of MENU outside the menu sends the status in Morse (frequency, power step, key mode, speed, battery), replacing the status display (REQ-SYS-060).
- **Key-mode selection without the key (REQ-SYS-163, finding-7):** holding ALT for 2 s steps the key mode (straight, iambic A, iambic B) and the radio announces the new mode in Morse; this path uses the buttons only, so it works while any key input reads closed.
- **Guest lock (REQ-SYS-066, finding-7):** MENU and ALT held together for 3 s, then an R on a third press of MENU, sets or releases the guest lock.
- The call sign is sent at power-on and when the headphones are plugged in (REQ-SYS-006).
- Faults are announced in Morse within 1 s through the headphones and shown by an LED blink code without them (REQ-SYS-067, 077).
- Low battery, the identification reminder and the separation reminder are announced (REQ-SYS-068, 069, 096).
- The menu override command path stays safety-critical (SRR decision 9); the menu never clears a hardware clamp.
- Straight-key decoding is adaptive (the highest firmware risk); paddle decoding is exact; if the straight-key decoder proves unreliable, menu entry is paddle-only.

**Firmware scope added (WP-PDR-35, 41):** Morse sender on the PWM tone path, adaptive decoder, menu state machine with R/N confirmation and time-out, number entry and read-back, pot reading with hysteresis, status and fault announcements, LED codes, PWM edge counter for REQ-SYS-182, envelope reference ramp and ALC set point, transmit sequencing (relay, 10 ms settle, GVA bias, CLK1, ramp; revision 3), the mid-ramp detector plausibility check (REQ-SYS-156), TX clock plan (transmit system clock and ADC clock gating per the WP-PDR-20 item, CLK0 and CLK2 off; revision 4), AGC control voltage, Si5351 I2C driver with the TCXO reference. Removed: display driver and encoder drivers. The audio stays analog, so no ADC audio path or DSP is added.

**Hazard notes.** HZ-004 (stuck transmission): transmit is disarmed in the menu and the hardware cutoffs stay independent. HZ-005 (hearing): every menu tone passes through the same capped path as the sidetone.
