## 1. Purpose, scope and phase

This analysis identifies the hazards of the cwht radio as it will be built and operated, assesses each in the uncontrolled state, proposes the controls the design, the firmware, the operator and the documentation must provide, states the residual risk reachable with those controls, and determines which firmware components are safety-critical. It covers the life of at most five units (SI-035) in the hands of the owner and licensed friends (SI-019, SI-030), from the owner's hand assembly through bench test, on-air use, charging, storage and firmware update.

In scope, following the SEMP section 7.1 list item by item: RF exposure of the operator and of bystanders (HZ-001, HZ-006); RF burn from the antenna connector (HZ-012); PA and enclosure surface temperature (HZ-003); lithium-ion charging, over-discharge, short circuit and thermal runaway (HZ-002, HZ-007); hearing damage from headphone level and sidetone (HZ-005); unintended transmission from a keyer, sequencer or PA-enable fault, a stuck key or a wrong plug, including out-of-band or over-power emission (HZ-004, HZ-008); sharp enclosure edges (HZ-013); a USB firmware load or reset that leaves the unit unsafe (HZ-014); the owner's hand soldering: burns, fumes and damage to assembled parts (HZ-015). The analysis adds: harmonic and spurious emission into other services (HZ-008), antenna connector mechanics (HZ-009), ESD and RF pickup on the jacks (HZ-010), charging while transmitting (HZ-011) and the bench test modes of `docs/conops/conops.md` section 3.5.2 item 2 as causes of HZ-004 and HZ-005. Out of scope: mission-only failures (a dead receiver, a wrong frequency display) unless they contribute to a hazard above; these are mission-critical matters of 03 section 4.3 and the risk register. A wrong transmitted frequency is not mission-only: it is HZ-008 cause C7.

Phase: this is the SWEHB 5.24 Phase 0 and 1 delivery (introduction, all known hazards with causes, controls, risk). Phase 2 (approved controls with verification methods, controls allocated to `REQ-*` ids) is due at PDR and updated at CDR; Phase 3 (verifications performed) at TRR and SAR. Each phase re-runs the safety-critical determination (03 section 4.1 step 5).

## 2. System and operating context assumed

The assessment rests on the owner decisions and research baselines listed in the SRR brief, in particular: 5 W A1A on 144.000 to 148.000 MHz from a Pico 2 (RP2350) controller; a 2S pack of two user-replaceable 18650 cells in holders; the Pico 2 micro-USB for both programming and charging, with the transmitter inhibited by hardware whenever USB power is present and charging paused while the radio is switched on; power steps 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 W within +/-1 dB with a 1 W default; a 0.5 W tune carrier with a timeout (10 s in the research; at most 5.5 s (TBR) in REQ-SYS-020 on the 5 s +/-0.5 s proposal of this analysis, package decision 36, section 11); a sequenced RF relay for semi break-in (SI-036); a harmonic low-pass filter at the antenna port after the T/R node; an SMA jack retained by the enclosure boss; headphones only, with a fixed hardware output ceiling; straight key and iambic paddle on one 3.5 mm TRS jack (SI-018, SI-034), keyer modes Straight, Iambic A (default), Iambic B, Ultimatic and Bug; PCBWay surface-mount assembly with the owner hand-soldering the through-hole parts (SI-031); all operators licensed (SI-030).

Operator model OPS-A (each licensed friend operates a loaned unit as their own station, own call sign; a licensee-settable receive-only guest lock exists) is assumed; OPS-C (an unlicensed guest keying under supervision as a third party, 47 CFR 97.115(b)(1), eCFR 2026-09-23) is treated as a covered misuse case, not a planned use (`docs/research/regulatory-corpus-and-operators.md` F4, F5). Postures assumed for exposure: nominal, radio on a surface or at waist level with the antenna base 20 cm or more from head and torso (mobile posture); off-nominal, handheld at the face (portable posture, SAR regime) (`docs/research/rf-exposure-evaluation.md` F1, F7).

Fleet horizon for likelihood: at most 5 units over about three years, taken as 15 unit-years.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Identification

Hazards were collected from: the five candidate hazards assigned in 03 section 4.2 (HZ-001 to HZ-005, ids carried unchanged); the SEMP section 7.1 scope list; every stakeholder input SI-001 to SI-036; the RISK-candidate, HZ-candidate and DECISION-needed items of the research reports named in the sources of each hazard; the ConOps modes, scenarios and bench test modes; the risk register; the regulatory corpus; and the findings of the first independent review, which added the toggling key-stream causes of HZ-004, the out-of-band fundamental of HZ-008, the test-mode causes of HZ-004 and HZ-005 and hazard HZ-015. Two of the assigned hazards were split so that each hazard has one exposed population or one energy path: HZ-001 covers the operating licensee (occupational tier) and HZ-006 covers bystanders, household members and non-licensee holders (general population); HZ-002 covers the charging side and HZ-007 the discharge side of the lithium-ion pack, because the PA path bypasses the charger and its protections (`docs/research/power-tree-and-charging.md` F11).

Each hazard record holds (schema `docs/safety/schema.json`): the scenario, the exposed persons, the operating phases, the causes with their initiating domain, the worst credible effect with the quantities that set the severity, severity and likelihood in the uncontrolled state with rationale and confidence, the firmware role in the terms of the para 3.2 criteria, the SWE-134 items its firmware controls implement, the controls with type, allocation, independence from firmware, status and the `control_req_ids` that implement them, the union `requirement_ids` the traceability tool reads, the residual assessment with its condition, the verification note per control (evidence classes of charter section 9), single point failure candidates, the fault tolerance achieved, related risks, stakeholder inputs, pending owner decisions, sources and an append-only history. This is the SWEHB 5.24 minimum content (scenario, risk, controls with barriers and warnings, verifications). The file also holds `open_questions`, the requests this analysis makes of other authors.

### 3.2 Severity scale (operational)

Severity is the worst credible consequence of the hazard in the uncontrolled state. The four levels follow common system-safety practice (the four-severity by five-likelihood form of MIL-STD-882; that standard is not in the corpus and nothing here quotes it; the definitions are this project's own). The last column maps each level to the safety consequence dimension of the risk register (`docs/process/06-risk-and-decision-analysis.md` section 7) so that the risk written under the hazard link rule scores consistently.

| Severity | Operational definition | Register safety level |
|---|---|---|
| Catastrophic | Death or permanent disabling injury to any person; fire not contained to the unit; lithium-ion cell venting with flame or explosion | 5 |
| Critical | Injury requiring medical treatment (second-degree burn, permanent hearing threshold shift); fire or smoke contained to the unit; RF exposure of the operator above the 47 CFR 1.1310(b) occupational limit, or of a bystander, guest or household member above the 47 CFR 1.1310(c) general-population limit; harmful interference to a radionavigation or other safety service (47 CFR 97.3(a)(23)) | 4 |
| Marginal | First-aid injury (minor burn or cut, temporary hearing discomfort recovering within 24 h); destruction of the unit without fire; RF exposure of the operator above 50 percent of the 1.1310(b) occupational limit or of a bystander above 50 percent of the general-population limit; interference to another amateur station or a Part 97 emission violation without a safety-service victim | 3 (first aid) or 2 (discomfort) |
| Negligible | Discomfort or nuisance without injury: an audible click, a warm surface below 45 C, cosmetic damage, a receiver birdie | 1 |

Any exceedance of an FCC exposure limit, for any person, is Critical; the Marginal band covers only the loss of margin below the limit. 06 section 7 level 4 names RF exposure above the applicable FCC limit for the operator, a bystander or any other person, which is aligned with this rule (OQ-SAF-020 item 6, Closed at 0.4.1-pha).

### 3.3 Likelihood scale (operational)

Likelihood is assessed in the uncontrolled state (the baseline architecture without the listed controls) against the fleet horizon of section 2. The anchors are chosen so that a single common fault or a single common operator error is B, and two independent faults are E.

| Level | Name | Operational definition |
|---|---|---|
| A | Frequent | Expected every time the initiating condition occurs, more than once per unit-year, or inherent to normal operation without the control |
| B | Probable | Expected several times in the fleet life; a single common operator error or a single common component fault (contact bounce, a mono plug, an undeburred edge, mismatched cells, a wrong image flashed) is enough |
| C | Occasional | Expected about once in the fleet life; needs an uncommon operator action or a single component or firmware fault |
| D | Remote | Not expected in the fleet life but credible; needs a rare fault, or one fault plus an uncommon condition |
| E | Improbable | Needs two or more independent faults, or a fault plus a deliberate defeat of a control; assumed not to occur in the fleet life |

### 3.4 Risk matrix and required response

| Severity \ Likelihood | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catastrophic | High | High | High | Serious | Medium |
| Critical | High | High | Serious | Medium | Low |
| Marginal | Serious | Serious | Medium | Low | Low |
| Negligible | Medium | Medium | Low | Low | Low |

Two cells differ from the usual 882-style default: Marginal-D and Negligible-C are rated Low here, because Marginal is bounded to first aid and D is "not expected in the fleet life"; the schema enforces exactly this matrix for `initial_risk` and `residual_risk.level`.

| Level | Required response |
|---|---|
| High | Unacceptable as a residual. A design change or an additional independent control is required before PDR; a hazard still High at PDR is a Major RID (01 section 10.2), and a hazard without an allocated control requirement can never be a lien from PDR onward (01 section 12.2) |
| Serious | Acceptable as a residual only with owner acceptance recorded in a decision memo, at least two independent controls of which one is neither software nor procedure, and verification of every control by Test |
| Medium | Acceptable as a residual with the listed controls allocated to requirements and verified, and the owner's acceptance recorded at SAR; Claude manages; listed at every review |
| Low | Acceptable; controls documented, allocated and verified by the method assigned in the requirement; listed at every review |

### 3.5 Initial versus residual, pending requirements, and the risk register

`initial_risk` is the uncontrolled assessment. `residual_risk` is the level reachable when every control that has an implementing requirement is verified; its `condition` states what must be true for the level to hold. A control whose requirement does not yet exist carries the status **Requirement pending**: it is listed so that the owner sees the proposal, the missing requirement is requested in `open_questions`, and it does not count toward the residual until the requirement exists. The condition then states the level expected once the pending controls are allocated and verified. The residual is what the owner accepts at SAR. A control whose requirement exists but whose adoption is still an owner decision (status Proposed, the decision named in `decisions_pending` by its SRR package section 13.1 number) counts toward the residual like any other Proposed control; its condition also states the residual that holds if the owner declines it, when the decision memo retires the requirement and the control becomes Dropped. Section 8.3 lists these decline cases with the section 3.4 response each requires.

Under the hazard link rule (06 section 1), every hazard whose controls are not yet verified is carried by at least one risk in `docs/risk/register.json` whose `related.hazard_ids` names it, the hazard's `related_risk_ids` names that risk back, and the risk's safety consequence is at least the level its severity maps to. The risk closes when the controls are verified and the hazard record persists. Section 12 lists the links and the register actions still needed.

### 3.6 Owner decisions and TBRs

Where a control's number rests on a research proposal the owner has not yet decided, the control text says "Proposed, owner decision pending at SRR" and the hazard's `decisions_pending` names the decision by its research id (RFX-D1, D-PWR-03, D-KN2 and so on) and, where the SRR package consolidates it, by its section 13.1 decision number (for example package decision 38). Where a number may change, the control carries a `tbr` object with owner, plan and closing gate: PDR for every number that an L1 requirement carries, including the debounce values of REQ-SYS-048 (charter section 7: L1 TBRs close by PDR), and CDR only for numbers that live solely in L2 requirements, mirroring the TBR policy of 02.

## 5. Hazard narratives

The paragraphs below give the decisive numbers and the decisive controls; the full records are in `hazards.json`.

**HZ-001, operator SAR.** Within 20 cm the portable-device SAR regime applies (47 CFR 2.1093(b), (d)(1)) and, because every operator is a licensee (SI-030), the occupational limits govern: 8 W/kg per 1 g, 20 W/kg per 10 g extremity, 6 min averaging with duty factor permitted (47 CFR 1.1310(b), 2.1093(d)(3)). No SAR measurement exists at project scale; by analogy to FCC-database VHF handhelds (0.05 to 0.35 W/kg per W at 50 percent duty) the licensee is at 17.5 percent of the limit for 5 W continuous CW and 43.8 percent for a 6 min 5 W carrier; the limit needs 28.6 W CW or 11.4 W carrier (`docs/research/rf-exposure-evaluation.md` F5, F6). The hazard is therefore Marginal (loss of margin, not an exceedance, which section 3.2 would rate Critical), and the controls hold the margin under an over-power fault and bound duty: power steps with a 1 W default and a deliberate 5 W selection, a 0.5 W tune carrier ending at most 5.5 s after it starts (REQ-SYS-020, TBR), the firmware stuck-key controls (K3), the hardware cutoff (K9) and the transmission-length backstop (K10, REQ-SYS-180, package decision 38), ALC, the handbook's exposure section (the information the occupational tier requires under 1.1310(e)(2) and 97.13(c)(1)), the ConOps posture and an engraved legend. The analogy's 7:1 spread and the homebrew form factor are the honest uncertainty, carried by RSK-030; if the RF exposure evaluation shows the limit can be exceeded, the severity rises to Critical.

**HZ-002, charging-side cell fault.** Catastrophic by definition; likelihood B because mismatched or reversed cells (C6) are a single common operator error by several users. The allocated layers are the charger's regulation and OVP at 4.20 V per cell with JEITA and a 12 h timer (REQ-SYS-081, 082, 089) and a 2S protector opening the charge path at 4.25 V (REQ-SYS-083); firmware adds dual dissimilar sensing (charger ADC versus RP2350 dividers, disagreement above 100 mV stops charging) and an insertion check (`docs/research/power-tree-and-charging.md` F5, F11, F15, F24). The two-fault tolerance claimed at 0.1.0 is withdrawn for now: the third layer, the 4.30 V secondary protector K3, has no requirement (REQ-SYS-083 is the 4.25 V layer; OQ-SAF-008), and every layer, firmware included, senses the cell junction through one MID sense net (300 ohm, HZ-007 K3), so one open or high-resistance sense connection plus a cell imbalance blinds per-cell detection everywhere. The Kelvin sense constraint K9 (a sense route per layer; OQ-SAF-009) removes that single point. Residual today Catastrophic-D, Serious (Low confidence in D: the firmware disagreement check may catch a floating MID input); with K3 and K9 allocated and verified, Catastrophic-E, Medium, accepted at SAR under RSK-007. The temperature window shares one NTC between the charger and the firmware lock-out, a named single point.

**HZ-003, PA and enclosure temperature.** The two-stage PA line-up draws 11.4 W DC for 5 W out and the module alternative dissipates 7.9 W (`docs/research/pa-device-candidates.md` F4, F8, F19); the pack supports about 1.5 h of continuous key-down. Re-rated Critical: in the uncontrolled state, long or stuck 5 W keying with the radio in a pocket can hold the skin-contact surface above about 55 C for minutes, a partial-thickness burn needing treatment, and drive the junction past 150 C with smoke contained to the unit (the burn-threshold figure is general burn-injury knowledge, not a corpus source; confidence Medium-Low). **HZ-008, harmonic, spurious and out-of-band emissions.** The 7th harmonic (1008 to 1036 MHz) lands in the aeronautical radionavigation band that carries the 1030 MHz SSR/IFF interrogation frequency (47 CFR 2.106 footnote US78, 91 FR 1405) and DME; the 3rd lands on 70 cm including the 432.300 to 432.400 MHz beacon segment; the 2nd on a military-only band (`docs/research/regulatory-corpus-and-operators.md` F9). Interference to radionavigation is harmful interference per se (47 CFR 97.3(a)(23)). ## 6. Safety-critical software determination (SWE-205)

### 6.1 Criteria and method

NASA-STD-8739.8B para 3.2 (via SWEHB 7.02 section 1.2): software is safety-critical if it is determined by and traceable to a hazard analysis and meets at least one of: (a) causes or contributes to a system hazardous condition or event; (b) controls functions identified in a system hazard; (c) provides mitigation for a system hazardous condition or event; (d) mitigates damage if a hazardous condition or event occurs; (e) detects, reports and takes corrective action if the system reaches a potentially hazardous state. The method is 03 section 4.1: for each hazard the firmware role is stated in these terms (`firmware_role.criteria` in `hazards.json`); a component is safety-critical when it is named in `firmware_role.components` of a hazard whose criteria are non-empty, and its criteria are the union over those hazards. The SWE-134 note (components that control, mitigate or contribute to a hazard, and software used to command hazardous operations) yields the same set.

## 8. Fault tolerance philosophy and single point failures

### 8.1 Philosophy (SRR entrance row 15; NPR 7123.1D App. G Table G-4 success criterion on single point failure and fault tolerance)

1. **Hardware before software.** For PA enable on a continuous key-down (REQ-SYS-055), the transmit inhibit with USB power (REQ-SYS-092), the charging cut-off (REQ-SYS-081, 083) and the audio ceiling (REQ-SYS-071, 073), the primary control is a hardware device or a passive network that acts with no firmware involvement (SEMP section 7.1); firmware adds detection, reporting and a dissimilar second layer (REQ-SYS-053, 054, 002, 088, 074). Two further hardware controls are proposed for SRR because firmware is otherwise the only automatic control of their branches: the transmission-length backstop (HZ-004 K12, REQ-SYS-180, package decision 38) and the over-temperature cut-off (HZ-003 K9, REQ-SYS-181, package decision 39).
2. **No single software event initiates a hazard (SWE-134 i), and no single software fault sustains a continuous carrier.** Every path from firmware to RF passes a hardware element with an independent time or state bound for a continuous key-down (the PA-enable monostable, REQ-SYS-055) and for reset, bootloader and USB-powered states (the pull-downs, the fail-safe relay and the VBUS inhibit: REQ-SYS-119, 092, 149); in firmware, RF needs two independent conditions (REQ-SYS-120). This item **holds for two branches only if the owner adopts the controls written for them**, and the analysis says so rather than claim it: a toggling TX_KEY stream retriggers the monostable and is bounded in hardware only by K12 (REQ-SYS-180, package decision 38; HZ-004 rows 4 to 8, HZ-014 C5), and an out-of-band fundamental from a frequency-control fault has no hardware bound at all, because the harmonic filter passes it, so its independence comes only from the verification unit K7 (REQ-SYS-182, package decision 40), a separate unit with a dissimilar method (count versus compute). A decline leaves the item unmet for that branch; section 8.3 records the consequence.
3. **Catastrophic hazards (the lithium-ion cell, HZ-002 and HZ-007).** Two-fault tolerance for overcharge is claimed only when the 4.30 V secondary protector has its requirement and each protection layer has its own Kelvin sense route (HZ-002 K3, K9; OQ-SAF-008, OQ-SAF-009); until then the overcharge branch is single-fault tolerant (charger regulation and OVP, REQ-SYS-081; 4.25 V protector, REQ-SYS-083; firmware dual sensing, REQ-SYS-088) with the shared MID sense net as a named single point (section 8.2 row 5), and HZ-002 is carried at Catastrophic-D, Serious. Every other cell cause is at least single-fault tolerant with dissimilar layers: charge temperature window REQ-SYS-082 (charger JEITA with the firmware check, sharing the NTC single point of section 8.2 row 6), charge timer REQ-SYS-089, insertion check REQ-SYS-087; discharge side, protector REQ-SYS-084 and fuse REQ-SYS-085 with the firmware soft cut-off REQ-SYS-096 to 099 and the rail window REQ-SYS-166, reverse insertion REQ-SYS-086. The Catastrophic fire case of HZ-015 is zero-fault tolerant until the owner confirms an auto-sleep station (K1; requirement pending, OQ-SAF-019).
4. **Critical hazards that firmware can initiate (HZ-004, HZ-008 C7, HZ-014)** are single-fault tolerant with dissimilar controls for a continuous carrier (firmware timeout plus hardware monostable plus reset-safe states), and single-fault tolerant for toggling streams with K12 and for the frequency-control branch with K7, zero-fault tolerant for each if its decision is declined; each such branch is a named single point in section 8.2 with its closing decision. The requirements are REQ-SYS-053, 054, 055 and 180 for HZ-004 (with the interlock REQ-SYS-052 and the watchdog REQ-SYS-131), REQ-SYS-009, 154 and 182 for HZ-008 C7, and REQ-SYS-132, 119 and 180 for HZ-014. HZ-003, Critical since 0.2.0-pha, is single-fault tolerant on its thermistor-failure branch with the hardware over-temperature cut-off K9 (REQ-SYS-181) beside the firmware inhibit (REQ-SYS-118, 155) and zero-fault tolerant without it. Critical hazards whose last barrier is a person (HZ-006) are backed by safe defaults and timeouts (REQ-SYS-011, 063, 064, 020, 065, 066) so that a rule violation alone does not produce the exceedance; the operator's discipline is the named single point of section 8.2 row 20.

## 9. Hazard to requirement and verification traceability (SWE-052, SWE-184, SWE-192)

1. **Field contract.** `hazards.json` carries `requirement_ids` per hazard (the field `tools/traceability.py` reads and cross-checks against `hazard_ids` in the requirements: `HAZARD_UNRESOLVED`, `HAZARD_INVERSE`; 03 section 8 row 2; 02 section 7 row 2) and `control_req_ids` per control; `requirement_ids` is always their union.
